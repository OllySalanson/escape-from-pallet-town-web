# UI, screens and sound

The title, the explorer run, sound, the menu stage and pixel-ui rules, cursors and pointers, the stage, keyboard ownership, frames, icons and the typeface.

Moved out of `AGENTS.md` on 9 Nov 2026 so that file stays small; nothing here was reworded. Keep it current the way `AGENTS.md` asks: a change that adds a subsystem or makes a note here untrue updates the note in the same change, and a new note goes in the doc whose area it belongs to.

## Notes

- **The title screen (`TitleScene.ts`) is the front page's banner come alive**, made of the game's own parts and nothing new: a band of Viridian City's real layers (`TITLE_TOWN`) drifting a pixel at a time under stepped dusk and a side vignette, sparkles, the name stamped out of Orange Kid itself into a gold logo (outline, rim and depth are the same lettering stamped again in other colours, into one canvas a slanted shine re-lights every few seconds), the README's tagline, and every fifteen seconds the trainer legging it along Viridian's main street - Red, or the save's own look once it has one (`SaveSummary.look`) - with the save's own partner (`SaveSummary.partner`, else a random starter) at their heel and Blue behind with his "!". What moves and when is `ui/titleScenery.ts` - pure functions of the time since the title appeared, every answer a whole pixel - and the menu redraws on its own so a keypress never rebuilds the town. `prefers-reduced-motion` gets the still picture. A phone opens on the same menu as anything else: it plays on the touch pad (see the touch pad note below), which took the place of the old needs-a-keyboard notice. The chase owns the bottom `CHASE_LANE` pixels, so the menu is laid out in the screen less that. `BootScene` paints the same dusk with a mint loading bar (no words: the font is still arriving) and the title fades in from it. Under the name it offers **CONTINUE, NEW GAME, and MAKE A MAP and PLAYTEST side by side on one row** (`ui/titleMenu.ts`, Phaser-free: the wording, the cursor rule and the layout - a choice marked `beside` takes the right half of the row before it; Up/Down walk reading order so every button is reachable, Left/Right only cross a row; `SaveManager.describe()` is what it reads - none, unreadable or a game with counts). MAKE A MAP opens the map maker and nothing else (the owner, 2026-10-10: the playtest button does not belong inside it). CONTINUE is dim and skipped by the cursor when there is no game to continue; a save the loader refuses is `unreadable`, never `none`, so it is not offered as empty; and **NEW GAME over any save asks first, with the cursor on KEEP MY GAME** (the key that woke the game is still down), because a save is never erased by one keypress. A browser that cannot write a save (`SaveManager.canSave()`) is told so on the starter screen instead of being offered a partner, because every later screen reads the game back out of the save and starting without one was a black screen. Space on a fresh browser still goes straight to Oak's table (`StarterScene` routes to it, next entry) and on a save straight to the lobby, which is what the playtest drivers rely on. There are deliberately no save slots for the ordinary game - `docs/screens/title-menu/README.md` says why - and the explorer run is not one: it is a second *file*, chosen on the way in and never listed beside a save (see the entry below). It is rebuilt on `RESIZE` and never scales a tile. It needs no font wait of its own - `BootScene` starts it only after `awaitGameFont()`. `tools/playtest/titleShots.mjs` photographs it in every state, and `--at=ms,..` steps its clock to exact moments; `docs/screens/title-dusk/` is the before and after.
- **The explorer run is a second game, not a difficulty.** `src/game/dev/playtestMode.ts` carries the whole of what the mode is and why; `playtestSave.ts` is the one save it deals. It exists so the maps can be *looked at* - four times the ground they shipped in, and a five-minute clock with a hunter on it is the opposite of a walk. Six things change and nothing else does. It is **its own save** (`PLAYTEST_SAVE_KEY`): `SaveManager` resolves its key per call through `activeSaveSlot()`, so every scene's argument-free `new SaveManager()` follows the live slot and no call site learned there are two; only `TitleScene` names a slot, to describe the other game without switching into it. The **player's side cannot be knocked out** - `hpAfterDamage()` in `battleEngine.ts` is the one function every path that takes HP goes through, and it floors the player at one; the flag is false everywhere else, which is what keeps `trainerMeasure.ts`, `encounterMeasure.ts` and the whole boss ladder reading exactly what they always read. The clock is `PLAYTEST_RAID_DURATION_MS` (eight hours, `HubScene.raidClockMs`) rather than stopped, so the hunter still arrives and the tiers still escalate - the escalation is measured from the raid's start, not from its length - and the chip says PLAYTEST instead of counting it down. **Every door is open and every keeper is still standing**: the doors go in `openedGates` as `gateKey(gate)` rather than into `defeatedBosses`, because a beaten boss is taken off the map (`withoutDefeatedBosses`) and fighting them is half the point - a trainer on an open door is walked up to, which `mapStructure.testkit.ts` already guarantees is payable. Every extraction is open (`WorldScene.isExtractionOpen`) and every tile of every map is surveyed, so the drop-in screen shows the country. And **shift walks three times faster**, in that mode only, because 150ms a tile across 64x72 is a long way to check a corner - taught on the objective chip's hint row beside `[O] GUIDE` and `[L] LOOK`, by the same rule the look is. The flag is a module variable and nothing stored: a reload lands on the title, which sets it back to `normal` on every ordinary path, so an explorer run is unreachable by accident by construction. Screens: `docs/screens/playtest-mode/`. **It carries a kit, one row a feature** (`dev/playtestKit.ts`): boxes of Pokémon each harbour game is played with (JUMPERS, PROMENADE, FIRST STAGES), 5,000 tickets, and `PLAYTEST_GAMES` - a new harbour game adds its row there with the kinds that suit it, and `playtestKit.test.ts` fails a game the save has no suited *and* no unsuited Pokémon for. The kit only adds and is applied to every resumed run too (`withPlaytestCurrent`), and marks every gym leader met, never beaten (`withEveryLeaderMet`), so each spars in the hall; a game that is a scene of its own is a `PlaytestGameScreen`. `?playtest` on the address goes straight in. The **PLAYTEST BOARD** (`dev/playtestBoard.ts`), a sign stamped on the lawn by the landing in that run only (solid only while it stands, so the ordinary yard's walks never see it), goes to any room or part of the harbour, sets the hour (`dev/playtestClock.ts`, which `windowLight` answers with in that run only - the harbour's light, its night people and a night raid all follow), starts a NEW DAY (the Game Corner's daily pays again) and opens any game; `tools/playtest/playtestKit.mjs` plays it. PLAYTEST on the title says so before a browser's first run starts (`titleMenu.ts` `sandboxMenu`: A SANDBOX, NOT THE GAME, cursor on BACK; the critic's problem 18, 25 Nov 2026), and `deploy.mjs`'s `startExplorerRun` answers it.
- Background music stays off, as the captain's standing preference (2026-09-19: sound effects welcome, music "pretty annoying") rather than a temporary switch: `SILENCE_BACKGROUND_THEMES` in `src/game/audio/AudioManager.ts` makes `startTheme()` a no-op while sound effects keep playing. Leave it and the scene `startTheme` calls alone; a theme written for later ships silent.
- Every sound effect is a named entry in `SOUND_EFFECTS` in `src/game/audio/soundEffects.ts` - square, triangle, sawtooth and noise voices as data, no audio files - and `audioManager.play(name)` is the only way one is sounded (`playWaking(name)` is the same call for a key or click that may be the page's first: a browser keeps audio asleep until a press, and `play` never wakes it, so the title's sounds go through it). **The one exception to "no audio files" is a Pokemon's cry** (the owner's call on playtest 28, 2026-10-11): `cry` and `faintCry` are entries of a second kind (`CryEffect`, channel `cry`) played as `play('cry', { species: dexId })` from FireRed's own samples, `public/assets/cries/<dexId>.wav` (`scripts/fetch-frlg-cries.mjs` from pret/pokefirered, `audio/cries.ts`, provenance recorded). A cry neither cuts nor is cut by anything but a fanfare: it *waits* for the battle, fanfare and cry channels, which is what puts it after the ball's pop and after the catch fanfare, and two Pokemon sent out together cry in turn. `play()` never waits on the network - a cry whose sample has not arrived sounds its tone `standIn` - so a fight's cast is fetched ahead (`loadCries`, from `WorldScene.transitionToBattle` and `BattleScene.create`). Which event cries is `battleEventCry` and `openingLineSounds` in `battleSounds.ts`: every send-out (the opening `Go, X!` included), a wild Pokemon appearing, a faint (lower, FireRed's faint pitch) and a catch - and an evolution cries as FireRed's `evolution_scene.c` does, from `BattleScene`'s evolution beat: the old species at "is evolving!", the new one with the fanfare, the old one again if B stops it - and a stone used from the raid pack sounds the same fanfare and new cry (`BagScene.giveTo`). The mixing rules live in `play()` and nowhere else: a channel says one thing at a time, anything that is not `ui` cuts `ui` (so the blip of the key that advanced a line never lands on the hit the line describes), a fanfare cuts everything, and the same effect asked for twice within `RETRIGGER_GUARD_S` sounds once. Which event gets which sound is pure and tested beside it - `battleSounds.ts` (battle events, and `BattleNote` for narration that carries a sound), `worldSounds.ts` (the edge-triggered ones: bump, tall-grass entry, hunter proximity) and `menuSounds.ts` (the DOM menus' baseline click, applied once in `MenuOverlay`). `soundEffects.test.ts` fails an effect that is too long or loud for its channel, identical to another, or played by nothing. A sound cannot be reviewed from a diff: audition it on the Test Lab's sound board (`?test-lab=1`), and check "did that play twice?" against `__audio.recentlyPlayed` on a dev build, which records only what was actually heard. Every effect is a one-shot, so none can outlive its scene; do not add a looping one. **Music ducks under a sting**: themes sound through a music bus under the master, and an effect with `ducksMusic` (every fanfare by default, plus the four one-off alerts that opt in - never a rhythmic one, which would hold the music down through a whole chase) takes it to `DUCK_LEVEL` for the effect's own length; two in a row are one quiet stretch. It works on whatever is on the bus while music stays silenced, so the day a theme is wanted it already ducks (`__audio.musicLevel` reads it; `docs/screens/audio-ducking/`).
- **N mutes everything, and the browser remembers it** (it was M until M became THE MAP, 25 Nov 2026) (`AudioManager.toggleMute`, `MUTE_STORAGE_KEY` in localStorage, read when the one `audioManager` is made): forgotten on every reload, a muted game sounded again on each visit (scenario loop, 22 Nov 2026). It is a per-browser convenience, never the save; a browser that stores nothing starts with sound.
- **A menu stands in the game's own box, drawn as fine as keeps its room** (the captain, 25 Nov 2026: menus "look stretched full screen on a big screen while the game itself sits in a smaller window"; firstmate's ruling the same day: the same box, never stretched, a step finer so the room stays). `src/game/display/menuStage.ts` sizes `#screens` - the fixed layer beside `#app` that every `MenuOverlay` is appended to, carrying its own `--px`/`--zoom` - to the canvas's own `computeStage` box, standing exactly where the canvas stands (`canvasMenu`), at `menuScaleFor`: the largest whole multiple, no coarser than the world's zoom and never under 2x, at which the box still holds a `MENU_STEP_WIDTH` x `MENU_ROOM_HEIGHT` (560x384) screen - the room the screens were laid out for. So at 1280x800 the world is 3x and a menu 2x, 600x384 game pixels; at 1920x1080 4x and 2x, 800x512; at 2560x1440 6x and 3x, 800x480. One step finer than the world was tried first, and then 360 high: each left the bench none of its box on a big window. It is a sibling and not a child of `#app` because `container-type` makes an element the containing block even for a fixed position. It used to be laid out against the whole window ("room before magnification", up to 1120 game pixels at 2x-4x), which on a big display was a menu across the glass beside a world drawn small in the middle; that rule is kept for the two cases the canvas box cannot serve - a touch screen, whose room the pad hands out (`touchLayout.ts`), and a window where the canvas runs under the FEEDBACK tab's 32-pixel strip (`FEEDBACK_TAB_STRIP`, on the edge `feedbackTabEdge` picks, which is the stylesheet's own rule). `stage.ts` and the canvas are untouched by any of it, which keeps everything world-anchored - the dialogue box, a world caption, the raid HUD - exactly where it was; `tools/playtest/worldAnchors.mjs` prints those positions so a change to either box can be diffed rather than eyeballed, and `tools/playtest/menuShots.mjs` photographs every screen at a real window size on a realistic save (two dozen Pokemon, twelve kinds of supply) and counts how many rows of each list a player can actually see; `--keys` walks the arrow keys over a screen laid out in columns and fails if any row of it cannot be reached without a mouse, which is the one thing a screenshot cannot show.
- **A collection fills the width it has, and one column is what a narrow window gets.** Every screen body was `minmax(0, 1fr)` and every list a single implicit track, so a wide display held a tall thin list down the middle with a scroll wheel on it - which is the fourth time the captain has raised crowding on these screens. A pane opts in with `data-columns` (`pixelColumns()` in `ui/pixelUi.ts`, carrying one of `COLUMN_MEASURES` - the narrowest a column of *that kind of row* may be, measured off the rows rather than chosen), `ui/columnLayout.ts` counts the tracks and `MenuOverlay.layoutColumns()` writes them, in the same measured pass that snaps text boxes - so a live resize relays the screen. **Every track is a whole number of game pixels**: the room left after the gaps is shared out one pixel at a time, first tracks first, because `repeat(n, 1fr)` lands on thirds of a pixel at most widths and draws every one-pixel frame in the list soft. Below two columns nothing is written at all, so a narrow screen is exactly the screen it was. `data-columns-max` and `data-columns-widest` make a centred row of cards instead of a stretched list (the starter picker, the lobby's three doors). Two things that follow: a generated box in a grid takes one *cell*, so the MORE strip carries `grid-column: 1 / -1` or it draws its rule across a third of the pane; and `align-items: start` is only on `.px-list[data-columns]` - a row of cards must stretch or three doors of different lengths stop reading as equal.
- **A screen is whole in the smallest window, and `menusAudit.mjs --fit` is what says so** (the small-window pass, 27 Nov 2026; `docs/screens/small-window/`). An 800x600 window is a body of about 400x210 game pixels (`data-room='tight'`, often `data-short` too), and four screens were broken in it: YOUR THINGS' locker stood ROOM FOR A FIND out of its card over the bar below and its chest showed three and a half of its eight squares across (it does not scroll sideways: at that size the locker stands beside the team, `grid-template-areas`, and the chest has the width), Bill's shelf for money pushed his shelf for parts off the screen, Brock's row of builds wrapped to two and left his room a sliver, and HELP's four cards were cut through their rows with the last keys out of reach. `node tools/playtest/menusAudit.mjs <build url> <dir> --window=800x600 --fit` opens every menu and names any card, row or line of words drawn past the body's edge, or cut through from above or below by a card that is not a list; **a new screen is run through it before it is called done** (it does not see a card squeezed to a sliver, nor a tile standing out of a pane that thinks it fits: look as well). Still broken there and listed in that folder: YOUR TEAM and the hall's bench, the Day-Care, THE CHEST's Pokémon. What a small room gets is written beside the rule it relaxes, under `.pixel-ui:is([data-room='tight'], [data-short])` (the form `style.test.ts` accepts): a card gives up a picture or a tally before it is cut (Bill's cards draw their thing at the icon's own size, Brock's builds are a name and their parts in one row, the locker's heading says KEPT and its button stands beside its squares), two panes share the height rather than one taking it (`minmax(0, 1fr)` twice, never `auto` first), and **a screen of words that can be taller than the window is one `px-scroll` pane with `data-reads`**, which `MenuOverlay` gives a MORE strip and reads on with the arrows - HELP is that now, and calls `overlay.layout()` as it opens because it has no cursor to wait for. A grid inside such a pane sets `grid-auto-rows: max-content`: a card that clips its own overflow has no height an `auto` row must keep, and is squeezed instead of scrolled. And a pane read on through holds the focus itself, so it is never one of its own rows (`standsOnARowOf`): taken for one, every scroll that left more below pushed it on by its strip's height, and no reading pane could be read back up.

The loadout's treatment chips are its `deeds`, for the same reason. **Every control inside a detail pane carries the pane's own `data-shows`**, or the cursor moving into it swaps the pane out from under itself. The dossier is as tall as what is in it, in a window that is a column flex (`:has(.px-dossier)` in `style.css`) so it can shrink to the screen: once list and dossier cannot both be whole the dossier gives way first, down to `--dossier-floor`, so how much of the list shows never depends on which row the cursor is on - a fixed band was one line and a MORE strip over half a screen of backdrop, with the raid party's TAKE below the fold (playtests 38 and 44); `roomFor()` in `columnLayout.ts` is the one rule that asks how much room the *screen* has (`data-room` on the overlay root, the only thing the stylesheet keys off it), because the band's five blocks need 820 game pixels to stand side by side and a band that wraps is a band cut through the middle. It is **three rungs, not two** - `tight` is `BASE_STAGE` itself, where a list, the band that answers for the row it is on *and* a second band under that cannot share one pane - so every `[data-room='narrow']` rule in the stylesheet carries a `[data-room='tight']` twin, held by `columnLayout.test.ts` because the failure is otherwise silent. `MenuOverlay.showDetailFor` swaps panes **within the control's own window**, not across the screen: Bill is two counters on one page, and swept screen-wide, pointing at his shelf blanked the pane under his barter table. A bought rung keeps its line and its price column says PAID. - **Every** DOM screen that belongs to the game is **pixel-ui** - lobby, starter picker, raid result, and since #156 the three a raid opens over the world (the pack, the party, the field guide): an overlay opts in with the `pixel-ui` class, its markup comes from `src/game/ui/pixelUi.ts` (`pixelScreen`, `pixelWindow`, `pixelCommitBar`, `pixelHpBar`, `pixelTag`, `pixelGrid`), and its styling is the `Pixel UI` block that ends `src/style.css`. The unit is one game pixel: `stageScaler.ts` sets `--px` (and the bare `--zoom`) on `#app`, the block calls it `--u`, and every measurement is `calc(var(--u) * N)` with `N` whole - never `px`, `vw`, `cqw` or `em`. One type size, 12.5 game pixels, because Orange Kid's stem is 0.08em and that is where a stem is one pixel, and no bold cut (`strong` is 400), so **weight and size are not levers and hierarchy is inversion, ink, a visible rule and space**. Three ranks, each the title bar or the window reused: `.px-title` is a dark strip 16 pixels deep across the whole screen (where am I); `.px-heading` is the same strip in miniature, 14 deep, bled out to its own window's frame with its note in `--bar-muted` (what is this panel); `.px-subheading` is a `--paper-sunk` band across a list in full `--ink` caps, ruled off in `--line`, with three times the room above it as below (what is this part of the list). Under them a name is plain caps in `--ink` and a detail is `--ink-soft`. Never `--paper-sunk` for a rule - on `--paper` it is a twelve per cent step that reads as a fill, not a line - and never `--ink-soft` for a heading: that is de-emphasis. A DOM screen may draw on a canvas of its own - the drop-in screen's map is one - and `#app > canvas` is scoped to the game's own canvas for that reason: unscoped, its `max-height: 100%` resolved a picture inside a pane with no definite height to nothing at all. Nothing inside a screen may resize the screen: `.px-screen`'s one column is `minmax(0, 1fr)` and the title bar's aside clips, because a grid's `auto` column takes its floor from the widest child's min-content and the title bar's `nowrap` back label and aside pushed the lobby 19 pixels past the canvas at the smallest stage. `style.test.ts` holds all of that. **No glyph is ever half-drawn**: a `.px-heading` note is whole or not drawn (the strip wraps a note that does not fit onto a second line the one-line strip clips away, so a note is never itself clipped or shrunk - a note a player needs, like the final check's squares, must be short enough to fit the smallest stage), and a `.px-scroll` pane whose foot would cut a row through its waist gives the whole row to its MORE strip (`scrollCoverHeight()` in `MenuOverlay.ts`, set as `--more-cover` by `markScrollCues()`) and clips off a line *beside* that row the strip's edge would still cut (`foldedRows()`, `data-folded` - Bill's detail panes are two columns whose lines interlock, so no one fold is clean in both), and a row of wrapped copy that is not a control (the result screen's contract) is measured as its lines beside its icon (`rowPieces`), so the strip falls between two of them rather than taking the whole row and its heading, and a `.px-subheading` is never the last line above the strip - with its rows all below the fold it goes under the strip with them (`orphanedHeading`; the drop-in pane ended on ERRANDS over nothing) - and a scrolled pane's top edge masks out the sliver of a row it cuts, down to the first whole row (`scrollTopCoverHeight()`, `data-above`) - the cursor scrolls a row into view by just enough to show it, which left the row above sliced through its name. `tools/playtest/headingShots.mjs` photographs every headed screen at both stages and audits each strip's note by geometry (`docs/screens/heading-clip/` is the before and after). `docs/screens/menu-hierarchy/` is the before-and-after at 3x and at the smallest stage. Orange Kid has no arrows and no tick, so none may be typed - the cursor and the tick are drawn masks (`--px-cursor`, `--px-tick`) and a HubScene test fails a screen that types one. Text is the only thing whose width is not a multiple of the unit, so `MenuOverlay.snapTextBoxes()` widens text-sized boxes to the grid after every render - and again on `document.fonts`' `loadingdone`, because boot waits for Orange Kid only `GAME_FONT_TIMEOUT_MS` and boxes measured in the monospace fallback stayed a third too wide (the starter picker's two type tags stopped fitting on one row; `tools/playtest/browser.mjs`'s `page.on('Fetch.requestPaused')` holds the woff2 back to reproduce it) - and `.px-screen` is its own transparent layer because that is what makes Windows and Linux antialias the face in greys instead of LCD colour fringes. Every selector in that block is scoped to `.pixel-ui` - through `:where()`, so the scope adds no weight and the cascade reads as written - because the older screens share class names with the lobby; `style.test.ts` fails an unscoped one. The flip side: a `:where()`-scoped rule weighs one class, so it can never pad a window against `.pixel-ui .px-window` (two). Bill's tight `padding: 0` sat dead for a release for that reason, and `style.test.ts` now fails any rule like it. Brock's ladder and payment are pixel-ui too (a rung is a `px-tall` row, and what the pointed-at rung does is the list's one `px-detail` pane, as on the contract board). How far a Pokemon is from its next level and what each of its moves does are derived by `src/game/ui/pokemonSummary.ts` (the top of the experience curve is read off the curve, because `experienceForLevel` clamps, rather than from a second copy of the cap) and drawn in the stash's own detail pane - see the entry below; there is no summary *screen*. Only the Test Lab (`?test-lab=1`) is still the older rounded style, which is a dev tool and nobody's game. **A screen a raid opens is the same screen as the one at base**, and that is a rule rather than a resemblance: `raidScreens.test.ts` holds all three to the shared parts, to `pixelColumns`, to `moveCursor`, and to having no layer of their own. It is read under the clock with the hunter on the map, so a layer costs more here than anywhere - the bag's pocket *tabs* are gone (every pocket at once, banded by `.px-subheading`, gear among them for the first time), the row itself is the act (Enter on a Potion opens the recipient list, so using one is four presses) and the pointed-at item's squares are lit by a class on a block rather than by a re-render (`BagScene.markPack`, off the `data-describes` key every `pixelGrid` block carries - the same key its row does, so pointing at a square answers for the row and back). The pane under a list of Pokemon is one module, `src/game/ui/pokemonDossier.ts`, shared by the stash and the raid party so two lists of the same thing cannot drift into two shapes; its CSS is `.px-dossier*`. One trap it paid for twice: **a `.px-scroll` pane may never have bottom padding of its own**.
