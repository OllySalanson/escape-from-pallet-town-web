# World mechanics

What stands on a raid map and how it plays: gates, bosses, landmarks, prizes, exits, locks, caches, trainers, cutscenes, interiors, ledges, contracts, errands, gifts, loot, figures, legends and wildlife.

Moved out of `AGENTS.md` on 9 Nov 2026 so that file stays small; nothing here was reworded. Keep it current the way `AGENTS.md` asks: a change that adds a subsystem or makes a note here untrue updates the note in the same change, and a new note goes in the doc whose area it belongs to.

## Notes

- **A door is a door, and a gate carries the key that opens it.** `MapGate` is a union: a **boss gate** names a `bossId`, and a **field-move gate** names a `fieldMove` (`src/game/world/fieldMoves.ts` - Cut and Surf, the tutorial's `CuttableTree` and `SurfableWater`). Both are one type because everything downstream of a gate - the collision `getWorldMap` builds per state, the caption, `mapStructure.testkit.ts`'s every-state sweep, the drop-in screen's dark - cares only that a door is shut or open; `gateKey()` is what it has to be told, the `bossId` for one and the gate's own id for the other, and `openedDoors({ defeatedBosses, openedGates })` is the one flat list every caller hands over. A field-move door is opened by walking up to it with a Pokemon that knows the move and pressing the interact key (`WorldScene.tryFieldMoveAt`, which records it through `SaveManager.recordOpenedGates` and rebuilds the map under a player who has not moved), and it **opens for good** exactly as a boss's gate does - the move is the key and a key is spent on a lock once, so a door does not shut behind a player who deployed without the right Pokemon. `raidProgress.openedGates` is optional with an empty default, so no save version bump. Three are shipped: Viridian Forest's COPPICE RIDE, the Floodplain's SHOAL CROSSING and Viridian City's TRADE HOUSE TREE (FireRed's own small tree in a gap of a fence), each opening a **place** rather than a short cut - both maps were measured for the short cut a field move could have been (the best single cut anywhere in that wood is four steps, the best straight crossing of that river twenty-two, which is three seconds of a five-minute raid), and `fieldMoves.test.ts` holds that neither shortens any walk the map already had. A third key is the hour: a **night track** (`NightGate`, a map file's door kind `night`) is wood by day and open for the whole of a night raid, keyed on the one word `NIGHT_KEY` that `nightDoors(plan.night)` adds to the opened list (`WorldScene.openedDoorKeys`, `generateRunPlan`) - nothing about it is saved, nothing captions it, and pressing at it by day says the fog has the track. HM01 and HM03 are `once` Bill barters like HM06; the machine snapshot is harvested over the whole 151 (`tools/moves/machines.mjs`, generated into `pokemon/generated/machineLearners.ts`) so neither door is a starter lottery - eight catchable species read Cut and four read Surf.
- **Every map has a boss, and every boss two doors.** Route 1's Overlook was the proving ground and the Floodplain has three keepers; Viridian City's Gatekeeper Ross holds the League fence and the forecourt's HIGH GATE, so beating him rings the League road to the Forest road; Pallet Town's Miller Vance holds the far bank of the millpond (TOWPATH GATE at its head, TOWPATH STEPS at its foot on the sluice apron - beaten, the town turns out to be a ring you can walk from the square to the South Gate without crossing the leat) and Viridian Forest's Lookout Pell holds the rock along the top of the wood (RIDGE GATE at the nub under the fire tower, RIDGE STAIR down into the stair clearing). A boss-held gate is a different map, not a flag checked at the door. `src/game/world/gates.ts` holds the authored `MapGate`s (tiles, a `bossId`, and both states as a `GateAppearance` - a material plus any props planted only in that state, never a tile number - and the gate overrides whatever the sketch drew under it), and `getWorldMap(id, defeatedBosses)` rebuilds the map from its sketch in that gate state and remembers it by which doors are open. `WORLD_MAPS` is every gate shut, which is right for a fresh save and for tests and wrong for a live raid: `WorldScene` asks through `mapFor()` and `generateRunPlan` takes `defeatedBosses`, so the player, the hunter's search, loot placement and the exit promised open at the start all read this raid's collision. `mapStructure.testkit.ts` runs every rule against every gate state (shut, each boss alone, all open) and adds the one a gate creates - every region a raid can start in holds a way out it can open from inside; `gates.test.ts` holds what only a gate can get wrong (a door nobody holds, a gate that seals nothing, a chain that cannot be opened in walking order from the front door).
- **A boss's party is measured over the real engine, never guessed.** `src/game/world/trainerMeasure.ts` plays a whole authored trainer fight out - best damaging move, next Pokemon in on a faint, seeded - `tools/trainers/report.mts` prints every trainer against a spread of parties while one is being chosen, and `trainerLadder.test.ts` holds the order the ladder settled on (Briggs, Wren, Vance, Cobb, Pell, Mott, Ross, Dane, Rudd, Holt, Nye) and keeps a new boss inside the band the others sit in (Nye is ordered but not banded: her all-Water team is a starter lottery left to the owner). Every number was re-measured on 2026-10-06 once the battle rules were FireRed's - the first set was taken with a trainer's replacement attacking in the turn its predecessor fell - Ross's first party (Nidorino, Spearow) was a starter lottery a Bulbasaur won 1% of, which only this said. Two things about the measuring itself. The move a measured player throws is `world/bestPlay.ts`, shared with `encounterMeasure.ts`, and it pays the **same-type bonus** and reads the **weather over the field**: scored as power times effectiveness alone it tied a starter's 40-power signature move with Tackle, the tie went to the first slot, and every measured starter answered every fight with Tackle - a level-7 Charmander went from 0% against the Floodplain checkpoint to about half, and correcting it moved every number in that report. And a **rung is the mean over four reference parties**, one per starter plus the team, because against a lone Fire lead the last two doors invert on a type matchup (Ember is 3x on Holt's Butterfree and half strength on Dane's Squirtle) rather than on difficulty. The optional `potions` argument plays the **pack**, a turn a Potion as the battle screen charges it: a door is measured bare, but a *toll* on a route is paid in supplies, and `floodplainCheckpoint.test.ts` is the one fight measured at the level a fresh save meets it. Guessing put Pallet's first draft two rungs above the Floodplain's last boss on the gentlest map in the game. Two things the table says that reasoning does not: a party with two Flying types takes a Bulbasaur to nil, which is the starter lottery Scout Lee's Squirtle exists to avoid, and the *anchor* decides the fight - Pidgey at 12 is a harder wall than Pikachu at 12. `trainerMeasure.ts` plays a double out too - it fields `party[1]` beside the lead, chooses for every engaged slot through `bestPlayFor` (which scores a spread move over both foes at the halved figure) and refills each empty slot from the bench. A boss is an authored trainer with a `bossId` (`src/game/world/trainers.ts`). Beating one is written to `raidProgress.defeatedBosses` at the moment of the win, not at extraction - `WorldScene.settleBossProgress()` does it on the battle return through `SaveManager.recordDefeatedBosses()`, which touches nothing but `raidProgress` - because a gate is the map changing rather than loot being carried out. The list the scene acts on is `plan.defeatedBosses` plus the bosses among this raid's `defeatedTrainerIds`, never a read-back from storage, so the door opens in the raid that won it even where nothing can be saved. A beaten boss is dropped by `withoutDefeatedBosses()` on every later raid - which is why, unlike any other trainer, a boss may stand in the lane it guards - and what is left is the open gate, captioned OPEN.
- **A landmark you finished with stays finished, and the map is where you read that.** A contract used to unlock an insertion and pay a reward and leave no trace on the ground it was banked on: you wound the Sluice Wheel, carried the ledger out through the culvert it drains, and next raid the wheel was standing there again with the culvert sealed behind it. `src/game/world/workedLandmarks.ts` is the answer, and it is authored the way a gate is - a row is a `WorldPoi` and the contract that finishes it, nothing more - because the state is **derived from `raidProgress.completedContracts` and never stored**. The tutorial's answer (a stable id and a blob per savable object, `Quests/QuestObject.cs`) is the opposite discipline and was deliberately not imported: a blob can disagree with the list, and every other permanent effect in this game (`secureItemStackLimit`, `contractUnlockedInsertionIds`, `getWorldMap`, Bill's standing) is a pure function of a list of ids. Something whose state is *not* the consequence of anything the save records would need a list of its own in `raidProgress` - a list of ids, still never a blob. Four things follow from one rule. The exit the landmark sealed is **open from the first second of every later raid** (`withWorkedExitsOpen`, derived from the landmark's own `unlockedExtractionLabel` so the two cannot drift), which is what stops this being decoration: an always-open door is a route and a risk. The landmark **still stands**, drawn with `landmark-worked` in the quiet `worked` tone and captioned with what it is now, because taking it off the map would leave nothing to read the change off; `WorldScene.tryActivatePoiAt` refuses it, so it neither opens what is already open nor pays out again. **A cache you emptied stays empty**: Oak's field station is the one landmark that is a cache as well as a seal, so the braid survey trades a standing two Poke Balls and a Potion for a standing way home - the only one of the four that costs anything, and the reason the rule is a trade rather than a gift. And the **lobby agrees with the raid**: `RunPlan.completedContracts` carries the list into the world (read off the plan, not storage, so it holds in a browser that cannot save), `DropInContext.completedContracts` carries it into the drop-in screen, whose exits say so and whose map lights the landmark and glyphs it `K` - the same payoff loop PR #138 built for a beaten boss. A contract banks on the way *out*, so unlike a boss it can never be finished mid-raid; the result screen says what the world kept, once, on the raid that bought it (`contractReportLine`). `workedLandmarks.test.ts` holds the authored rows and both ends of that agreement; `tools/tileset/minimap.mts --completed=` draws the lobby's picture big enough to criticise, and `docs/screens/worked-landmarks/` is the before and after of all of it.
- **A raid is for something, and what you are going out for is a *prize*.** `WorldLoot.chance` was the way to lay an evolution stone; it is now the whole rule (`world/loot.ts`, `isPrize`): a piece rolled on its own odds is rare enough to be an event, so it is **seated in its own `district`** (the tile moves every raid, the place does not - and a district this raid cannot walk to simply does not hold its prize, rather than re-seating it somewhere it does not belong), **drawn with a gleam in `CANOPY_BAND`** so the light shows over a tree crown, **captioned with the `prize` voice**, which unlike every other name speaks whenever it is in view at any distance (`ui/captionReveal.ts`), and **named on the raid HUD from the first frame it is seen until it is taken** (`prizeChipView`, carried through a fight on `RaidCarriage.seenPrizeIds`). None of that is decoration: greed needs an object you can see costing you your clock, and before this a hauler frame at one raid in eight seated anywhere on 16,384 tiles was a thing nobody ever met. The wall map's close-up says what a map is worth *without* saying what this raid holds, under the same dark the wildlife pane uses. **Five evolution stones now ship** - `fire-stone`, `water-stone`, `leaf-stone` and `moon-stone` beside the Thunder Stone - which makes thirteen evolution lines live; each map holds one of its own, in the country the Pokemon that reads it lives in (a second Moon Stone in Viridian City's border fields, where the Nidoran are; Fire in Route 1's charcoal burn where the Vulpix verge is, Water on Pallet's beacon headland behind Salter Cobb, Moon at Viridian's stone row beside the Clefairy of Beech Flat, Leaf in the Floodplain's withy beds behind the Cut door, Thunder in DEEP STAND where the Pikachu are). All five are also **repeating** barters at Bill's counter at five materials each, priced in the materials of the country each is found in - the only repeating deals on the boat besides the map maker's Pickaxe, because a stone is spent and evolution is the reward this game most wants to keep paying. And the result screen asks once more, after it is too late: `prizesLeftBehind()` gives every ending a `leftBehind` line in the report's `pressure` - what was *seen* and walked past, never what was merely on the map. `loot.test.ts` holds that every prize names a district its own fallback tile is inside; `runGeneration.test.ts` holds that a prize out of reach is not laid at all.
- **Every way out is a ride, read off the ground it stands on** (the captain, 5 Nov 2026: "actual vehicles that would be used in Pokemon"; `world/exitVehicles.ts`, drawn by `ui/ExitVehicleSprite.ts`, `docs/screens/exit-rides/`): a minecart under a roof, Mr. Briney's boat on deep water beside the exit, Pidgeot within two tiles of a cliff, else the Bicycle - nothing stored and no map names one, so a maker's exit and a stitched stand-in get theirs by where they are, and the maker draws and names the same ride (`maker/exitRides.ts`, an exit's mark box reaches two tiles because a boat lies out on its water). It is drawn and nothing else: the exit is still its one tile with its rules, timers and collision. A shut ride waits at `LOCKED_RIDE_ALPHA` in `MARKER_BAND` and comes in as it opens; an open one is drawn in `CANOPY_BAND` (exits stand in pockets of wood, and a tree's tip hid the ride as it hid the old square); stepping on one boards it (`WorldScene.boardRide`: player faded on the scene clock, partner put away, caption gone) and it leaves inside `RUN_RESULT_DELAY_MS`. Barred exits keep their rubble or gate until opened. The art is `exit-vehicles.png` (`scripts/cut-exit-vehicles.mjs`: Briney's boat from Emerald, FireRed's Bike Shop bicycle by its top layer, a minecart drawn here) and `rides/pidgeot.png` (`cut-hgss-followers.mjs`'s `RIDES`); `tools/playtest/exitRides.mjs` and `makerExitRides.mjs` photograph and board them.
- **Locks and keys: most locks give to a Lockpick, a special exit to its own key** (the owner's call, 2026-10-12). A `WorldPoi` may be `locked` - a cache that opens only to a raid that spends a Lockpick on it (`openPoi` spends one as it opens; the pack's room decides nothing, because a cache is searched a piece at a time), one per shipped map, paying more than any open cache beside it. An exit may be **barred** - `ExtractionRequirement` `dug` (rubble, the map maker's Pickaxe exit) or `key` (a gate only its named key turns) - and `itemThatOpens`/`isBarredExit` in `extractionPoints.ts` is the one question: barred is a wall until opened (`WorldScene.barredExitAt`/`tryOpenBarredExitAt`, `ActiveRunSession.exitsOpened`), open for that raid only, and **nothing that promises a way out may count one** - the standing board's exits, `mapStructure.testkit.ts`'s way-out rule, `hunterFlee.test.ts`'s window and the hunter's `reachableExitPositions` all leave them out. Every shipped map has one key exit and its key (packable, one square, never used up, lost with the pack) laid as a prize in another district of the same map, and four have rubble (Viridian City's rock has no pocket that is not under a crown). The words are FireRed's (`Text_ItNeedsCardKey`, `Text_CardKeyOpenedDoor`, `CinnabarIsland_Text_DoorIsLocked`). A **map file** locks an exit with `opens: { when: 'key' }` to FireRed's own Card Key (`MAP_FILE_EXIT_KEY`, one card for every file, as one card opens all of Silph Co.) and marks the item spot it lies on `key` - laid at the shipped keys' odds (`CARD_KEY_CHANCE`) and out of the spots' rotation, so no other spot's contents move; `isBarredFileExit` is the file side of `isBarredExit`, and `mapFileChecks` refuses a lock with no card or a card with no lock. `tools/tileset/pockets.mts -- <map> [--district=<id>]` lists a map's free, canopy-free dead ends - where a barred exit or a crate can stand without shutting a lane - and the free ground of one district for a prize's fallback tile.
- **A cache is searched, never paid out** (the captain, 2026-10-29: "in Tarkov you search crates"). Opening one (`openPoi` in `world/pois.ts`: the Lockpick, the exit it opens, `activatedPoiIds`) starts `world/cacheSearch.ts`: the lid, then each unit of its reward comes to light after `SEARCH_PIECE_MS` plus `SEARCH_SQUARE_MS` a square, drawn by `ui/SearchPanel.ts` down the right of the screen and never a dialogue box. A number key takes that piece, the interact key takes everything found and everything still to come, any direction closes it and walks on. The search is charged to the raid clock exactly as the clock is (`advanceRunClock`), so the hunter's idle step keeps coming; where it got to and what was taken is `ActiveRunSession.cachesSearched`, so a search walked away from or cut short by a fight carries on, and a cache leaves the map only once its last piece is taken (`piecesLeftIn`). Ground loot is still picked up instantly.
- **A cache pays what its place is.** The forty-one named caches in `world/pois.ts` used to pay the same five supplies between them and four of them paid character for character the same two, so as destinations they were interchangeable and only distance told them apart. They are authored per place now, on one vocabulary: medicine from water, springs, kitchens and presses; **balls from places that catch** (a decoy, a hide, a warren, a bird-scarer, a hollow oak); and a Brock material from the place that *makes* it - parts crates out of quarries and engine houses, lamp oil out of kilns and lamp stores, mooring rope off wharves and wrecks, linen out of laundries, shrines and lambing kits, cable off fuse reels and pit saws, radio valves off the relay's own boats. That is the same rule the wildlife tables and the ground-loot materials already followed; the caches were the layer that did not. `pois.test.ts` holds the *shape* rather than the contents: every payout distinct, drawn from the whole catalogue rather than from medicine and balls, no two maps paying the same list, and **never money** - a cache is a guaranteed pickup and the money faucet is measured off each map's loot pool alone (`hub/trader.test.ts`), so money out of one would be an unmeasured second faucet.
- A trainer is a price on a route only if walking the route is what starts the fight. `WorldScene.tryInteract()` needs the player to press the interact key, so a trainer standing in a one-tile lane is a locked door, not a cost. `src/game/world/trainerSight.ts` is the answer: an authored `sightRange` makes the trainer challenge anyone who steps into the straight run of tiles they are facing, `WorldScene.tryTrainerChallengeAt()` fires it after extraction and before the tall-grass roll, and `createTrainerWatch()` shades that ground and captions the trainer so the price is on screen before the player is in it. The Floodplain checkpoint and Route 1's boss are authored with a watch; the other three trainers are still spoken to. **A trainer who holds no gate is a toll, and a toll has to be payable at the level the route is walked at.** Only a boss may be a wall, because only a boss has a door behind it that stays open; every other trainer stands up again next raid, so "come back stronger" buys them nothing. RAIDER MAYA was two level-7s ten steps into the first contract, which a level-5 starter beat 0% of the time with its whole pack open - and at level 10 with nothing packed was still 10% for the Fire starter against 100% for the Water one - for a road measured at two steps and nine tall-grass steps (forty steps to anything east) - see her party's note in `trainers.ts` and `floodplainCheckpoint.test.ts`. What a toll is paid in is **supplies**: the pack, not the raid. **Every starter has to be able to pay a toll**: `TOLL_BAND` in `trainers.ts` is the rule (each starter alone, at the level the toll is met - 8, or 10 in Viridian Forest - with three Potions, wins at least 60% and is no more than 35 points behind another), `tolls.test.ts` holds every toll to it and `npx vite-node tools/trainers/tolls.mts` prints the grid while a party is being chosen. Being caught is a beat, not a cut: it is an authored cutscene (see the note below), and `src/game/world/trainerApproach.ts` is only the geometry it walks - the ground between the trainer and one tile short of whoever they caught. The payoff is `liftBeatenWatches()`: a beaten trainer is simply not rebuilt, so on the return from the win the shading is redrawn and released tile by tile from the trainer's end, once per trainer (`ActiveRunSession.watchesLifted`, because the world is rebuilt after every later wild fight too). `mapStructure.testkit.ts` holds every watch to the rules that keep it fair (never over an insertion, an exit or a warp arrival) and `floodplainRelay.test.ts` holds that map's road-versus-reeds choice to route facts.
- **An authored beat is a list of actions, not a method.** `src/game/cutscene/cutscene.ts` is the machine - the action vocabulary (`wait`, `say`, `turn`, `move`, `emote`, `teleport`, `show`, `fade`, `sound`, each carrying the tutorial's own `waitForCompletion`), a `CutscenePlayer` driven by elapsed ms, and `checkCutscene`, which is what makes the three raid constraints checkable rather than remembered. `src/game/world/cutscenes.ts` is every beat there is, authored as data. `WorldScene.playCutscene()` starts one (its first instant plays on the same tick, so the turn and the sting land where the player was caught), `advanceCutscene()` gives it the frame from `update()`, and `endCutscene()` is on the per-raid reset list and is called by `transitionToBattle` - a beat overtaken by the fight it was leading to must not be left holding the frame. The three constraints, and why: **the raid clock is charged for time the player can spend and never for time the world spends on itself**, so a self-running beat is free (and bounded by `CUTSCENE_TIMED_CAP_MS`, or there would be an authored way to stop the clock) and a `say` is billed exactly as any other dialogue; **every beat is skippable or self-advancing**, so the only beat that may wait is the dialogue box, which prompts; and **a cutscene may not move the player**, because `WorldScene`'s step machinery owns their tile and extraction, warps, tall grass and the district plate are all read off it - turning them is free of all that and is all any beat has wanted. Two are authored: the trainer approach (the mark, the walk to one tile short, the line) and the hunter's catch (both figures turn to look at each other, a breath, then the rival's own two `caught` lines from `hunters.ts`). A mark is seated a tile above the figure it belongs to and drawn above every figure, so `markWouldCoverThePlayer()` drops it in the one geometry where it would sit on the player's own face - hidden rather than drawn over something, the same rule `labelPlacement.ts` follows. `teleport`, `show` and `fade` are the tutorial's vocabulary and are held by `cutscene.test.ts`; nothing authored uses them yet.
- **A place with a roof on it is the same map with a lid, never a second scene.** `src/game/world/interiors.ts` authors a `MapInterior` - the rectangle the lid covers, what it is made of, its mouths, whether its floor rolls for wildlife and how dim it is - and Pallet Town's THE DELVE is the shipped one, the level under the quarry hill. The tutorial's interiors are a Unity scene behind a portal and that is the one part of it this game must not take: a map here is an arena, an exit takes whoever steps on it, and the hunter's pursuit is a breadth-first search over one collision grid, so a second scene would be a safe box with a loading seam on it. The floor is therefore drawn in the sketch like any other ground and **every structural rule, measured route and hunter search sees ordinary tiles**; what is added is `MapLayers.roof`, one tile layer above the canopy painted by `buildMapLayers` through the ordinary autotiler (so a hillside has a rim), which `WorldScene` lifts while the player is under it, plus a veil at `INTERIOR_DIM_DEPTH` so the floor is dark and the figures on it are not. Four rules, each paid for: **two mouths always**, because a one-door room is the shape of the hunter-in-the-neck trap, and `interiors.test.ts` holds the stronger form - no floor tile is a dead end, so any single tile being stood on still leaves every other tile a way out; **it breaks the trail and is not a safe box**, so the hunter walks to `HunterState.lastSeen` (`hunterQuarry()`) rather than to the player, is never blinded or frozen, may follow you in and still catches you on contact - and the chip says `HUNTER OFF THE SCENT` rather than anything about the player; **it is worth going in for**, which is its own district and a floor that rolls on every step (`floorRolls`, read at the one roll site in `WorldScene`), the only ground in the game that is not grass and still costs fights; and **dark is atmosphere, never a lock** - a cave gated behind Flash would be the corridor-with-a-key this game already refuses. Two traps the delve paid for: the lid is above the canopy, so a lattice tree crown left inside one is a beech growing in a mine (the test fails one, and the crown row is two above the trunk); and felling a lattice tree gives its tile back to `bare`, which on Pallet is grass, so a block that cuts ground beside a trunk opens the trunk's tile too - the delve draws its own floor row as rock to seal what its cutting felled. The delve joins THE HANGER to THE QUARRY in nineteen steps against thirty-seven round the hill, which is an alternative rather than a wormhole; a cave joining the valley's two lobes (143 steps apart, and authored that way) would be a map-design change and is the captain's call.
- **A shipped map's buildings can be walked into, and a room is built exactly as a player map's is** (`world/buildingInsides.ts`): the room is a `MapFileArea` in FireRed's room art and each door a `MapFileLink`, and `getWorldMap` lays the rooms east of the outdoors through `composeAreas` (`mapAreas.ts`, which `composeMapFile` also calls) with `INSIDE_MARGIN` of dark round them - never a second way in, so the hunter, the checks, loot, the standing board and every walk go through the door as they do on a player's map. Three consequences: the grid is wider than the map you see, so a picture of a map is sized by `pictureSize`, never `map.width`; every walk over a shipped map passes `map.links` (`mapStructure.testkit.ts`, `unreachableTiles` and `walksLengthenedBy` take it now), or a room reads as an island; and a stop or a heading in a room is named by the room, never by a compass point of the grid (`standingBoard.ts`'s `compass`, `areaRoutes.ts`). Two rules, both in `buildingInsides.test.ts`: **two doors, always** - the shipped rooms sit behind pairs of buildings that touch, one room behind both doors (the Floodplain's press house and its store, Old Town's last two houses) - and **no floor tile a dead end**, the Delve's rule. A place with two doors onto the outdoors `hides` whoever is in it (`hiddenInArea`, read beside the Delve's lid in `WorldScene.playerIsHidden`): the hunter walks to the doorstep it last saw them on, so leaving by the other door is the point; one door never hides anyone, on any map, because a hunter on the only doorstep is the trap. A new map with rooms names its outdoor size in `OUTDOORS` (the test holds it to the built map).
- **A ledge is a route only the player has.** `src/game/world/ledges.ts` authors the one-way drop: a `brow` of tiles, the `drop` direction and how many solid tiles the hop clears. Nothing about the map changes to make one - the brow is ground already walked, the bank is already the sheet's art and already solid, the landing is already walkable - so every structural rule goes on being asked of the collision alone, and the hunter, whose pursuit is a breadth-first search over that collision, simply cannot follow. `WorldScene.tryLedgeHop()` fires it where the step planner refuses a step, before the bump, with its own duration, an arc and its own sound; the hop is a whole step of the raid (clock charged, tall grass rolled, whatever it lands on worked). `ledges.test.ts` holds what an author can get wrong: the way round has to exist in every gate state and be at least four steps longer, the landing may not be an exit (an exit would end the raid on a step taken to escape), an insertion or watched ground, and pushing any direction from the landing must find nothing. Viridian Forest's EAST RISE is the shipped one, and the reasoning for it rather than Route 1's identical bank is in that file.
- **O in a raid is Professor Oak on the radio** (`WorldScene.answerRadioKey`, the captain's 30 Oct note on menus): two lines in the box (`ObjectiveGuideModel.radio`, the route's first and last), free of the raid clock as the screen always was, no screen; O again, or the `[O] FULL LIST` chip on the box (`ui/RadioChip.ts`), is the whole field guide, and ESC hangs up. A press mid-step is answered where the step ends (`radioDue`). The field guide is `ObjectivesScene` in `src/game/scenes/ObjectivesScene.ts`; derive its objective text from `buildObjectiveGuide()` in `src/game/objectives/ObjectiveGuide.ts`, and have it resume only the `WorldScene` instance it paused. The guide reads the contract's own `briefing`, so a new contract writes its lines in `contracts.ts` and needs no change here. It names a map's landmark the same way - from the `WorldPoi`'s own `label` and `description` - because the guide is read on four maps and a landmark name written as a literal there sent players on the other three to a place that does not exist.
- **An errand is a favour from somebody at base - one of the four keepers, or a person out about THE HARBOUR - and its thing is real** (`src/game/hub/errands.ts`, the captain's spark: "the crate is real and takes four squares; doing a favour means leaving loot behind, and a wipe means telling Brock you lost it"). Oak wants OAK'S PARCEL (MILLBROOK, pays five Poké Balls), Joy her medicine bag (the farm at ORCHARD TERRACES, Leftovers), Brock his crate (the quarry in GREYSTONE PEAKS, four squares, TM39), Bill OLD AMBER (PINE HOLLOW, an Eevee) - all on Greystone Vale since 29 Oct: laid on the maps retired on 22 Oct, none could be asked of a new save, and an errand's `place` is its district's *name* (`errandDistrict`), because a stitched file's district ids are only its order. Two more are asked out in the yard (`ErrandGiver` is a keeper's screen or `harbourGiver(id)`, an id in `base/harbourLife.ts`): the S.S. Anne's captain wants his SHIP'S LOG from THE WRECK (HM01, FireRed's captain's reward) and the Safari Warden, who walks the grass above THE COVE and cannot be understood without them, his GOLD TEETH from SAFARI MARSH (HM04, FireRed's). A harbour giver is a person out day and night, so a favour can always be answered (`errands.test.ts`); speaking to them runs the same conversation as a keeper's with nothing opened after (`BaseScene.speakForFavour`), and their "!" walks their beat with them (`placeHarbourMarks`); they have no `ERRAND_DISPLAY`. The model is the tutorial's quest (`Quests/QuestBase.cs`: lines for asking, completing, an item required and rewarded; a status saved by name) plus what a raid can lose: `raidProgress.errands` is one `ErrandRecord` each (started/completed, how often the thing was `found`, how many losses the giver has `lossesHeard`), optional, no version bump; everything else is derived by `errandBeat`. The thing is an item of effect `errand` - found-only, so never packed, lost with the pack unless the secure container kept room for its kind (the secure screen offers that room only while it is still out there), excluded from `SupplyItemId` so nothing that repeats can name one. **Asked face to face, never over a screen** (the captain, 29 Oct: the overlay "ruins the flow"): speaking to the keeper in their room - the mat or across the counter - says the favour in the room's own dialogue box, one box a line (`errands.test.ts` holds every line to 68 characters), and ends on its `question` with YES and NOT NOW in the box's frame (`BaseScene.speakToKeeper`, a `ChoicePrompt`, YES first as FireRed's box is, ESC is NOT NOW); only YES records it, a NOT NOW is asked again after the next raid (`declineErrand`/`errandToSay`, keyed by `raidsDeployed` like `pendingMoves.ts`, nothing stored), and then whatever the key was for goes on. A keeper with something to say wears FireRed's "!" (`BaseScene.markKeeper`). The whole of every favour is read on one quiet screen, opened with O anywhere at base - the raid's field-guide key - (`hub/favourList.ts`, `ui/FavourListOverlay.ts`): what is home, in hand, who wants a word and where (never what they want; they say it), and what is done. Asked only once a raid has been gone on and its map's front door is deployable (`errandOpen`; `errands.test.ts` holds that its district is reachable from that door with every gate shut). **Laid** by `generateRunPlan`'s last argument (`errandsToLay(...).map(errandLoot)`): a prize (`chance: 1`) seated in its district on a stream of its own, so no other roll moves. **Found** is written mid-raid (`SaveManager.recordErrandFound`, as a beaten boss is), which is what lets the giver ask after a thing that did not come home whichever way the raid ended. **Handed over** by speaking to the giver with it in the vault (`SaveManager.completeErrand`, one write, before a line of the thanks is read), and then it stands in the giver's room (`ERRAND_DISPLAY` in `base/rooms.ts`, drawn as its own icon). Oak's cork board lists errands in hand, the wall map's close-up names the one on that map, a door says when its keeper has something to say (`base/doorStatus.ts`), and the result screen says who to take it to, that it went down with the pack, or that it was left behind (picked up, then dropped for room - `snapshot.foundItems` against the ledger), and the giver's `lost` words are true of either. `tools/playtest/errands.mjs` plays the base half; `raid.mjs --errands=brocks-crate --grab=brocks-crate` the raid half; `docs/screens/errands/`.
- A giver is `src/game/world/gifts.ts`: an authored NPC (the `WorldEntity` named by `giverId`) who hands over one Pokemon, once, in their own words - `WorldScene.speakForGift()` from `tryInteract()`. **The gift is made inside the raid and is at risk**: it rides in the pack as a catch does (`RunManager.registerGiftedPokemon`, into the party while there is room, else the raid's caught stash), banked on extraction and lost on a wipe or timeout, and the giver says so. It is *received* only when a raid banks it - `RunResult.gifts` is written to `raidProgress.giftsReceived` by `SaveManager.bankRun`/`bankContract` (`withGiftsReceived`) - so a lost gift is offered again, never lost for good, and the giver's state is derived (`isGiftSpoken`: banked, or already carried this raid) with nothing stored beside the list. No save version bump: the list defaults to empty. A gift must not outclass the starter choice (`starterIdentity.test.ts`): the shipped one is a level-2 Pikachu - FireRed's Pikachu knows Thunder Shock from level 1, so no level keeps a typed attack out of its kit, and `gifts.test.ts` holds a gift under every starter in stats and in what it wins over the map it is given on (70% of the Floodplain at level 2, against 82% at 3 and a starter's 75-82%) - a new gift is a row in `POKEMON_GIFTS` plus an entity at the head of a one-tile lane.
- Contracts live in `src/game/objectives/contracts.ts` - the data is the design. A contract earns its place by changing the shape of a raid, so each one carries the mechanism that does it: several `markers` (the braid survey is three stops, made in any order - so a stop's `collectedMessage` names the stop only and `contractStopMessage` counts what is left), a `requiredExitLabel` (only the West Culvert banks the cordon ledger), or a marker's `carriedIn` (the warden's resupply spends Potions out of your own pack). Adding a contract that is only a new position on a map is adding a waypoint. They unlock in a chain via `unlockedBy`, one contract belongs to one map so choosing an insertion *is* choosing a contract (`contractForMap`), and `contracts.test.ts` holds the whole set to those rules.
- A contract's permanent reward is derived from `raidProgress.completedContracts`, never stored separately: `secureItemStackLimit()` in `contracts.ts` is the whole of the secure-slot upgrade - it takes the banked contracts *and* the built workshop upgrades, both required, because a defaulted second list would silently price a paid-for locker at nothing - so a save can never disagree with either list. A contract pays in items once or in capability for good, never in supplies that refill - the warden's resupply used to raise the wipe restock, which made a reward a subscription. `SaveManager.bankContract()` is the one banking path and grants each reward exactly once; `bankFirstContractRun()` is only its alias. A save written before contracts were a list is migrated by `firstContractExtracted`.
- Once the authored chain is banked the board is the standing board, `src/game/objectives/standingBoard.ts`: one generated contract per deployable map, and **nothing about it is stored** except `raidProgress.standingContractsBanked`. The board is a pure function of that count and the rest of `raidProgress` (deployable maps, beaten bosses, unbuilt workshop rungs), so it cannot be re-rolled by reloading and turns over only when one of its contracts is banked. Every contract comes from a template built on the three mechanisms `contracts.ts` already has (`survey` is three spread stakes, `dispatch` a `requiredExitLabel` that is never the exit nearest the case, `resupply` a `carriedIn` paid back a grade up, `sealed` a stop behind a gate still shut - the board naming the door and its boss, and only on a board with another map on it, because a board stands until something on it is banked), with tiles measured off the map in walking steps rather than authored - walked empty-handed (`walkedEmptyHanded`: a Strength boulder and a rock barrier are walls), because a fresh save was once dealt a survey stake behind the vale's Card Key shutter. Escalation is `hunterPressure` on the contract, spent through `hunterThreatFor(party, pressure)` - the same ladder the loadout is priced on, clamped to it - and only the board's top contract carries the full pressure, stepping down to none so the safe raid stays on offer at safe pay. It pays in what Brock consumes (`workshopMaterialKinds()`, plus a Pokemon of the place its first stop stands in, at that place's own odds, wherever the contract cost a fight that could not be declined - never one of the three starters' lines, because a starter is chosen rather than met and the board cannot know the partner). `boardContracts()`/`boardContractForMap()` are what the lobby and the deploy ask, never `availableContracts()` directly. `SaveManager.bankContract()` takes a standing contract **whole** rather than by id, because the raid that carried it can change the progress it was drawn from (the sealed contract is finished by beating the boss whose gate it was drawn behind); the round in its id is what makes it pay once. The lobby lists it in the same scrolling row-and-detail board the authored chain uses, most hunter first; what a row and its detail say is `src/game/hub/contractBoard.ts`, Phaser-free, so everything a contract costs (the door, the exit, the pack, the hunter) is on screen before the insertion is chosen. What the result screen says of a contract that was not banked is `contractReportLine()` in `src/game/objectives/contractReport.ts`, which is handed the progress the raid *left behind* and promises "stays on the board" only if the board dealt from that progress still holds the same contract - compared by content, because a redealt standing contract shares its id (round and map) with the one it replaced. A stop's position is worded as a part of the map ("the north-west of the map"), never a heading - the field guide prints the heading from the player on the line above, and the walk to it (`ObjectiveGuideContext.stepsTo`). **A briefing never counts steps from a landing**: the ground is measured from the map's front door, and the board is dealt before the landing is chosen, so "326 steps from Greystone Vale" was read by a player who had dropped in at FARMHOUSE. **An exit a job names opens for anybody by its own rule, no later than `EXTRACTION_UNLOCK_DELAY_MS`** (`jobMayNameExit`): never barred, never sealed on a landmark the job did not ask for, and never late enough that healing debt can cut the raid's clock short of it (the midgame bug hunt of 11 Nov 2026). The rim's Job line says how much of the clock the job is just walking (`standingJobWalk`: landing, every stop in the best order, then a way out that banks it, as time against the raid clock because fights cost none of it); it is measured when asked and memoised, never when a board is dealt, because the suite deals boards for hundreds of seeds.
- Finishing a step on an extraction tile resolves the raid before anything else is rolled into the same tick (`WorldScene.tryExtract()` returns whether it took the step). Authored exits stand in tall grass, and rolling an encounter first banked the raid and then tore down the scene the result screen was scheduled on, leaving the player in a dead world. A step is not the only way onto an open exit: one can open under a player waiting on it (a timed exit's locked line invites exactly that, and the beacon opens on the insertion tile) and a battle can hand the raid back on one, so `update()` also asks `tryExtractWhereStanding()` whenever the player is at rest with nothing on screen to read. Both go through `extractThrough()`.
- **The first contract is softened for a newcomer, by data**: a toll may carry a `firstContractParty` (`world/trainers.ts`), which `trainersForContract` swaps in on any raid carrying the first contract - Raider Maya's lead Pidgey a level down - and `generateRunPlan` applies it, rolling nothing. `npx vite-node tools/encounters/firstRaid.mts` plays the whole first raid (teaching fight, reed fights, Maya) with HP, Potions and experience carried, which is how a change to the first hour is measured.
- A map's `loot` table is a pool, not a layout: `generateLoot()` in `runGeneration.ts` draws half to all of it per raid and re-seats every piece on a free tile, so the authored `position` is only a fallback and no two raids lay the same ground - except on a file map, where a maker's item spot *is* the place: `layItemSpots` keeps each laid spot on its own tile and rolls only which are laid and what is in each (`fileMapLoot.test.ts` pins every shipped landing's loot by fingerprint, so a change here that moves a shipped roll fails) (`runGeneration.test.ts` holds that across 500 seeds per insertion). A design review once read the table and took it for what the player sees. It is **one RNG stream across every map**, so adding a landmark or a loot row to one map moves the rolls on the others: filling Pallet's valley in stopped seed 1 rolling a wild encounter on `consecutiveRaids.test.ts`'s first step in Viridian. The fix is to make the test ask for what it means (walk until a roll lands) rather than to tune a map around a lucky seed.
- Generated runs never randomise an exit out of existence, and the exit promised open from the first second is only *forced* where the map has not authored one: it used to force the first exit with no authored requirement regardless, which on three maps is the timed one, so the Mill Stair, the Route Outpost and the Forest Clearing were open at 0:00 under signs that say they open later. `generateExtractionPoints()` in `runGeneration.ts` offers every authored exit on a warp-reachable map and only varies the timing of exits with no authored `requirement`; `runGeneration.test.ts` guards the guarantee across 500 seeds per insertion.
- A figure with no design of its own is drawn from the one `character` sheet, which holds a single character and no second design, so nothing in that art distinguishes them. `src/game/world/characterPresentation.ts` is what does: role tints (the player is the only figure in that sheet's true colours) plus the player's own head chevron, a pure pixel table so `characterPresentation.test.ts` can assert the rules without Phaser. There is deliberately no mark at the feet: the teal ground ring #73 added was painted over the sprite and cut the player's feet off, and was taken out on 2026-11-05 (the captain: "why would we have a blue circle?") - mark the player from above, never over their sprite. `WorldScene.setPlayerPosition()` is the only place the player moves, and it carries the chevron and the player's y-sorted depth with it. The chevron is drawn over every figure, so it gives way to anybody standing in the tile above the player (`headMarkCoversSomebody`, asked each frame by `WorldScene.settleHeadMark`): that is where a player stands to talk to someone, and it covered their face for the whole conversation; the player is drawn in front of them there, so nothing is lost.
- **Townsfolk keep a beat; trainers never do.** `src/game/world/npcIdle.ts` is the schedule - Phaser-free, handed a frame's milliseconds and answering with the beats inside it - and `WorldEntity.idle` authors it: `roam` is the whole set of tiles a person may ever stand on besides the one they were placed on, `glances` the ways they look, `beatMs` their own interval (never shared with a neighbour, or a street marches in step). It runs outside the raid clock and off `Math.random` rather than the run's seed, so the street costs nothing and a seed still plays the same raid; beats stop dead while anything is on screen to read, and a figure owns both ends of its step until the step has finished drawing. A figure blocks its own tile, so a beat can break a map: `mapStructure.testkit.ts` holds every map with **every** beat tile solid at once - stricter than any position the game can be in - and asks separately that each beat tile is walkable ground one step from the last, that nothing a raid is for is ever stood on, and that no walk between two named places is longer with the whole street off its mark. A trainer has no beat on purpose: a watch is authored from position and facing and is the price on a route, so a trainer who turned would move the ground the player is being charged for. **It is not a cutscene and could not be one**: a cutscene is a finite authored list that owns the frame, locks the player out and stops the raid clock until it is `done` (`checkCutscene` caps its self-running time at two seconds), while a beat is endless, runs *while* the player walks, and must never take the frame or a millisecond of the clock. What the two do share is how a figure is drawn: `idleFrames()` answers in fractional tiles exactly as `CutsceneActorFrame` does, a townsperson walks a tile on elapsed milliseconds rather than on a tween for the same reason a cutscene actor does, and `WorldScene.placeFigure()` is the one place any figure that is not the player is positioned, depth-sorted and posed.
- An authored `WorldEntity` or trainer can instead name a `design` from the registry in `src/game/world/characterDesigns.ts` - FireRed/LeafGreen figures under `public/assets/characters/`, preloaded by `BootScene` with their walk cycles. `getWorldCharacterAppearance(role, design)` is the one rule: a designed figure is drawn from its own sheet untinted (a multiply tint only muddies finished art) and is never marked, and the `protagonist` designs are refused on every role but the player, so no one else can be dressed as the hero. Every non-player figure is made by `WorldScene.createFigure()` and turned by `faceFigure()`, because a frame index depends on the sheet's width (`getIdleFrame(direction, sheetColumns)`) and only the stored appearance knows it. The designs stand 17-20px tall against the shared sheet's 23, soles on the same row; `headPixelY` in the registry is where each head starts, and the player's chevron is still placed from the shared sheet's head. Adding a design is a row in `scripts/cut-frlg-characters.mjs`, a registry entry and a provenance line - `characterDesigns.test.ts` fails any one without the others. Twenty-one designs ship, and two things about the last four. A design's `kind` may be **`named`** - Professor Oak, Nurse Joy, Bill, Brock, who are somebody in particular rather than a class of person, which is why the base screens name one by id and a map should not cast one as scenery; and `walks` is false where the sheet gives a figure one cell a facing and no cycle at all (Joy and Brock stand at a counter in the game the art comes from too), so the cut repeats that cell across the idle and step columns and gives back the one pixel the sheet draws a standing figure higher. Which unlabelled row of the sheet is which person was settled against `pret/pokefirered`'s own named object-event graphics, frame for frame - nothing was taken from it, and guessing had put Bill three rows off. Only the Route 1 trainer is cast on a map so far; placing people is map work.

- **The four legends are bosses you can keep** (`world/legends.ts`, the captain's ask of 2026-10-12): Articuno, Zapdos, Moltres and Mewtwo each stand as a map-maker-style standing Pokemon (`LEGEND_ENTITIES`, spread into `WORLD_ENTITIES`) on a dead-end tile behind one map's keeper - Articuno by Pallet's beacon behind Cobb, Zapdos on Route 1's Overlook behind Wren, Moltres on Viridian Forest's Raven Crag behind Pell, Mewtwo in the Floodplain's vault behind Holt - at FireRed's own levels (50, 70) and moves. - **Wildlife is authored per district, beside its name.** `MapDistrict.encounters` (`src/game/world/districts.ts`) is the table a place's tall grass rolls on, built from the named `*_WILDLIFE` tables in `src/game/pokemon/encounters.ts`; `encounterTableAt()` in `localEncounters.ts` resolves a step (district table, else the map's `encounters`), and `WorldScene.encountersAtCurrentTile()` asks it. A raid varies every district's table on its own seed stream (`RunPlan.districtEncounters`, keyed by district id), so giving a place wildlife moves no loot, trainer or hunter roll of any seed. `PALLET_TALL_GRASS` and `VIRIDIAN_FOREST_TALL_GRASS` are now only the map fallback (no shipped district falls back to them - `districtEncounters.test.ts` fails tall grass in an unauthored place) and the standing board's reward pool. A new district with tall grass needs a table; a new species is an entry in the table of the place it lives in. `starterIdentity.test.ts` still pins the fallback, and the same 'every signature move has a target' rule is held **per map** in `districtEncounters.test.ts`, not per place. - **A night raid meets what comes out at night** (`world/nightRaids.ts`). Night is the player's own clock, the same `windowLight` THE BOLTHOLE's window reads (stars from nine until five), decided once when the raid deploys (`generateRunPlan`'s last argument, `RunPlan.night`) so nothing changes mid-raid, and the drop-in screen reads the same clock (`DropInContext.night`, heading `Wildlife by night`).
