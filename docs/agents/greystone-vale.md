# Greystone Vale

The one map in play: how it is built, filled, gated by gyms, and its far places' mechanics.

Moved out of `AGENTS.md` on 9 Nov 2026 so that file stays small; nothing here was reworded. Keep it current the way `AGENTS.md` asks: a change that adds a subsystem or makes a note here untrue updates the note in the same change, and a new note goes in the doc whose area it belongs to.

- **Greystone Vale is the game's own map drawn as a file, and for now the only map a save is offered** (see the roster entry below; `greystone-vale`, 384x384, a 6x6 plan of 64-tile districts: the fourteen drawn in the middle, and a ring round them of six far places planned, Stormcrown, the Lost Garden, Frostreef, Ember Isle and the Moor Chapel drawn, open sea and wild wood - a district not yet drawn is a sealed wood the stitcher lays, an `island` is sea, so the map plays while it grows). **The far places are planned to be hard to reach on purpose, each a different way** (Stormcrown only from a sixth cave floor, the Moor Chapel only on a night raid, the Lost Garden through a boulder puzzle, the Bunker behind the Card Key and a lift, Frostreef and Ember Isle only by boat on the tide), and each one's brief - way in, why it is hard, what is only there, what the game must learn to build it - is its `reach` in `plan.ts`; boats are `sailings`, and `docs/screens/greystone-vale/README.md` ('The far places') says which new mechanics are shared and so which to draw first. **384 was measured before it shipped** (same README, 'Growing to 384'): +1.3 KB gzipped and the same frame rate, but about 86 MB more JS heap in a raid there, growing with the grid's tiles, wood and sea included - so it is on `LAZY_MAPS` (`worldMap.ts`): built the first time something asks for it, never at boot, and in a raid's content only when the raid is on it (`authoredContent` in `runGeneration.ts`, its authored keeper included), so no seeded roll elsewhere moves; a picture's frame is sized off what a screen shows (`largestMapSize(mapIds)`, `mapPictureSize` reads a lazy map's file rather than building it). Its source is a mapcraft world (`tools/mapcraft/worlds/greystoneVale/`, one program per district, `docs/screens/greystone-vale/`); what ships is the stitched file `world/maps/greystoneVale.json`, written by `world.mts --write=` only when the whole world passes, and `worlds/greystoneVale/greystoneVale.test.ts` fails a file that is not what the programs draw - so **edit a district's program and write the file again, never the JSON**. It is a `BuiltInMapId` whose content is that file built by `buildFileMap` under its own id (`builtInFileMap` in `playerMaps.ts`, `contentFromFile` in `worldMap.ts`), so every lookup a player map's content goes through - `fileMap(id)` behind `entitiesForMap`, `poisForMap`, `districtsForMap`, `gatesForMap`, `ledgesForMap`, `trainersOn`, `extractionPointsOn` - answers for it, and a rule asked of a map reads `extractionPointsOn(mapId)`, never `EXTRACTION_POINTS`. **It carries everything a finished map does and no test excuses it**: Boatswain Cragg (`trainers.ts`, the ladder's last rung) holds the Sunken Quarter's two sandbagged doors - a map file's `boss` door kind, keyed by `keeper` and allowed only on the game's own maps (`maxBossDoors`) - the Relay Mast opens the Linesmen's Track (an exit's `opens: { when: 'landmark' }`, `maxLandmarkExits`; checks never count one as a drop-in's way out), the Service Gate and the Strongroom are its key exit and locked crate (asked through `extractionPointsOn`/`poisForMap`, never the authored tables), and `BUILT_IN_FILE_PRIZES` lays its packs. **It is offered through the finished chain** (`MAPS_OPENED_BY_THE_FINISHED_CHAIN`, derived in `availableInsertionIds`): with every other map retired the chain reads as finished from a new save, so its front door is a new game's landing and the standing board deals on it from the second raid (the first carries no job: `hub/firstRaid.ts`). `MAPS_IN_PROGRESS` (`world/mapRoster.ts`) is the list that would hold a drawn map back again - only the explorer run, no wall map, no pennant or badge (`STRUNG_PENNANTS`, `caseBadges`). With the old maps put away it carries no evolution stone, TM, furniture or double battle: those were laid on the retired maps. Its legends are all five, each in its FireRed home (`maxLegends: 5`, and `maxPokemonLevel: 70` for Mewtwo): Mew on Stormcrown, Zapdos in the Turbine Hall, Moltres in Ember Isle's crater, Articuno on the Frozen Lagoon's far sand, Mewtwo in the Bunker's B4F war room - the retired maps stand the same four, and a legend is caught by kind, so one caught anywhere stands nowhere. By `density.mts` it stands at 6.1 against the others' 4.5-7.4 (from 20.6 before its caves, districts and far places were peopled - `worlds/greystoneVale/caveLife.ts` floor by floor, and each district's own people, signs and standing Pokémon: a figure in a room's or a lawn's open middle, never a passage, because a figure is a wall to a walk out; the vale may stand 384 Pokémon and 96 berry trees, `BUILT_IN_MAP_FILE_LIMITS`, which was raised for filling it a ring at a time; nothing stands where an ice slide or a spinner arrow could meet it, which is what `frostreef.test.ts` and `bunker.test.ts` hold) - fill it with places, not by excusing it; `density.mts -- greystone-vale` lists the thinnest places first. The structure and safety rules hold it as they hold every map, split across eight files by tiles because its flee sweeps alone ran seven minutes in one and the night track asks each twice (`mapStructure.greystoneVale1-8.test.ts`, a part's `tiles` in `mapStructure.testkit.ts`). A district's landings are rows in `RUN_INSERTIONS` too (`greystoneVale.test.ts` holds them to the file), and drawing a district drops its neighbours' stand-in ways out, which can put their far corners past the hunter's blind window: run `hunterFlee.test.ts` and give the far side a hidden way out of its own (the Sunken Quarter's Fishers' Boat covers Gull Strand's cove). **Stormcrown is the first far place drawn** (`worlds/greystoneVale/stormcrown.ts`, README 'Stormcrown'): reached only from the caves' sixth floor B5F, which is built on a seed of its own (`greystoneDeep`) so the five shipped floors never move, joined by one hole in B4F; it is the first outdoor ground no road reaches, so mapcraft's one-piece check walks links, and a plan district may `rain`. Its blind rounds were judged against FireRed routes redrawn in the game's tiles from pret block data by a scratch converter - rebuild one before judging, and expect its noise to make a broken control beat a real route. **The Moor Chapel is the fifth far place drawn** (`worlds/greystoneVale/moorChapel.ts`, README "The Moor Chapel"): reached only on a night raid, by the night track on High Moor's sheep track, with a churchyard and a two-floor tower crypt that roll the `haunted` habitat and the Spell Tag as a prize. No player's map may name a prize (`maxPrizes: 0`), so a district that carries one returns `limits: planLimits(plan)` to be checked alone; a district's stand-in landing is just inside its first opening that is not a night track (`standInOpening`); and `raid.mjs --clock=night|day` plays a raid at that hour by time zone, `--attach=PORT` drives a browser already running and `--deep` chains doors. The Greystone Caves are `worlds/greystoneVale/greystoneCaves.ts`: five floors built once whichever districts are drawn, carried in Tanglewood's file, each mouth on a floor of its own - a district with a mouth plants a `cave-mouth` at a rock face's foot and adds `caveMouthLink(cave, mouth, at)` to its links. A gate in one district locked to a card lying in another is the plan's `cardKey` (the Power Station's Service Gate and Pine Hollow's card): `districtFile` gives each district walked alone a stand-in for the other half and `stitch` drops both, because a lock with no card and a card with no lock are each a fault. A wood is drawn a letter a lattice cell (`MapProgram.cells`), because a lane on an odd tile is eaten by the conifers; and judged blind, lanes through thick wood with a sand path that ends at something read as a place, where islands of trees in open grass read as a stamped checkerboard.

- **The vale has secrets: a nook, a clue beside it, and a condition fixed as the raid deploys** (the harbour arenas plan's step S1; `world/secrets.ts` is the data and its header the rules, `world/rumours.ts` what the harbour says about them, `secrets.test.ts` holds both). `RaidConditions` (hour, weekday, month, the lead's types, field moves known, what was packed) is read by `HubScene` at deploy and passed as `generateRunPlan`'s last argument; `RunPlan.secrets` is what is open and what each nook holds next. A nook and its clue are things in the district's own program tagged `secret: '<id>'` (an item spot, a standing Pokemon, a person, signs) - only on the game's own maps (`maxSecrets`) - and a `line` nook is the water of the secret's place, biting on the first cast. A tagged spot is no part of the map's pool or rotation and its tile is reserved every raid, so no other roll moves; once everything in a nook is found it holds a berry off `SECRET_STREAM`. Found is derived as the Pokedex is: every save writes `raidProgress.secretsFound` from what the vault holds (`secretsHeldBy`), so a find lost with the pack is there again next time; a gift is found when banked. Finds are `relic` items (found-only, lost with the pack): the torn chart's pieces and the fossils Oak's FOSSIL MACHINE (`hub/fossils.ts`, the lab's west machine) wakes over one raid.
