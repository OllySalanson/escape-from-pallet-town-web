# The hunter

The hunter pack, the one shared hunter, the rivals, fleeing and trainer engagement. `docs/hunter.md` is the deep dive behind the numbers.

Moved out of `AGENTS.md` on 9 Nov 2026 so that file stays small; nothing here was reworded. Keep it current the way `AGENTS.md` asks: a change that adds a subsystem or makes a note here untrue updates the note in the same change, and a new note goes in the doc whose area it belongs to.

## Notes

- A wild battle must be handed everything a trainer battle is: `WorldScene`'s wild payload once dropped `hunterState` and `defeatedTrainerIds`, so the world came back with a fresh hunter - arrival announcement and all - after every wild fight. `resumeThreatsAlreadyAnnounced()` is the other half: `create()` runs on every return from battle, so a threat tier the player was already warned about is restored silently rather than re-announced.
- **A raid alone is hunted by a pack: a hunter in every district** (the captain, 29 Oct: "a hunter in each area ... if you lose it then it goes back to its own one", "run at the same speed as them ... sometimes they get stuck behind something for like one turn"). `src/game/world/hunterPack.ts` is the whole rule, Phaser-free, and its header and constants are the design; the deep dive behind every number is `docs/hunter.md`, and `npx vite-node tools/hunter/chase.mts [-- --pause=1000 --grass=0.1]` plays hundreds of chases on the real vale - run it on both sides of any change to the pack's numbers. One hunter per patch (`huntingGroundsOn`, `world/mapHuntingGrounds.ts`, which is `huntingGrounds` told who stands where, so a loop has no corner under a figure or behind a boulder: a district at least 32 tiles each way that no bigger one holds - the vale's nineteen - else the whole outdoors), each the rival `rivalsForGrounds` gives it (the raid's own rival keeps the landing's district; no two neighbours share a face). They come out at the old arrival time (`spawnDelayMs`, `hasPlayerSetOff`, `isHunterEligible`) on a corner of their loop at least `HUNTER_ARRIVAL_CLEARANCE` from the player, patrol at a stroll, notice in a cone or by hearing (tall grass hides past arm's length), say FireRed's "!" for `HUNTER_ALERT_MS` (and a green "?" while one looks about where it lost you, the chip shouting `MISTY LOST YOU` when it gives up - `WorldScene.drawSearchMark`, `hunterShout`), then chase **in real time at the player's own pace** on the raid clock (so a box, a search, a bump or standing still is ground lost, and a fight, a cutscene or the briefing stops them with the clock), snag for one tile on a forced turn after a straight run (`HUNTER_SNAG_*`), keep track round one corner (`HUNTER_TRACK_MS`), and otherwise run to where they lost you, look about and walk home. They follow you out of their patch only within `HUNTER_TAIL_STEPS`, and out of it give up the moment they lose sight. A fight they were not in lets every chaser come on `HUNTER_FIGHT_CATCH_UP_STEPS` but never into touching distance (`afterFight`). The pack rides the run session (`ActiveRunSession.hunterPacks`, by map), so a fight hands back the same hunters; the one `HunterState` in `RaidCarriage` now only carries the outcome of a fight with a pack hunter (`HunterPack.engagedId`, settled by `WorldScene.settlePackAfterFight`). Every fairness rule still holds: walking into any hunter catches (`tryWalkIntoHunter`), a fled hunter is thrown back by `findHunterBreakawayTile` with the exits and stands blind and solid for `HUNTER_SEARCH_MS` before walking home, a catch is asked only at rest with nothing to read, and the enrage turns every nearby hunter on you at twice your pace and brings the keeper of your district back once if you beat it. Hunters further than `HUNTER_AWAKE_RANGE` from the player hold still and the pack's searches reuse their arrays, so a chase costs what the few near hunters cost. **A raid with a friend still shares the one step-locked hunter below** (`hunterPackHere()` is undefined in a room), and a scene test that builds `WorldScene` without `create()` has no pack and drives that path.
- The one hunter (a room's shared hunter, and the enrage's returning keeper) arrives at a fixed walking distance: `findHunterSpawnTile()` in `src/game/world/hunter.ts` takes every tile exactly `HUNTER_SPAWN_DISTANCE` steps away, so the spawn is always reachable and always the same lead time, and returns null rather than place it closer than `HUNTER_MINIMUM_SPAWN_DISTANCE`. `WorldScene.placeHunterIfDue()` retries on the next tick when it does, and `moveHunterToCurrentMap()` must run after `createMap()` on a warp or the search reads the map the player just left. Its *first* arrival also waits for `hasPlayerSetOff()` in `src/game/world/hunterArrival.ts` - the player has walked `HUNTER_SPAWN_DISTANCE` steps, counted on the run session because the scene is rebuilt after every battle - so it cannot land beside someone still reading the insertion screen. It is a floor on the arrival, never a delay added to it. And like the breakaway it is handed this raid's exits: a ring tile that would stand in the one lane between the player and every way out is offered only when every tile of the ring would.
- **The hunter mirrors the party, and an ordinary hunter is easy** (the captain, 2026-09-23: one Pokemon against one, "pretty easy to beat" like Tarkov's AI raiders). `hunterTeamFor()` in `src/game/world/hunter.ts` fields one Pokemon for each of the player's that can still fight *when caught* (`createHunterTrainer` is handed `WorldScene`'s live party, so a raid that lost two on the way meets fewer), paired in the party's own order (the hunter's lead answers the player's lead, so a strong Pokemon in reserve only sets the level of the one that answers it - paired highest first, the explorer's Lv 99 Charizard had Blue open at 95 against a Lv 50 lead), each a rung's `levelOffset` below the one it answers, led by the rival's own `signature` and then `HUNTER_ROSTER` (`hunterLineupFor`), each **evolved as far as its level reaches** (`hunterSpeciesAt`: a level line at its own level, a stone at the lowest level a FireRed trainer fields the evolved form, `HUNTER_STONE_EVOLUTION_LEVELS`) - the captain's answer to playtest 33, where a Rattata at 39 against a Charizard let every evolved party ignore the enrage. A signature must answer no starter's type, because the lead meets a lone starter alone and a typed lead is a starter lottery (Blue's Pidgey beat a Lv 10 Bulbasaur 100%): `hunter.test.ts` holds every rival's last rung to a floor against every lone starter, so a new signature is measured before it ships. `HUNTER_TIERS` is four offsets (-4 to -1) on the unchanged 0/120/180/240s schedule and the enrage is +3; every ordinary rung sits below the party, which `hunter.test.ts` holds. **Measure, never argue**: `npx vite-node tools/hunter/measure.mts [-- --potions=3]` plays every rung against a spread of real parties over `trainerMeasure.ts`, and its summary is the table in `hunter.ts`'s header - re-run it after any change to stats, the roster, a signature or the offsets (it measures each rival, `--rival=` for one, and late evolved parties beside the early ones). `hunterThreatFor()` in `hunterThreat.ts` is now only what the final check can say before the raid (who, how many, the lead's level, against which of yours) and the standing board's `contractPressure`, which is the one thing that still opens the hunter a rung higher and 10s sooner (`HUNTER_ARRIVAL_LEAD_PER_TIER_MS`, still re-divided from the 25s floor). A strong party no longer arrives the hunter sooner. The place grades on the drop-in screen kept the old ladder's levels as `PLACE_GRADE_LEVELS` in `dropIn.ts`.
- **Five hunters take turns, and differ only in their words and their lead** (the captain, 2026-09-23: Blue first, then others, "all the same apart from their names and their personalities and their art and their lines"; all five must be characters players remember; 2026-10-11: "each rival leads with a signature Pokemon instead of the shared Rattata"). `src/game/world/hunters.ts` is a name, a `named` FireRed design (`characterDesigns.ts`), a personality, a `signature` first stage (Blue's Eevee, Misty's Psyduck, Lt. Surge's Voltorb, Koga's Grimer, Sabrina's Venonat - each from that character's own FireRed team; the header says why each, and which were measured and refused - Surge's Pikachu, Koga's Koffing and Sabrina's Drowzee went when the starters took FireRed's own learnsets) and the caught (its first line gloats about the pack), getaway and defeat lines; the signature is the only thing on it that reaches a fight. `rivalForRaid()` rotates **per raid**, off the raid count `raidProgress.raidRecord` already keeps (nothing stored); `HubScene` reads it before `startRun` counts the raid so the final check names the same rival the raid is hunted by, and it rides in as `HunterTuning.rivalId`. `temperament` is the marked hook for the harder hunter the captain means to add later: every shipped rival is `ordinary`, and a `hard` one is a new value plus its own offsets. Giovanni is deliberately held back for that role. `tools/playtest/hunterShots.mjs` photographs all five and the loadout default (`docs/screens/hunters/`). **A gym leader beaten at their gym stops hunting that raid** (the owner, 11 Nov 2026, after the hunt report met KOGA on the Safari Road straight after the SOUL BADGE): `rivalsStoodDown()` in `hunters.ts` reads the raid's beaten trainers against `ValeGym.rival`, and `WorldScene.ensureHunterPack()` - run every time the world is built, so first right after the gym fight hands back - calls `HunterPack.standDown`, which gives each patch that leader kept to another rival by `withoutStoodDown()` in `hunterPack.ts` (fewest touching patches alike, then the next in turn), keeping the hunter's place and state. A leader beaten in an earlier raid hunts as before. A friend room's one shared hunter is not stood down; `scenes/gymLeaderStandsDown.test.ts` drives both cases on the real vale.
- **The hunter's arrival never stops the player** (the captain, 2026-10-29): no dialogue box. `WorldScene.hunterArrives` plays the arrival sting and starts `world/hunterWarning.ts` - three red pulses round the screen's edge (`ui/HunterEdgeGlow.ts`, under the HUD chips, over the map and anything a search puts on it), then a slow heartbeat while the hunter is within the chip's distance and has not lost the player, held steady under reduced motion (`ui/reducedMotion.ts`) - and the hunter chip names the rival and shouts `BLUE ON YOUR TAIL` / `IS BACK` for `HUNTER_SHOUT_MS`. The rival speaks first at the catch.
- The one hunter's pursuit (a room's) is a breadth-first search in `src/game/world/hunter.ts`: `findHunterPursuitPath()` returns the whole route so `WorldScene.advanceHunterPursuit()` searches once per tick however many steps the hunter takes, and `chooseHunterPursuitStep()` is the single-step wrapper. A search runs on every step of a chase, so it must stay cheap on the 384-square vale: the flood stops at the hunter's tile (`buildDistanceField`'s `stopAt`, the same route the whole field gives), and `WorldScene` hands every hunter search `hunterBlocker()`, built once a call, never a callback that scans every figure per tile - that pair cost ~90 ms a step and walked a chase at half pace (`hunterChase.test.ts`). Equal-length routes are broken deterministically towards the long axis of the chase; an unreachable player makes it close on the nearest tile it can stand on and hold. It is **step-locked, plus a step every `HUNTER_IDLE_STEP_MS` (6s) of raid clock the player stands still** (`hunterIdleSteps`, `WorldScene.advanceHunterWhileStill`, driven from `advanceRunClock` so only charged time counts, an open box included - a box held open was the last free way to stand still; a completed step restarts the count). The catch that idle step makes is asked by `update()` at rest with nothing on screen to read, so a fight never starts under a box. **A beaten hunter is gone for the raid except for the enrage**: `hunterAtEnrage` re-arms a hunter beaten before the clock ran out to arrive afresh, once (`HunterState.enrageAnswered`), so one beaten during the enrage stays beaten. `scenes/hunterIdle.test.ts` drives both through the scene.
- Fleeing is two different mechanics. The hunter's escape never fails and is priced in raid time by `RunManager.registerHunterFlee()` (schedule in `src/game/run/fleePenalty.ts`); it also disengages, via `beginHunterDisengage`/`findHunterBreakawayTile`/`tickHunterSearch` in `src/game/world/hunter.ts`, with `WorldScene` choosing the fallback tile because only it knows the map's collision. A wild escape is FireRed's `TryRunFromBattle` in `src/game/pokemon/battle/escape.ts` (the captain's ruling, 2026-10-10): at least as fast on the plain Speed stat always gets away, slower rolls `floor(128 * mine / theirs) + 30` per earlier attempt of 256 - held at certainty rather than wrapping as FireRed's `u8` does, so it always terminates. Both surface their cost on the command label before the player commits - see `battlePresentation.ts`.
- The hunter's escape is a purchase, and both halves of it are derived rather than felt. The price is unchanged from PR #66 because the clock is not spent on walking (the greediest Floodplain route is ~25s of a 300s raid), so 40s is affordable and the escalation still ends a raid that flees every contact. What was wrong was the goods: `findHunterBreakawayTile` takes the player's *heading* (`WorldScene` passes `this.facing`, the only read the world has on where they are going) because a heading-blind fallback parked the hunter on the player's own route about a third of the time and lengthened that route about a quarter of the time. And `HUNTER_SEARCH_MS` is sized to the longest walk from any tile to its nearest exit at the slower measured step cost, so the window always covers the way out. `hunterFlee.test.ts` recomputes both from the maps themselves - a redrawn map that strands a corner fails there, not in a playtest. It also takes this raid's **exits** (`mustReach`), because the one thing the goods must never be is a lid: on 379 of the Floodplain's tile-and-heading pairs the fallback used to land across the player's only route to every way out, which is a jar sold for 40s of clock. `doorsFrom()` beside it reads a map as doors from where one person stands - which tiles take ground away, and which shut away every goal - in one Tarjan pass, and the ranking prefers a tile that does not seal, at the same separation, which on all four maps costs nothing at all. It is a preference and not a promise: a pocket whose one way out is a one-tile lane has no unsealing tile at any distance. See the occupancy rule below for the floor under it.
- An authored trainer fight has no exit, and that is the design rather than a fault: a trainer is the toll on a fast route, and a toll that can be declined once the bill is read is not a toll. What was missing was the warning, and `src/game/world/trainerEngagement.ts` is the one place both roads into that fight get it - the caption over a watch (`trainerWatchCaption`, drawn by `WorldScene.createTrainerWatch`) and the prompt raised when the player presses the interact key at a trainer (`trainerChallengePrompt`, drawn by `src/game/ui/ChoicePrompt.ts` and owned by `WorldScene.askForTrainerChallenge`). The prompt starts on BACK AWAY, so the key that opened the conversation can never start the fight, and an arrow stops at the end it points at rather than wrapping (`ChoicePrompt.moveSelection`, FireRed's no-wrap YES/NO), so Right towards BACK AWAY can never flip it onto the fight; there is deliberately no prompt on the watch, because it would arrive after the step the watch charges for and declining it would refund the toll.
