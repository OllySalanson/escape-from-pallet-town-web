# Drawing a map

How a raid map is drawn and judged: MapSketch, materials and catalogues, the FireRed sheet's rules, mapcraft, structure rules, captions, depths and districts.

Moved out of `AGENTS.md` on 9 Nov 2026 so that file stays small; nothing here was reworded. Keep it current the way `AGENTS.md` asks: a change that adds a subsystem or makes a note here untrue updates the note in the same change, and a new note goes in the doc whose area it belongs to.

## Notes

- The overworld's tile collision is built by `buildCollisionData()` in `src/game/worldMap.ts`; interactive NPC and sign definitions live in `src/game/world/npcs.ts`.
- Each raid map is authored tile by tile in its own file under `src/game/world/maps/`, as character art through `MapSketch` in `src/game/world/mapGrid.ts` - `draw(x, y, rows)` puts a block of one-character-per-tile art on the map and `plant(x, y, name)` stands a named landmark on it. Edit the drawing, not a generated grid: the previous pass built maps out of one-tile polylines and `for` loops over staggered offsets and produced exactly what it was. A map may declare **stamps** - letters of its own that stand a landmark where they are drawn, anchored at a chosen cell (a tree at its trunk) - so a wood is part of the picture rather than a list of coordinates that drifts from it. A block's ground is laid before its stamps; walkable ground that a *later* block cuts under a tile a stamp `blocks` takes the landmark away and gives its tile back to `bare`. That is what lets a district be **carved out of solid forest** instead of built up from blocks that must meet: woods drawn as grass with trees on it left a walkable seam between every pair of trunk rows and along every block boundary, one of them the whole length of the map's east edge.
- A map names **materials and roles, never tile numbers**. `src/game/world/tileset/materials.ts` is the vocabulary (grass, turf, tall-grass, earth, sand, beach, paving, stone, gravel, ford, water, hedge, tree, cliff, fence, wall, ice, boards and the spinner floor) with the one character each is written as; `catalogue.ts` is the schema; `autotile.ts` turns eight neighbours into a role; `buildMapLayers` in `tiles.ts` assembles four layers (ground, overlay, detail, canopy), the collision, the tall grass, any shoreline and the planted props. Nothing outside a catalogue knows a tile index, which is what makes swapping the art data-only - and the role set is exactly the thirteen-piece block a FireRed material is drawn as, so `frlgTileset.ts` maps one to one onto it. `FRLG_TILESET` is the sheet maps are drawn on, and `FLOOD_TOWN_TILESET` (`floodTownTileset.ts`) is that ground with a *chosen* list of the CC0 sheet's buildings standing on it - the FireRed sheet has one building, and each object was looked at on this grass before it went in. The four older maps draw from it; Viridian City draws from `KANTO_TILESET` (its own entry below). Sheets share one numbering and `Overworld.png` runs to 2439, so the FireRed sheet starts at 3000, the base's at 5000, the Kanto sheet at 6000, the player's house at 7000, the town buildings at 8000, the insides' (`areaSheet.ts`) at 11000, THE RAID ARENA's front (`base/raidArenaSheet.ts`) at 19000 the boardwalk's planks (`tileset/boardsSheet.ts`) at 22000 and its hall (`base/arenaHallSheet.ts`) at 23000, last because it grows with the hall; at 2000 every Overworld tile from row 25 down drew from the wrong sheet, and `catalogue.test.ts` now holds the whole loader list apart rather than only the catalogues that exist. Tall grass is the sheet's spiky plant tile, which therefore is never a grass accent: the sheet's own tall-grass bed reads as topiary nobody would walk into. No shipped map draws from `CLASSIC_TILESET`, `OVERWORLD_TILESET` (the CC0 sheet) or `POKEMON_GROUND_TILESET` (GBA ground with its objects) any more; they are kept because `tiles.test.ts` and `worldMap.test.ts` hold the catalogue rules - shoreline, tints - against them. A prop carries its own collision and is the last word on its own tiles: a cell marked as one you may stand on is ground whatever it is laid over, which is what makes a bridge deck over a river a bridge (and means a gate under a deck must be shut by props standing on it, not by the material beneath). A cell drawn on the canopy layer may never also be a wall, and never opens what it hangs over - `buildMapLayers` enforces that, because a crown over a figure means the figure can be under it.
- **Ground is one art family or it is not ground.** `tileset.png` is GBA Pokemon art (91% exact 15-bit colour, grass at hue 152); ArMM1998's CC0 `Overworld.png` is hue 123 and 11%, so a seam between the two grasses reads as two games stitched together. Objects travel freely - `character.png` is from the CC0 pack and every figure has stood on GBA grass since the first commit. `pokemonGround.ts` is that split: classic materials, Overworld props. A catalogue is chosen per map in `worldMap.ts`, so one map can be redrawn without dragging the others through it.
- Two things the FireRed sheet decides about how a map is drawn. **Earth and paving are two tiles wide or not at all**: the sheet draws a lane's fringe on one side of a tile, so a one-tile lane has a fringe down one side and a bare cut down the other - what is one tile wide is grass or tall grass, which have no edge to get wrong. And `buildMapLayers` asks all eight neighbours, so a lane turning a corner gets the sheet's inside-corner tile (for the Floodplain's whole first life only four were asked, and every inside corner was a square notch), while a material's `joins` lists what it meets without an edge - a ford and deep water, or a ford reads as a pool with a bank down both sides. **The FireRed sheet's pine was cut without its trunk** (its art filled two rows of a three-row footprint and stopped at its lowest branches), so `pine`/`pineAlt` are `KANTO_CONIFER` in every catalogue - the whole route conifer, same 2x3 footprint and collision - and `tileset/pine.test.ts` fails a tree with an empty row. **A broadleaf is taller than its 3x3 block**: FireRed draws the top five pixels of its crown in the tile above, and the sheet was cut to the block, so every tree on every map stood with its crown sliced flat until a playtest named it. That strip is `PropDefinition.brim` (`FRLG_OBJECTS.TREE_BRIM`, cut by `scripts/cut-frlg-tree-brim.mjs`), drawn on its own `MapLayers.brim` above the canopy, deciding nothing - not collision, not `crowned`, clipped at the map's top edge - and a caption keeps clear of only the leaf drawn there (`brimRuns`); `frlgSheet.test.ts` fails a brim that does not meet its crown. A tree in a map's first rows loses its brim to the edge, which is why the base's top row of trees stands a row down. Every object on that sheet has its plain grass baked into its corners, so a map file's `rock` plants `rockClear` (`scripts/cut-frlg-clear-rock.mjs`), which stands on sand without a square of lawn. A tree's crown tiles have grass baked into their corners and are drawn over the figures, so **a crown may only hang over thicket or grass, never over a tile somebody walks (one tile is enough to stand hidden on - Viridian shipped one, and the map expansions brought back two hundred), and never over the one band of sky a caption needs beside the thing it names**. The walked-tile half is CI's: `crowns.test.ts` asks it of every map in every gate state through `MapLayers.crowned` (canopy that is not `PropCell.walkedUnder` - an arch's span is walked under, a crown never is). A forest stamp lists its crown row in `blocks`, so a lane cut under a crown fells the tree to thicket by construction; a tree standing in the open has thicket drawn under its crown (invisible, the crown is opaque), which makes it three tiles deep to walk round - move it a row rather than shut a lane under it. For the rest - `tools/tileset/crowns.mts` lists the trees that break that and `plantThicket.mts` proposes what to plant where the wood is only a hedge thick; crowns.mts clears a caption's sky three tile rows deep, but a seat is placed in pixels and not tile-aligned, so a 48px caption straddles **four** rows and a crown in that fourth row blocks a seat the tool has passed - which is why Viridian's TOWER STEPS still seats itself under the player (`whyHidden.mjs` is what says so; deepening the band asks for ten trees across two maps, so it is map work, not a tool patch); a playtest found exits with no name, a road with a green square in it and a hunter standing unseen before either existed. The sheet's ledge is `bankWest`/`bank`/`bankEast`, and a bank is solid: a ledge may only stand where nobody can get on top of it, with growth or a fence along its brow, **unless it is authored as one that can be gone over** - see the ledge entry below.
- Shorelines are never placed by hand. A catalogue declares a `shore` rule and `buildMapLayers` derives every piece from the collision, so a nudged bank cannot leave a bare seam. A sheet with no shoreline art simply gets a plain edge.
- To see a sheet or a map rather than imagine it: `docs/tilesets/README.md`. `tools/tileset/contactSheet.mjs` prints any sheet zoomed, gridded and numbered (committed for both sheets under `docs/tilesets/`), `atlas.mts` draws the catalogue, `props.mts -- <catalogue>` draws every landmark with its collision hatched, and `renderMap.mts` draws the real maps through the real layer builder. `mapReport.mts -- <map> [--runs] [--clashes]` is the inner loop while drawing: the structure numbers with coordinates, every over-long lane listed once, stranded pieces, landmarks drawn over each other (a crown eats a roof one tile away) and trunks left standing on roads. Viridian Forest shipped with no forest in it because nobody could see what they had authored. `density.mts -- <map> [--old=x0,y0,x1,y1]` is the other half of the same question and the one an **expanded** map is judged on: for every walkable tile, the walking steps to the nearest *permanent* authored thing - exit, drop-in, landmark, sign, gate tile, trainer, contract stop - with loot deliberately left out, because it is re-seated map-wide every raid and counting it flatters a map with nothing standing in it. `--old=` splits old ground from new, which is where a seam shows: a map can pass every rule in `mapStructure.ts` and still be a trail through trees. Shipped: Pallet Town 4.5, Floodplain 5.5, Route 1 6.1, Viridian Forest 7.8. Use it the way it was meant - **measure, then fill the thin stretches by adding places, not by padding trails**: Pallet's mean went 5.4 to 4.5 and its worst 19 to 14 on five things, and three of the five stood in the ground that shipped rather than in anything new, because the tool is just as willing to say the old drawing is the thin part.
- **An AI draws a raid map with `tools/mapcraft`** (the map research, 2026-10-12; `tools/mapcraft/README.md`). A district is a short `MapProgram` on the file's own letters, planned first (road skeleton, ledges with a job, one thing per screen) and fixed in three look-and-fix rounds; `look.mts` compiles it, runs the hard checks (the game's `checkMapFile`, then every tree whole, every open tile walkable-to, and the brief's walking rules), prints pattern-language advice against FireRed's route medians - advice, never a gate - and draws it through `tools/tileset/mapPicture.mts`, which `renderMap.mts` shares. A huge map is a world plan (`worldPlan.ts`: equal districts, one idea each, crossings fixed before either side is drawn, even sizes so seams never cut a tree) whose districts start sealed except at their openings (`startDistrict`) and are stitched by `stitch.ts` (an undrawn district is wood, so the map plays while incomplete); `caves.ts` draws Mt. Moon-style chamber floors joined by ladders; `world.mts` is that loop. **Greystone Vale** (`tools/mapcraft/worlds/greystoneVale/`, plan in `docs/screens/greystone-vale/`) is the first new raid map drawn this way: fourteen 64-tile districts, each its own program, shipped beside the game's own maps as the stitched file `src/game/world/maps/greystoneVale.json` (its own entry below). Judge maps blind in the same tiles, one at a time, with deliberately broken real routes in every batch (a judge that rates one above a real route is discarded): a "which is real" test inside one conversation leaks, and a classifier trained on the real maps learns proportions, not layout. Cut a district's view at the size of the real places it is beside and framed as they are (a window through the middle of a town reads "no frame" to every judge), and have each judge write a picture's notes under its own code before reading the next, or notes land under the wrong code; Millbrook's three rounds are in `docs/screens/greystone-vale/README.md`. A route-like district is judged as 24x40 views beside FireRed routes, and High Moor took eight rounds to learn what passes: a framed corridor about twenty wide, never an open field; rock in masses, never as standing stones or sprinkled boulders; places along the road rather than a pattern over the ground ("an arrangement, not a place"); and a road the land shapes, winding round what stands in it, never a straight road with things set beside it. Orchard Terraces added one more after five rounds that each passed one judge: a frame ruled straight the height of the slope is itself a pattern, so the wood steps in and out, a ledge runs from one of those steps to the road, and grass comes in whole beds of unequal size. Greystone Peaks added one more: **a cliff is a wall only when it is the frame** - rock standing on lawn reads as a block however big, and the same rock running out of the view along a corridor's side reads as a cliff, as FireRed's Route 24 draws it. A district with a cave mouth carries the cave's floors and ladders as well as the district that owns them, so each passes its checks alone, and `stitch.ts` takes a place or ladder two districts carry alike once. The Crossing added two about water: FireRed's straight channels and plain pools pass beside a cliff or a road, while a river stepping sideways in short jogs or a pool of stacked rectangles reads as generated; and a river is drawn after anything that could cut it - a tall-grass bed drawn later severed a bend no hard check sees. **A player's map holds at most twelve ways out, eight landings and twelve trainers** (`MAP_FILE_LIMITS`, which `submit_map` mirrors), and the game's own maps drawn as files hold more and may be bigger (`BUILT_IN_MAP_FILE_LIMITS`, read by `builtInFileMap` and by mapcraft for a plan marked `builtIn`: 384 square against a player's 256, measured first - see the vale's entry): a vale of thirty-six cells does not fit a player's counts. A stitched world's stand-in exits into undrawn districts count until those districts are drawn, and a district far from every way out needs a hidden one of its own for `hunterFlee.test.ts` - the vale's woods, peaks and strand each have one. Mapcraft's hard checks do not ask the shipped maps' landing rule - walking straight from a landing meets a wall within four steps every way (`mapStructure.testkit.ts`) - so run `mapStructure.greystoneVale1.test.ts` (the part that asks every rule but the sweeps) before trusting a new landing.
- A map is judged as a network of passages, not by how much ground it has: counting walkable tiles says nothing, because an open field scores well on it. `src/game/world/mapStructure.ts` computes the two numbers that do say it - longest straight walk and connected open ground - and `tools/tileset/mapReport.mts` prints them; they are **advice, never a test** since the captain retired the nine-step and no-clearing rules for every map on 2026-10-03 (P8 of the player map maker: the only gate on a map is whether it works). `mapStructure.testkit.ts` holds every map to the does-it-work rules (and Viridian City alone to the FireRed numbers it was drawn for - see its entry): reachability of every exit and landmark from every insertion, a sealed map edge, a fair hunter arrival from any tile, and a flee that buys real separation without walling the player in. Change a map and re-run that suite rather than eyeballing the result. One thing that suite cannot see: it asks whether every exit and landmark can be reached with every gate *open*, and with them open anything reaches anything. A two-row bench in a two-row yard sealed the Floodplain's front door off from its own quay, route board and Ferry Dock for eleven commits and every rule passed, because the quay could still be reached through three bosses and the keep. What a fresh save can walk to is therefore pinned by name, per gated map - `floodplainRelay.test.ts`, 'a fresh save, from the front door' - and `tools/playtest/raid.mjs --exit=LABEL` leaves by a named exit, waiting for a timed one, because the driver's nearest-open-exit rule never chooses one. Two more things every map is held to that a connected map can still get wrong: an open exit takes whoever steps on it, so an exit is a pocket a player steps *into*, never a through-tile - `mapStructure.testkit.ts` fails one that cuts ground off, and (`walksLengthenedBy()` in `mapStructure.ts`) one that any walk between two places is shorter over than round: district doorways, gates, insertions, landmarks and contract stops, asked again with each trainer's watch shut. A place of several tiles is as near as its nearest, so an exit may stand at the side of a two-tile lane. The Floodplain's Signal Fire stood in the neck between the keep's court and the causeway, so the sluice keeper's reveal ended the raid halfway along it; it is now a nub of stone under the tower, and no exit is excused. `raid.mjs --via=x:y,..` walks through tiles that are not a destination, which is how a reveal is walked; and a landmark is worked by walking over it, so one standing on a through-lane opens its sealed exit for anyone passing - Viridian's Fire Tower and Route 1's field station both did, and both now stand in a nook beside the lane.
- **A figure is collision, so wherever one stops is a door - and a door in the neck of a pocket is a wall.** The captain fled on 2026-09-19 and the hunter settled in the gap he had come through: for `HUNTER_SEARCH_MS` it is blind (it will not engage) and solid (it will not move), so he was sealed in with nothing to do about it. The answer is a rule about *who may occupy a tile*, held by `mapStructure.testkit.ts` per map and per gate state (`doorsFrom()` in `hunter.ts` names them). A figure the player may **walk into** may stand on a door, because walking into them is the way through it: the hunter is caught by (`WorldScene.tryWalkIntoHunter`, which routes into the same `hunterCatchCutscene` a pursuit contact plays, whatever the search window is doing), and a trainer is fought (`askForTrainerChallenge`, and a beaten one is never rebuilt, so the tile comes back for good) - both a price, neither a wall, and what each needs is that whoever is shut in can reach them. A figure the player may **not** walk into - a sign, a townsperson keeping a beat - may not stand on a door at all, at any tile of that beat, because there is nothing to pay and nothing to wait for. That is what makes the trap impossible by construction rather than unlikely, and it is why the map-side fix (the flee that no longer seals, above) is a preference rather than the guarantee. `hunterWall.test.ts` holds the scene half: the tile is still solid, walking into it still catches, and a hunter that is merely near is still just a bump.
- A caption is drawn beneath figures and a canopy above them, so writing seated under a tree crown or the gatehouse's walk-under cells would be hidden by the thing it is beside. `CaptionSurroundings.canopy` in `labelPlacement.ts` is the rule: canopy is ground a caption may not take, exactly as a person is, and the ordinary order of seats does the rest - the other row, slid along it, then beside the subject - so a shut gate whose keeper stands below it is captioned beside its arch rather than under its roof. `WorldScene.canopyInView()` hands over only the crowns in the camera's view, merged into row runs and recomputed when the view crosses a tile, because a wooded map has hundreds and this is asked every frame. The rule is strict, so on the map side keep forest trees out of the space a caption has to sit in - list what stands there with a script before removing one; a guessed tree was one that did not exist. Judge captions in the 3x `PIXEL_WINDOW`, never the 1x logic window: the small window gives the game a smaller stage, captions have less room in it, and a survey run there reported the toll bridge's caption hidden when every player sees it.
- Hedges, trees and tall grass are the same leafy art in the classic tileset, so on maps built out of them nothing distinguishes a wall from a lane. `TREE_TINT` and `TALL_GRASS_TINT` in `tileset/classicTileset.ts` are what does. A tint is declared per material on the catalogue and applied by `WorldScene.createTileLayer`, never by the scene naming tile numbers; tints multiply, so they can only darken - and a sheet with real art declares none.
- `public/assets/frlg-tiles.png` is the ground sheet, loaded as `frlgTiles`. It has the thing the 104-tile classic set has none of: transition art. Eight ground materials arrive with a complete 13-tile edge set including the four inner corners, which is what lets a path turn a corner instead of ending in a rectangular step. `src/game/world/frlgSheet.ts` is the addressing layer - constants only, no logic - and `frlgSheet.test.ts` reads every coordinate back out of the PNG, so a cut that lands on empty sheet fails there rather than in a playtest. Two things it cannot do: its trees are 3x3 with the trunk on the bottom row, and its tall grass and hedges are drawn as two-row beds, so neither is a one-tile wall. It is cut from a Pokemon FireRed/LeafGreen rip and the source sheet must never be committed - see `public/assets/ASSET_PROVENANCE.md`, which quotes the publisher's terms.
- **A caption is an answer, not a signpost.** The map used to draw a permanent window over every exit, landmark, gate, drop-in, contract stop and route boundary in view, and on the 400x256 stage seven at once left no map to walk - the captain could not see where he was going, and no seating rule could have fixed it because every one of those captions had found a legal seat. `src/game/ui/captionReveal.ts` is *whether* a caption speaks, as `labelPlacement.ts` is where it sits: a `name` speaks only while the player is within `CAPTION_NEAR_STEPS` (5) walking steps of any tile it is about; a `warning` (the trainer watch, `CANNOT BE FLED`) always speaks, because the step it prices is decided from wherever the shading can be seen; an `exit` speaks near, and also once it is **open** and the clock has gone red (`EXIT_CALL_MS` is `RAID_CLOCK_URGENT_MS` - the same second, on purpose). Holding **L** is the look: every caption in view speaks while it is held, which is the map-editor view asked for rather than imposed, and the objective chip teaches the key (`RAID_KEY_HINTS`). Steps rather than a square reach, because four across *and* four down is eight steps and named three things at once on Route 1's apron. A `WorldLabel` is built with a `speech` (`voice` plus the tiles it is about) and answers `describe(audience)` each frame; `CaptionRequest.speaks` carries that into the seating, where a silent caption is still an obstacle and still one of its group's doors in view. Nothing that was permanent is lost: the objective chip names the contract stop and its bearing, the arrival plate the district, the field guide the exits and what opens them. `tools/playtest/whyHidden.mjs` lists the silent captions and how far off they are, `--look` holds the key, and `tour.mjs --look` / `--remaining=SECONDS` photograph (a tour stop inside a cave or a room frames that place) the looked-at map and the last of a raid.
- A map caption is seated, not positioned: a `WorldLabel` is given the *rectangle of map it names* (`tileRect`/`figureRect`/`landingRect` in `WorldScene`) plus a preferred side, and `placeCaptions()` in `src/game/ui/labelPlacement.ts` seats every caption on the screen together each frame - authored side first, then the other row, then beside the subject, each slid along its edge to stay in view - taking the first seat clear of the view's edge, the raid HUD chips (`RaidHud.occupied`), the FEEDBACK tab where it stands over the canvas (`feedback/feedbackTabCover.ts`, a page element the scene reads each frame), every captioned subject, the `keepClear` list (`WorldScene.captionKeepClear()`: signs, crates, standing figures - the hunter among them, placed a tile at a time so a caption moves once a step rather than chasing it - and watched ground), an open dialogue box (passed as furniture, so a warning is hidden while a briefing is up rather than drawn half under it) and the captions already seated. One keeper's doors are one sentence: gate captions carry a `group` and, when two or more of a group's doors are on screen, `resolveGroups()` silences them and seats the one `speaksFor` caption (`jointGateCaption()` in `gates.ts` - OVERLOOK GATE + STEPS) by the first door with a clear seat, because seated one each the second door lost its ground to the first and to the keeper's warning, and a door nobody can read is a wall. The raid clock owns the screen's top-right corner and the camera stops at the map's edge, so nothing captioned stands in a map's north-east corner (the Overlook Stile did). Seating order is priority, and a warning outranks a name: a caption made with `warns` (only the trainer watch, whose third line is `CANNOT BE FLED`) is seated before every other, whatever order the scene created them in (`seatingOrder()`) - a boss stands at the gate they hold, and in creation order the gate's name took the one clear seat and the warning went undrawn on two of the Floodplain's three doors. Among names the one nearest the player is seated first (`CaptionRequest.nearness`, which `WorldLabel.describe` measures in steps): the harbour's doors stand two tiles apart, and in creation order OAK'S LAB took the row and BROCK'S WORKSHOP went unnamed with the player on its own doorstep (`base/doorCaptions.test.ts`). The player is the one obstacle that is conditional (`CaptionSurroundings.player`, from `WorldScene.captionPlayer()`: figure plus chevron, on whole tiles - standing and stepping-to - so a caption moves once as a step begins): a caption gives the player room while any other seat is clear and is offered its two rows again lifted out past the player after every ordinary seat, and only a `warns` caption keeps a seat *under* them rather than vanish - and, last of all, one over ground a name is about (Viridian City's Lass Ada stands between two terrace ledges named across their whole bands, and her CANNOT BE FLED was never drawn) - a name with nowhere else is hidden, because a name now speaks only once the player has walked up to the thing, which is exactly when they are in its way. One with no clear seat, or whose subject has scrolled off or is wholly under the dialogue box (`isCovered` - the Bunker's grunt was otherwise named over the player's head, above an empty corridor), is hidden rather than drawn cut or over something; a held seat is kept while it stays clear so nothing flickers. A caption missing in play is asked about, not guessed at: `tools/playtest/whyHidden.mjs <url> name:x:y ["CAPTION TEXT"]` stands the player there and prints what was wrong with every seat, what the caption names and what was seated before it. The same file's `placeDialog()` moves the world's dialogue box to the top when the bottom would cover whoever an `interrupt()` is about (the hunter arriving south of you). It is Phaser-free, so the rules are in `labelPlacement.test.ts` rather than eyeballed - extend it there, never by nudging a caption's coordinates in a scene.
- What is drawn over what on the overworld is `src/game/world/depths.ts`, and only there: terrain (the ground, overlay and detail tile layers), the watch shading, map art (`MARKER_BAND` - exits, caches, landmarks, contract stops, signs), captions (`CAPTION_BAND`), figures (`FIGURE_BAND`, y-sorted with the player), then `CANOPY_BAND` - the one band above the figures, which is what gives a wood an inside. The rule is that writing never covers a person - captions used to sit at depth 3-5 against a player at 2.007, so standing on the Floodplain ranger station hid the character completely. `atRow()` carries the y-sort; `depths.test.ts` holds the ordering and fails a scene that goes back to a raw `setDepth(3 + y / 1000)`.
- Every map names its parts: `src/game/world/districts.ts` holds each district as rectangles of tiles (first listed wins an overlap), and `WorldScene.noteDistrict()` raises the name as an **arrival plate** under the objective chip when the player walks into one - the way the games this is dressed as name a place - for `PLACE_PLATE_MS` of reading time, which does not run while a dialogue box is open (a raid opens on its briefing). Dropping in is an arrival; a battle handing the raid back is not (`arrivedFromBattle`), because the scene is rebuilt after every fight. It exists because a stranger who toured the Floodplain once could place every exit and gate from memory and still drew Old Town as two unrelated spots: every landmark said its own name and nothing said the town's. `districts.test.ts` holds that no walkable tile of a districted map is nameless and pins which place every exit, landmark and drop-in belongs to. Old Town is also held together on the ground - the town's own paving runs from the drowned street past the chapel and the last house to the South Gate, and its churchyard stones stand in the chapel's pool - because a name on a plate is not a substitute for a place reading as one.
- Every heading the raid prints - objective chip, hunter chip, field guide - is `compassBearing()` in `src/game/world/bearing.ts`, the nearest of eight points. Each used to name a bare quadrant, so a kit two tiles west and eleven south read `SW` under a briefing that called it SOUTH; `bearing.test.ts` holds the first briefing to the chip.
- The classic tileset has no water art - its `POND_WATER` tile is the same green as grass - so `CLASSIC_TILESET` declares `WATER_TINT` on the water material. Do not paint detail-layer decorations over water: they render as one-tile lawns in open water.
- Overworld movement only turns in place against a blocked tile (`planNextGridStep`), so a landmark on walkable ground can never be faced from beside it unless a lane runs *into* that neighbouring tile pointing at it. The Sluice Wheel had no such lane, so the West Culvert it opens had never been openable. `WorldScene.advanceStep()` therefore also works a landmark you stand on, as it already does for loot and contract stops. All three return the line to say rather than showing it, so a deed done on ground a trainer is watching arrives in the same dialogue as the challenge instead of silencing it; `pois.test.ts` pins that, and which landmarks still fail the facing rule.
