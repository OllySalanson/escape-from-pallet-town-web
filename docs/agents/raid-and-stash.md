# The raid and the stash

What a raid carries, costs and settles: Brock's ladder, materials, packs, the bag grid, the secure container, money and Bill, the clock, healing, reports and settlement, deployment and the drop-in picture.

Moved out of `AGENTS.md` on 9 Nov 2026 so that file stays small; nothing here was reworded. Keep it current the way `AGENTS.md` asks: a change that adds a subsystem or makes a note here untrue updates the note in the same change, and a new note goes in the doc whose area it belongs to.

## Notes

- **Brock's Workshop** is the stash's one sink: `src/game/hub/workshop.ts` holds the ladder of permanent base upgrades, paid for in **materials only**, and it is a sink rather than a shop - no currency, nothing handed back, capability or information only. It builds the *base* and only the base: the raid pack is deliberately not on this ladder and can never be, because a rung is permanent and a pack is a thing you lose (see the pack entry above). The save records only `raidProgress.workshopUpgrades`; every effect is derived from that list by the owner that already existed (secure slots in `contracts.ts`, the bay's `RecoveryTerms` in `recovery.ts` as shares of the raid, the mast's line in `raidHud.ts` from `hunterIntelFor`, the beacon in `runGeneration.ts`), and a raid reads the same ids off `ActiveRunSession.workshopUpgrades`. `SaveManager.buildWorkshopUpgrade()` is the one path that spends. **Brock is not the shop and Bill is not the lab**: one builds, the other deals - see the trader entry below. **Brock takes no Pokemon** (the captain, 2026-10-12, playtest 42 option A): when every rung also cost one to four Pokemon, a wipe took the team *and* froze the base, and eleven raids built nothing with materials to spare - every Pokemon a rung asked became one more unit of its material, 37 in all, and a rung is asked again in place before it is built (`HubScene.armUpgrade`, the same `armedDeal` Bill's barters use). The price is never the raid's own stock: potions, balls and antidotes are what staying alive spends, so a ladder priced in them competed with the raid, and the wipe restock would refund any kit spent into it (`Stash.spareCount()` still guards the whole price and only bites if a rung is priced in kit again).
- **Materials** (`radio-valve`, `cable-coil`, `parts-crate`, `lamp-oil`, `mooring-rope`, `linen-roll` - `MATERIAL_IDS` in `src/game/items/items.ts`, effect type `material`, the bag's Other pocket) exist only to feed Brock, and every rung's `cost.supplies` is made of them. They have no field, battle or treatment use, are never packed (`DeploymentFlow.adjustItem` refuses them and the loadout list hides them), are field loot from each map's pool in `worldMap.ts` (which are live varies per raid through `generateLoot`), and are no part of `MINIMUM_SUPPLIES`, so the wipe restock never hands one out or counts one. They must not become a faucet: their only exit is the ladder, and the standing board pays them only while a rung still wants them (`workshopMaterialKinds`), falling back to `FINISHED_BASE_PAY` supplies once the ladder is built. **A material is found, not brought, so the secure container holds *room* for it:** `adjustSecureItem` sets aside squares for a kind the loadout never packed, and what fills them is decided by the pack at the end (`survivingSecureItems`); `validateSecureSlot` exempts them from the "was in the loadout" check; and `Stash.applyWipeLoss` adds a secured material back because its loop over brought items never met it, so its caller must hand over the slot already cut to what the pack held. A new material needs a `MATERIAL_SHARE` entry, an icon (drawn by `scripts/draw-material-icons.mjs`) and a rung that asks for it; `items.test.ts` holds that. Money shares every one of those found-only rules, which is why the rule is asked as `isFoundOnly()` (material **or** currency) everywhere it means "found in a raid rather than packed for one", while `isMaterial()` still means only "Brock takes it".
- **A pack is gear, not an upgrade, and a lost raid takes it with everything in it.** The stash grows for good - that is Brock and the secure container - but the thing a haul is carried in is an item you own, choose before every raid and lose if you do not walk out, which is what makes the choice fresh every time. `src/game/items/packs.ts` is the whole of it and its header carries the reasoning; four sizes six squares apart (Satchel 12, Raid pack 18, Ranger pack 24, Hauler frame 30), authored as `effect: { type: 'pack', grid }` rows in `items.ts` so **the size is in one place and derived, never stored** - the save records which packs are in the vault because they are items, exactly as a workshop effect is derived from a list of ids. `DeploymentFlow.packItemId` opens on the biggest pack at base (a default, never a cage, like the secure container filling itself) and `bagGrid` is read off it, which is the one seam: change what feeds that and the pack has changed everywhere. It rides into the raid on `RunLoadout.packItemId` and `ActiveRunSession.packItemId`, because the worn pack is never in the bag - it **is** the bag - so nothing in the supply accounting can name it and the secure container cannot protect it; `SaveManager.applyWipeLoss()` takes it *after* the supply accounting, so a *spare* pack found in the field is ordinary loot and is protected by kind like a material (a player who wore a Ranger pack and found another keeps the one they secured). Packs are `isFoundOnly` - never packed at base, found in the field - and excluded from `SupplyItemId`, so no contract, shelf or rung can ever hand one out. **Nobody is stranded**: the Satchel is a line of `MINIMUM_SUPPLIES`, and `servesAs` reads the kit as a capability, so any pack answers it - `createStartingStash` issues the Raid pack *before* the restock runs or a new save would open in a Satchel, and the loader's `ensureAPack()` repairs a save written before packs with the Raid pack so nobody loses squares. Packs are rolled field loot on every map (the two vast ones hold the big ones); a found pack costs four squares to carry out. On a screen a pack is a still 16x16 icon (`scripts/draw-pack-icons.mjs`, held by `items/packIcons.test.ts`); **on the figure it is baked into their sprite sheet** (`look/wornPack.ts`, drawn as rows of pixels on a FireRed figure's own rows and moved for the classic figure and the Bicycle's rider): one texture per look and pack, composed once by `lookTexture(scene, look, pack)`/`bikeTexture(scene, pack)` and never a second sprite, so it bobs, turns and falls with the figure and costs a frame nothing. Layers say which way it sits (`behind`, the figure, `front`, the head, `over`), a step's shift is read off the frames (`stepShift`), and nothing about it is a stat. A raid wears `runSession.packItemId`; the base wears the rack's choice in the hall and otherwise `bestPackIn(vault)` (`BaseScene.wornPack`), and other players hear it as `b` on a harbour move (`readHarbourMove`, believed only through `isPackId`). The title's chase and the boutique's mirror draw no pack. `npx vite-node tools/look/wornPacks.mts -- out.png` draws every pack on every body; `tools/playtest/wornPacks.mjs` plays it. Screens and the whole derivation: `docs/screens/packs/`.
- **The Bicycle is the one bet a raid can carry** (the captain, 2026-10-12): FireRed's own, ridden from the raid pack (USE / WALK, `BagScene.rideOrWalk` through `BagSceneData.bike`), twice the pace anywhere but a room of kind `inside` - FireRed's `allow_cycling`, which caves and the Underground Path have - and through a door into a room is off it for good, as FireRed's warp is. `world/bicycle.ts` is the whole rule. It is **3x2, the one item wider than two** (`itemGrid.test.ts` names it), so it is a third of a Raid pack and wider than the starting secure container; it is a prize at 5% on two maps and Bill's dearest barter, dealt again when the vault holds none (`reofferedWhenNoneHeld`). **A ridden tile is still one step**: only `stepDurationFor` changes, so the hunter's step-lock, the grass roll, the survey and the idle step (which counts time stood still) are untouched and the raid clock - charged for time - is what it buys; FireRed's 80% bike encounter rate is deliberately not followed. Riding rides through fights on `ActiveRunSession.riding` and is re-asked by `WorldScene.settleRiding` on every rebuild, door and pack change; the figure is `player-bike.png` (32x32 frames, origin a quarter in). `scenes/bicycle.test.ts` drives all of it through the real scene.
- **The bag is chequered squares, and each item takes a different number of them** (the captain's own words, 2026-09-19). `ItemDefinition.footprint` is authored beside an item's name in `src/game/items/items.ts` - nothing is more than two squares on a side - and `itemGrid.ts` is the only module that knows a container has corners: `packContents(contents, size, cargo, arrangement)` seats what the arrangement names, packs whatever is left, and returns where each piece sits, what would not go in, and how many squares are used. **Contents and layout are two different things and only one of them is the inventory.** Contents are still `BagContents`, one record of id to quantity, so the wipe ledger, the supply delta, the result screen and every count are untouched by the grid; a **layout** is a `GridArrangement`, a list of *seats* (a piece, a square, and whether it is lying down) that can only say where something sits and never that it is there - see the arranging entry below. `containers.ts` holds the two sizes a save starts with (`RAID_BAG_GRID` 6x3, `BASE_SECURE_GRID` 2x2); both grow where the thing that pays for them lives - `raidBagGridFor()` in `hub/workshop.ts` off a `bagRow` rung, wired with nothing selling it yet, and `secureGrid()` in `objectives/contracts.ts`, a column per cordon ledger and per locker. `Bag` carries a `capacity` and `add()` now returns false when a thing will not fit; every caller already checked that boolean, so loot pickup, boss gear and the run's collection seam all refuse out loud without new wiring. The vault's bag has a `null` capacity on purpose: a warehouse has no size, and capping it would make banking a raid a thing that can fail. A material is two or four squares against a Potion's one, which is what makes a good raid the raid where the pack runs out before the map does - and `itemGrid.test.ts` holds the wipe restock kit to fitting the starting pack, because that promise is the one place a player has nothing left to trade. A container also holds **cargo** - see the next entry - which is a `GridCargo` rather than a catalogue row, seated before every supply and listed separately in `GridPacking`, so nothing that counts items ever meets one.
- **The player lays their own pack out, and what they laid out is what they get back** (the captain's ruling, 2026-09-20: eleven of eighteen squares used, seven free, and a four-square find refused). Two halves. The **model** is `GridArrangement` in `items/itemGrid.ts` - a list of seats, honoured *before* anything is packed automatically, so adding a Potion can never re-shuffle what somebody placed; a seat for something no longer carried is dropped, a seat the container has outgrown is given back, and an empty arrangement is the automatic pack exactly as it was. `items/gridArrange.ts` is the rules of moving one piece (pure: `canSeat`, `seatPiece`, `turnPiece`, `pieceAt`), and picking anything up **freezes the whole picture first** (`arrangementOf`), which is what makes "only the thing you moved moves" true rather than hoped for. The **screen** is `ui/gridArranging.ts`, shared by the loadout, the secure container and the in-raid bag: every block is a button, ENTER takes and puts down, the arrow keys carry a square at a time, R turns, ESC puts it back, a pointer drags, and at base (loadout and secure container, never the raid bag, where taking out is dropping loot) a right-click or DEL takes one of a piece back to the vault (`GridArrangingHost.remove`) - all of it operable with no mouse, because a block the cursor cannot reach can never be picked up. `pixelGrid`'s `arrange` option is what draws it, and the ghost over the target square, green or red, *is* the answer to "will this go here". Three things that cost time to learn. **Rotation is not a convenience**: three 2x2 blocks in a 6x3 pack fill two whole rows between them, so the captain's case cannot be solved without laying the 1x2 Super Potion down - which is why first-fit now falls through to `searchSeats`, an exact backtracking search with rotation, whenever it turns something away (budgeted, and never run on the vault, which has no size). **An auto-tidy is worse than no arranging at all**, so TIDY is a control the player presses and never something that happens to them - except that a *find* is not on a screen they can rearrange from, so `Bag.takeFind` and `clearPackRoomForPokemon` re-seat the pack around a find or a catch rather than refusing it, and say so. And **pressing on an item's icon starts Chromium's own image drag**, which swallows the rest of the gesture: the block's children are `pointer-events: none` and `pointerdown` prevents its default, which is why the block takes the focus by hand. The layout rides in `raidProgress.packArrangement`/`secureArrangement` (optional, empty default, no version bump), into the raid on the `Bag`, and home again on **every** ending: `WorldScene.recordRaid` for extraction and the clock, and `BattleScene.resolveRunWipe` for a raid lost in a fight - which is the one that was forgotten, and which no unit test can see, because a raid's endings are three scenes and the suite has no `localStorage`. `tools/playtest/packArrange.mjs` plays the whole of it - keyboard and pointer, base and raid - and `docs/screens/pack-arrange/` is what it looks like.
- **A Pokemon carried home takes four squares, six when it has evolved once and nine when it has evolved twice** (the captain's ruling, 2026-09-19). `src/game/pokemon/pokemonCargo.ts` is the whole rule: the stage is counted off `evolution.ts`'s own table rather than stored on a species, so importing the other 144 needs no footprint written for any of them, and the shapes are 2x2, 3x2 and 3x3 because the grid is drawn. **Your deployed party is not cargo** - six at four squares each would be 24 against an 18-square pack - so what costs squares is what you are *bringing back*: `RunSnapshot.caughtPokemon`, which gifts join, turned into pieces by `run/raidCargo.ts` and handed to `Bag.setCargo()` on every scene that rebuilds the world (re-derived, never counted up, because a battle tears the overworld down and hands the same pack back). Three catches is twelve of eighteen squares, which is the decision: catching costs the room you were keeping for Potions and loot. A pack with no room **refuses the catch before the ball is thrown** (`BattleScene.throwBall`) and the giver keeps their gift and says so (`PokemonGift.offerNoRoomLine`) - losing it afterwards would be the same fact told dishonestly, and finding out should not cost a ball. In the **secure container** those squares are the container's own, so the 2x2 every save starts with holds exactly one first-stage Pokemon and nothing else, an Ivysaur needs a column first, and a fully evolved Pokemon is three squares tall against a container that only ever grows columns - it can never be secured, which is why `secure-locker-2` now brings a column as well as a slot (two Pokemon are eight squares; `workshop.test.ts` states that as the one rung allowed two effects). The locker says so rather than advising what cannot work: `hub/secureGrowth.ts` lists the columns a save can still get (Brock's unbuilt rungs, Bill's berth, a contract on a map in play), `LoadoutCapacity.secureGrowth` carries them, and `DeploymentFlow.secureFit` answers `fits`, `grow` or `never`, so a refusal says "take something out" only when emptying would seat the Pokemon, names a way to grow only while one is left, and tells a third stage plainly that a wipe will take it - the row's help line and `secureRoomNote()` ask the same (the characters bug hunt, 11 Nov 2026, found a Charizard told to do both with every locker built). None of it ever reaches the partner, which is not the container's to protect at all (two entries down).
- **A refusal for space is a doorway, never an instruction the player cannot follow.** The pack refusing a catch used to say "drop something from your BAG first" - a screen a battle has no door to, so the only obedient move was to flee, which loses the Pokemon the refusal was about (the captain, 2026-09-20). The refusal now *is* the choice: `packRoomChoices()` in `run/raidCargo.ts` prices every kind in the pack in squares and asks the packer whether putting one down is enough on its own (four free squares scattered round a Potion are not a seat for a 2x2 Pidgey), and `BattleScene`'s `make-room` mode draws them in the battle panel's own frame - one page of six, the prompt answering the row the cursor is on, `KEEP THE PACK` as the first row so declining is as visible as dropping, and the cursor opens on it, because the press that read the refusal is often followed by another and a drop is for good (the characters bug hunt, 11 Nov 2026). The ball is **held by id** and thrown the moment a drop has bought the room; the last of that ball is never offered, or the list would invite a second trap in the same breath. The drop costs no turn of its own - the throw that follows is the turn the player meant to spend - and what it costs is the item, for good. The whole class is held to the same question, *can the player do this from where they are standing*: ground loot and a gift are refused with the thing still there and the bag one key away, a piece of a searched cache the pack will not take stays in the cache with the panel saying `NO ROOM FOR X: [B] PACK` (the search remembers what was taken, so nothing pays twice), and a beaten boss's gear now **waits** in `RaidCarriage.unclaimedBossGear` until there is room instead of being destroyed with nothing the player could have done. `tools/playtest/packFull.mjs` is the only way any of it is reached end to end; `docs/screens/pack-full/` is what it looks like. A thing put down - X on a pocket row, or the battle's make-room drop - lies on the tile the player stood on for the rest of the raid (`layDroppedPiece` in `world/loot.ts`, kept on `ActiveRunSession.droppedOnTheGround` because a fight rebuilds the world), and stepping back onto it takes the drop back rather than recording a find (`RunManager.registerPickedBackUp`). A disc read in the pack plays a two-second moment any key skips (`teachMomentBody`); `tools/playtest/raidPackFixes.mjs` plays both.
- **The secure container fills itself, Pokemon first, by level, and the partner has a place of its own** (the captain's addition, 2026-09-19), so nobody deploys with their best unprotected because they did not open a screen. `src/game/hub/secureAutofill.ts` is the rule and `DeploymentFlow.refillSecureSlot()` runs it on every loadout change *until the player touches the container*, after which it is theirs - a default, never a cage. When no Pokemon in the party fits, it secures the packed medicine instead, without remembering that as the player's preference. **The partner is never in it: it has a protected place of its own beside the container** (the captain's call of 11 Nov 2026, option B, after a bug hunt found every evolved starter deleted by its first wipe - six squares against the 2x2, nine against a container that is always two tall - which the 7 Nov partner-first ruling could not reach). `Stash.applyWipeLoss` keeps the vault's partner by its own mark whatever the slot says, counted against neither the container's Pokemon nor its squares, so every ending that wipes honours it without being told; the run carries it as `SecureSlot.partner` so the report, the defeat beats and the raid's pack screen can say so; and `DeploymentFlow.partnerOnRaid` is how the locker's PARTNER row, the rim's Safe line, the rail stamp and the marshal know (it is never `riskedPokemon`). The container then fills itself highest level first among the rest; the starter's line leads it only on a save whose partner was lost before the place existed. A save that laid the partner out in the container has that seat taken out on load (`partnerOutOfTheContainer` in `SaveManager.ts`). Every other Pokemon's risk is exactly what the container makes it. What is remembered for next raid is `raidProgress.securePreference`: a **flag** for the Pokemon half, because it is a policy and the ids change every raid, and the supplies as themselves. It is optional with a default, so every accepted save version keeps loading with no version bump (`SaveManager.test.ts` pins that), and `HubScene` writes it on the way out of the lobby rather than on the way home, because a raid that goes badly chose it too. The sharp edge is stated on screen rather than worked around: the Pokemon rows carry their price in squares, and `HubScene.secureRoomNote()` says why nothing else fits and what would change it.
- **The secure container was filled at the door, and what it accepted is what it is holding.** A Pokemon takes squares by evolution stage and can evolve mid-raid, so a starter secured as a four-square first stage is a six-square second stage by the time the clock runs out - and re-measuring it at settlement threw out of `WorldScene.update`, with the raid already lost and nothing to catch it. `RunManager` records `secureFootprintsValue` when the slot is filled and `validateSecureSlot` takes those squares rather than measuring the Pokemon again, so the fit is still checked and growing is not a reason to lose protection. They are matched **by the Pokemon, never by the slot object** (`acceptedFootprints`): every scene resolves a wipe with the run session's slot, which is not the manager's copy, and an identity check on the slot froze every lost raid with an evolved secured Pokemon (playtest 32 #1) - `consecutiveRaids.test.ts` runs the clock out through the real `WorldScene` for it. Reachable by anything that can evolve, which since the import is most of the roster.
- The secure slot is lists all the way down - `SecureSlot.pokemon` on the run side, `pokemonIds` on the stash side - because the second locker protects two. How much a wipe protects is read from the save inside `SaveManager.applyWipeLoss()`, never passed by a scene: `Stash.applyWipeLoss` used to cap stacks at a literal two, which destroyed the third stack the cordon ledger pays for while the result screen reported it safe.
- **Money is loot, never a score, and Bill is where it goes.** It is the **Pokedollar** (the captain, 2026-09-23), one catalogue row with the id `money` (`items.ts`, effect type `currency`, the Other pocket, a bundle to a square through `stackSize`): found in every map's loot pool, carried in the pack, refused by `DeploymentFlow.adjustItem` because it does nothing in a raid, and destroyed with the pack on a wipe unless the secure container reserved squares for it. There is no wallet anywhere in a save - a player's money is `stash.itemCount('money')` and nothing else, which is what the captain's ruling of 2026-09-19 means by loot. `src/game/hub/trader.ts` is the whole trader and carries the four binding constraints in its header; `SaveManager.buyTraderStock/takeTraderBarter/buyTraderBerth` are the only paths that spend, and `HubScene`'s `trader` view only renders. **Nothing at his counter spends on one press**: a shelf row, a barter and the berth each arm a question (`HubScene.traderArmed`, `armedDeal`) with the cursor on KEEP THEM, and his cottage's YES/NO box opens on NOT NOW for every deal (`billTalk.ts`'s `spendsForGood`), because a double ENTER on a Potion bought two and spent the trip's ration (the midgame bug hunt of 11 Nov 2026; `tools/playtest/billShelf.mjs` presses both twice). A new way to spend at base asks the same way, or swallows the second press inside `RELEASE_ARMING_MS` as the Boutique's BUY does. Three parts, and they must stay three: **standing** derived from what has been banked (contracts, bosses, and Pokedollars *turned over* - never held, `traderMoneySpent`), which gates everything; a **shelf** of supplies only, typed `SupplyItemId` so the compiler refuses a price tag on gear, gated by standing and by a per-raid ration (`traderRationUsed`, cleared by `RAID_RESOLVED` like the ward's bed) and priced above what a raid finds, so buying supplies can never make raid scarcity decorative; and a **barter table** of gear and the evolution stone, paid in found goods and never in money, so the best things cannot be bought at any price. The **berth** is the heavy money drain and the one sentence that tells the two base screens apart: Brock *builds* a secure-container column for good, Bill *rents* the same column for one trip (`traderBerthPaid`, also cleared by `RAID_RESOLVED`, and read by `secureGrid()`'s third required argument). Every price is set against the faucet, which is the money in `worldMap.ts`'s pools - `trader.test.ts` recomputes that from the maps themselves, so a redrawn map that pays out too much fails there rather than in a playtest. **An amount is written `₽40`, never "40 Pokedollars"**: `formatMoney` for a sum, `itemAmountFor` in a sentence and `itemCountTag` in a row's count, because these games print money as a sum with the sign in front. Orange Kid has no glyph for the sign (U+20BD, the rouble sign everybody types for the Pokedollar), so `scripts/draw-pokedollar-glyph.mjs` draws one on Orange Kid's own grid into `public/assets/battle/pokedollar.ttf`, a one-glyph TrueType face joined to the `'Orange Kid'` family by `unicode-range` in `style.css` - which covers the DOM and the canvas's bitmap text in one move, since both ask the family - preloaded by `index.html` and loaded by `awaitGameFont()` through `GAME_FONT_SAMPLE`, because a face behind a range is not fetched until its character is drawn. It carries Orange Kid's vertical metrics number for number so a line with a price in it is no taller. The id was `scrip` until then, and a save is wire format: every item id read out of a save goes through `currentItemId()` (`RETIRED_ITEM_IDS` in `items.ts`) and `traderScripSpent` is read as `traderMoneySpent`, so renaming an item again is a row there, not a version bump. `items/currency.test.ts` fails any `scrip` left in the game's own strings. The name on screen is `Pokédollars`, accented like Poké Ball. Screens: `docs/screens/pokedollars/`.
- The quarantine ward's free bed is per-raid state, not an upgrade effect, so it is stored: `wardTreatmentsUsed` rides beside `pendingRecoveryMs` and is cleared by the same `RAID_RESOLVED` on every raid ending, so a reload cannot hand it back. `wardBedIds()` gives the bed to whoever it saves the most on across everyone hurt at base, which is what makes a row's listed price the price whether it is clicked alone or in "recover all"; it never waives the revive.
- **An abandoned raid costs its pack** (the captain's ruling on playtest 11's B1): the vault in storage is the pre-raid vault until a raid ends, so a tab closed, a reload or a crash used to refund the whole raid. `HubScene.startRun` now writes `SaveData.raidInFlight` (`run/raidInFlight.ts`: the map, the pack, what was packed and what the secure container held), every ending clears it through `RAID_RESOLVED`, and `BaseScene.create` calls `SaveManager.settleAbandonedRaid` before anything in the base can spend: the pack and everything in it are lost (the secure container keeps what was packed into it, never anything found), the team comes home as the vault holds it, the kit is restocked as after a wipe, and the base's dialogue box says so (`abandonedRaidLines`). Two tabs share one save, so a raid being played in another tab is left alone: the deploying page writes a pulse beside the save (`raidHeartbeat`, every 5s, believed for 90s because Chromium throttles a hidden tab to once a minute), and only a fresh pulse from another page counts - `pagehide` takes the pulse away, so a reload or a tab closed and reopened settles at once, and a page is named for its own life because a duplicated tab inherits session storage. A crash leaves a pulse that looks live, so the page also holds the raid's Web Lock (`raidLockName`) and its pulse says so (`locked`); the rim and `BaseScene` ask `RaidHeartbeat.stillLiveElsewhere` before believing a fresh pulse, and a pulse whose page held the lock that nobody holds now is dropped and the raid settled at once - the rim used to refuse with "Finish it there first" for up to 90 seconds after a crash (raids bug hunt, 9 Nov 2026). `HubScene.startRun` starts the run in memory before it writes the deployment or the raid in flight, so a run the manager refuses costs nothing. The explorer and try-it saves never record one. `scenes/abandonedRaid.test.ts` plays the reload, the close, the crash and the second tab. An abandoned raid has paid for nothing (its length is not known, so it is read as the shortest): it keeps the recovery debt, the ward's bed and Bill's ration for the next raid that runs, exactly as a raid out before the first hunter does (`paidOff` in `SaveManager.ts`; a reload used to make every heal free).
- **The raid in flight owns its Pokemon and its pack until it ends, whichever tab the base is open in** (the 9 Nov bug hunts). Two tabs share one save, and the vault in storage is the pre-raid vault until the raid's ending writes its own copy of every Pokemon it took back over it and charges what it spent against the supplies - so a heal, a TM, gear taken off or given, a Day-Care stay or a packed Potion spent in the other tab's base was undone, doubled or let a Pokemon dodge its wipe. `RaidInFlight` names the deployed stash ids (`pokemonIds`) and what the base owed when it left (`owed`); `SaveManager.save` refuses any write that keeps the raid in flight and changes one of those Pokemon or spends a packed supply below what was packed (`raidHoldRefusal`), so no screen can get round it, and the screens say so first in their own words (`awayOnRaid`, `spareAtBase`, `outOnRaidLine`: BILL'S PC tags the row ON A RAID, Joy heals only who is at base, the Day-Care Man refuses, YOUR THINGS refuses). Every ending is handed the raid's id (`ActiveRunSession.raidId`) and settles only the raid still in flight (`endsRaidInFlight`, `SaveManager.ownsRaid`): a raid settled as abandoned while its tab was frozen comes home to a result screen that says another window settled it (`ExtractionReport.settledElsewhere`) and writes nothing, and its heartbeat stops rather than overwrite the next raid's pulse. An ending pays off only what was owed at deployment, so a heal booked in the other tab mid-raid is still owed by the next raid. `scenes/twoTabRaid.test.ts` plays each order the hunts played.
- The beacon exit stands on the insertion tile itself (`beaconExit()` in `runGeneration.ts`), which is what lets one upgrade serve every map and every drop-in point without authoring a tile: the landing is reachable by construction and no trainer watch may cover it. It carries an authored `elapsed` requirement at half the *deployed* clock and spends no randomness, so a seed plays the same raid with or without it.
- A level-up never deletes a move. `Pokemon.gainExperience` learns a move into a free slot and otherwise queues it on `Pokemon.pendingMoves` (returned as `movesToChoose`); `resolvePendingMove(move, forgetIndex | null)` is the only thing that forgets or declines, and `pendingMoves` is saved beside `moves`. The question is one reusable pixel-ui screen, `openMoveChooser()` (`ui/MoveChooserOverlay.ts`, markup and wording in `ui/moveChooser.ts`), written against a Pokemon and a move so a TM or tutor asks it too. Two askers: `BattleScene` (a `BattleNote` with `offerMove` opens it once the announcing line is read, then re-syncs the combatant's move list) and `HubScene.offerPendingMoves()` on entering base, the only place with someone to answer - Escape there is "decide later" - that Pokemon is skipped until the save next deploys and the next queued one is asked (`hub/pendingMoves.ts`, keyed by `raidsDeployed`, nothing stored) - in a fight it is "do not learn". A raid's answer travels home in `RaidCondition.moves`/`pendingMoves`, because settlement replays experience on a reloaded Pokemon and would otherwise re-derive the level-up and lose the choice; a condition with no moveset (nobody was asked) leaves the move queued for base. The chooser ignores input for `MOVE_CHOOSER_ARMING_MS` because it opens on the Space that finished the last line and a Space *release* activates the focused button. Levelling can evolve, learn and offer in one call, and the beats go in that order: XP, grew, evolved (the sprite and plate change with that line), learned (a free slot), then the chooser for a move with no room - a Charmeleon reaching 36 becomes Charizard and is offered Wing Attack; held gear is untouched by the choice. `restoreMoveset` resolves names through the whole evolution line, as the save loader does. Ivysaur, Charizard and Pidgeot-line learnsets pass four moves, so this path is live; tests that need a fifth use `Charmeleon` at 35.
- Every path back into play after a wipe must leave the player able to attempt a run: `MINIMUM_SUPPLIES` and `Stash.restockMinimumSupplies()` in `src/game/stash/Stash.ts` own that guarantee, and `Stash.ensurePlayable()` and `SaveManager.applyWipeLoss()` route through it. `Stash.swapStarter()` deliberately does not: the swap is reachable without a wipe, and a wiped player was already restocked by the wipe before the swap was on offer. It is a last resort and not an allowance: `Stash.supplyShortfall()` counts anything that does the same job towards a kit line (a Super Potion is a Potion, a Great Ball is a Poke Ball, an Antidote is neither), the kit is the same for every save, and only the shortfall is handed over - so it never removes anything, cannot be farmed, and gives nothing to a vault that can already field a raid. Supplies are meant to be scarce enough that banking them matters; do not add a faucet that refills itself.
- **Boxes are where a Pokemon is kept, not who owns it.** `Stash` keeps the flat `listPokemon()` and a box assignment beside it (`BOX_CAPACITY` 30, `listBoxes()`, `movePokemon()`, `renameBox()`, `addBox()`, `removeBox()`), every Pokemon in exactly one box and never two, so the swap-partner offer (`canSwapStarter`), the loadout, the secure slot and the wipe all read the whole vault and a boxed Pokemon is exactly as spendable and deployable as before. Read `listPokemon()` for "what do I own"; `listBoxPokemon()` is for a screen showing one box. A full vault grows a box in `addPokemon`, so banking never fails. The save is `SavedStash.boxes`, optional with no version bump (absent = everything in box one; unknown ids dropped, unboxed ones placed by `Stash`'s constructor), pinned in `SaveManager.test.ts`. A stored Pokemon of a species the game does not know is not loaded, but `keepUnreadPokemon` in `SaveManager.save` carries it into every write exactly as stored, so a rename or a newer build's species is never destroyed by the next save; a new game from the title clears the file first. Finding beats capacity: `src/game/hub/stashBrowser.ts` (pure) is the sort and find - as kept, species, level, fit first; a name-prefix search across every box - and `HubScene.browseBar()` leads the stash and the loadout's Pokemon list with it. How the player is looking (`boxScope`, `stashSort`, `stashSearch`) is scene state and never saved. A box name is printed raw on a dozen DOM lines, so `tidyBoxName` takes `< > & "` out of every name typed or loaded - a box named `<!--` once blanked the whole Pokemon Center. The stash's text fields (rename, find) are the one place the overlay keyboard lets a field own its keys, so their Enter is the form's submit and their Escape and arrows are read on key *up* (`wireBoxForm`); `MenuOverlay`'s cursor rests on inputs. Screens: `docs/screens/storage-boxes/`.
- **"How many?" is one widget, and its ceiling is worked out by whoever owns the limit.** `src/game/ui/countSelector.ts` is the +/- pair with a running total (markup and the key rule, Phaser-free); `HubScene.stepCount()` is the one place a change lands, for the loadout (`DeploymentFlow.packLimit`/`setItemQuantity`, the pack's squares), the secure container (`secureLimit`/`setSecureSquares`, in squares beside cargo) and Bill's shelf and table (`traderStockLimit` - the smaller of the ration and the purse, `traderBarterLimit` - what the vault's goods cover; `buyTraderStock`/`takeTraderBarter` take a quantity and charge money, ration and goods for every one). The selector shows only numbers that will be accepted: the plus at the ceiling is `aria-disabled` and its help line is the reason, a held key or End clamps to it, and a request bigger than fits is met as far as it goes. Left/Right step by one, Shift or Page Up/Down by five, Home/End to the ends; Up/Down walk from one selector to the next of the same kind in reading order (`neighbourCountIndex`), because a list of counts laid out in columns put the Poke Ball stepper beside the Potion one where no arrow key could reach it, and leave the list at either end. The workshop has no selector because nothing there is a quantity - a rung's price is fixed and its payment is a chosen set of Pokemon - so do not add one for symmetry. A row with a selector beside it is a `.px-pair` (the row still acts, the selector says how many). Screens: `docs/screens/count-selector/`.
- What a raid takes is `DeploymentFlow` in `src/game/hub/deploymentFlow.ts`: choices made in any order on THE RAID ARENA's floor, `confirm`ed only by looking down from the rim. **The stash's medicine is packed by default** (the captain, 2026-09-23, after a playtest lost its first fight with an empty bag), and `BALLS_PREPACKED` Poké Balls beside it: `packMedicine()` fills up to `MEDICINE_PREPACK_SHARE` (half) of the pack's squares, each row says "packed for you" while `isPrepacked()` holds, and it is a default, never a cage - any row can be taken out, and a change of pack packs the untouched default again rather than refusing a smaller pack over squares the player never chose (`chosenContents`). `HubScene` renders its panels; only `deploy()` on a confirmed flow yields a party, so nothing outside the flow may pre-select a raid party or start a run without the rim. The flow itself puts a vault's **only** Pokémon in the party by default when it can fight (`packLonePokemon`, playtest 47; a fainted Pokémon is never packed - its row says FAINTED - and a lone one revived from the loadout goes in), on the medicine's terms - and **the bench comes back as it was left** (`raidProgress.lastBench`, written at the door, read by `packLastBench`; anybody lost or fainted is simply not there), because home, SPACE and ENTER are all going again asks of a team of any size - so a driver adds a Pokémon with `takePokemon()` in `tools/playtest/deploy.mjs`, which never clicks a row already in the party back out.
- **What a whole map is known to hold, and a map that is dark where nobody has walked.** The rim's card says only what a raid has at stake (its entry below); all of this is read on the wall map's close-up, where a list is what was asked for. `src/game/hub/dropIn.ts` is what it says, Phaser-free: the place's own description, its **grade** (four rungs of level, `PLACE_GRADE_LEVELS` - the levels the old hunter ladder opened on - placed by the highest level met on the ground reached from the front door before the first door, keepers left out: `entryGround`, the captain's call on playtest 6's D3, because graded on the whole map the starting map read a rung above the maps it unlocks), its ways out and doors **named only once found** and counted otherwise (`ExitLine.known`/`DoorLine.known`, `stillToFind` - the wall map's close-up too), and the wildlife of *only the places the survey has reached*. Difficulty is never typed in and never measured live: `world/encounterMeasure.ts` gives the real win rate but is two hundred battles a level, so the screen states levels, which are facts. `src/game/world/minimap.ts` draws the picture as character art - one character a tile, in the map's own alphabet, with a palette - built every frame from `WorldMapDefinition.terrain` (materials, added for this) and the collision beside it in the gate state the save has earned, so a redrawn map cannot leave a stale image. **It is drawn as big as the window lets it be**: the map is a window down the whole left of the screen, and `ui/mapPicture.ts` fits it in the overlay's measured pass (`MenuOverlay.onMeasure`) at the largest whole number of game pixels to the tile (`fitPicture`), condensing several tiles to a pixel only below that, with every mark ringed once a tile is big enough to ring (`paintMinimap`). The frame is sized for the biggest map (`own` fit) so nothing moves when the cursor changes the place; `tools/tileset/minimap.mts` draws the same picture big enough to criticise, and `tools/tileset/sampleSurvey.mts` writes a walked-ground record to photograph it with. **The dark is not blank**: unwalked ground is shaded a four-tile block at a time by what is mostly in it, water in a colour of its own, so a map keeps a coastline and a treeline without giving away a lane, a door or a clearing - a full bird's-eye view of an unexplored map would hand over the answer the vast maps are built to make you go and find. What lights it is `raidProgress.surveyed` (a bitset per map, `world/survey.ts`; a whole Floodplain is under a kilobyte) plus two derived lights that cannot go stale - every landing the player holds, which is what stops a fresh save being a black square, and every gate their beaten bosses opened, which is the payoff loop: push further, come back to base, watch the dark retreat. The survey is accumulated on `ActiveRunSession.surveyed` as the player walks (a disc, `SURVEY_RADIUS`, not the camera's view - a map uncovered a screen at a time is never dark again) and written once per raid ending, because a save is the whole game serialised and a step is 150ms. `raidProgress.raidRecord` counts raids per map, deployed where it is committed to and closed where it ends. Both default to empty and need no version bump; `SaveManager.test.ts` pins that. Every row of the "down there" pane is a focusable `aria-disabled` button, because a pane with no controls in it cannot be scrolled with the arrow keys. Screens and the whole derivation, from when this was a screen of its own: `docs/screens/drop-in/`.
- Anything that is a recovery route rather than a next step belongs where the thing it acts on lives - the swap-partner offer and each Pokémon's own price are on BILL'S PC, beside the Pokémon they act on. Preparation never packs a party. Every step must fit `BASE_STAGE` without a browser scrollbar - lists scroll inside their own window (`.px-scroll`), the screen never does, and a pane with more below its fold says so in words - a ruled strip reading `9 MORE` with a drawn arrow, over a drawn scrollbar track (`data-more`, set by `MenuOverlay.markScrollCues()`; three pixels of soft ink was never a cue, and an arrow alone never said whether the row you want is one down or thirty). How far below the fold is asked in *game* pixels, not screen pixels, or half a game pixel of text rounding is a MORE strip at 4x and nothing at 2x - and every committing action lives in the full-width `pixelCommitBar`, never at the bottom of a column.
- The raid duration is `RAID_DURATION_MS` in `src/game/run/raidClock.ts`, which also records the route measurement it was derived from; `raidClock.test.ts` holds the relationships a change must not break (every hunter tier reachable, every exit openable in time, the objective route never a sprint, the flee cost a visible share). The clock only advances inside `WorldScene.update`, so battles, the bag, the party screen and the field guide are all free - a raid's clock cost is walking, dialogue and hesitation, and a duration argued from walking alone will be wrong. A window that loses focus pauses the world scene under a plate until a click or a key (`world/windowPause.ts`, the captain's call on playtest 14; the resuming key is spent on the plate). It pauses with `this.sys.pause()`, never `this.scene.pause()`, which is queued for a frame a window at the back may never draw; and it is off in test mode, whose drivers open second tabs.
- Between-raid healing is priced in raid time, not items: `src/game/hub/recovery.ts` owns the prices and the cap, `Stash.recoverPokemon()` performs the restore, and the debt rides in the save as `pendingRecoveryMs`. It is subtracted from the raid clock when a raid starts and only cleared when that raid resolves (`bankRun`/`bankFirstContractRun`/`applyWipeLoss`), and then only as much as was owed when it deployed (`RaidInFlight.owed`), so abandoning a shortened raid cannot shed the bill and neither can a heal booked in another tab while it was out. A raid out again before the first hunter could come (`DAY_CARE_LEAST_RAID_MS`, `resolvedBy` in `SaveManager.ts`) does not settle it, nor the ward's bed or Bill's ration: a 9-second dash used to make every heal free. And a debt already at `MAX_PENDING_RECOVERY_MS` when somebody walks up holds back every treatment that would cost clock (`isRecoveryHeldBack`; Joy says raid first) unless nobody at base can raid: the cap's freedom is the batch's reward within one visit, and across visits it was free healing for a looper whose raids never settled the debt. Every price there is a share of `RAID_DURATION_MS` rather than an absolute number of minutes, and `MAX_PENDING_RECOVERY_MS` is literally half of it, so shortening the raid can never leave a recovered party deploying into a zero clock; `recovery.test.ts` and a HubScene test pin both.
- Every way a raid can end - extraction, the timer expiring, and losing the last Pokemon - builds a report with `buildExtractionReport()` in `src/game/run/extractionReport.ts` and hands it to `ExtractionScene`. The report is passed exactly what the caller wrote to the stash rather than deriving it, so the screen can never claim a reward the save did not receive (a contract's payout is in that ledger and is also handed over as `contractPaid`, so the summary never calls it something brought out "plus" the contract, and the ledger tags its rows REWARD rather than BANKED - `ExtractionReport.ledgerPaid`/`ledgerFound`); for the same reason a lost raid's footer is told `fitToRaid` off the stash the wipe just wrote (`anyoneFitToRaid` in `hub/recovery.ts`), because a wipe restocks the kit and revives nobody; wording and grading live in that module so they are testable without Phaser. What a raid had at stake is never the ledger alone: the pack and the gear on every unprotected Pokemon are never secured, and a survived raid's catches rode home outside the slot too (`describeStakes`, `describeLoss` - the defeat line-up names a loss in the same words). Experience is the one part of the haul the ledger cannot list, so it is reported per Pokemon from `RunSnapshot.deployedExperience` - the party's experience at deploy, recorded by `RunManager` because a raid is played on the stash's own Pokemon and by the time it resolves their own numbers are the end of the raid. It counts towards `gradeHaul` (which counts a pile of money as one find, `POKEDOLLARS_TO_A_FIND`, or ₽40 alone was `loaded` and the crowd roared), and only Pokemon that came home are listed: a wipe deletes everything outside the secure slot, and reporting a level on one of those would be the cruellest way to be wrong. A relic carried out (`RELIC_ITEM_IDS`: the chart's pieces, the smugglers' chest, the fossils) is graded as boss gear is, four points, and headlines the report ("You dug up the smugglers' chest!"), because graded as one Potion the end of the treasure hunt read "You scraped out." (playtest 51, finding 2).
- A defeat opens the result screen on a short line-up sequence before its accounting: `buildDefeatSequence()` in `src/game/run/defeatSequence.ts` owns every word, and `ExtractionScene` plays it as additive `[data-beat]` states over one tableau. It is offered only for `cause: 'defeated'` - a raid lost to the clock has no line-up on the ground - and it needs `report.fallen`, which `buildExtractionReport` fills from the deployed party and everything caught or gifted on the raid (a catch fights and falls like anyone deployed) plus the `lastStand` Pokemon the losing scene passes in, because by wipe time every party member is at 0 HP and otherwise indistinguishable. Nothing in it advances by itself: each beat holds until a key or a click, and it carries its own `prompt` (`PRESS SPACE`, then the blinking `promptIndicator` the battle dialogue uses) because a beat that waits without saying so is a dead end. The player arrives pressing SPACE - it is how they read the faint off the battle screen - so `createBeatGate()` swallows a held key's repeats for good and any press inside a beat's first `holdMs`, and the prompt only appears once the beat is listening; two ordinary presses used to reach the last beat unseen. `leadInMs` is the tableau arriving and listens to nobody. All three raid endings then land on the same `showReport`, which disables its own way out for `INPUT_LOCK_MS` and waits - no ending moves the player on.
- A raid is played on the stash's own Pokemon objects and on a Bag copied from its supplies, but every write-back path reloads the vault from storage first, so nothing a raid does survives it unless it is carried across explicitly. `src/game/run/raidSettlement.ts` builds that carriage for all three endings - the condition each deployed Pokemon came home in, plus a signed supply delta of the bag carried out minus the supplies carried in - and `Stash.applyRaidCondition()`/`applyRaidSupplies()` write it. That condition carries what the raid earned as well as what it cost: experience travels as an absolute total rather than a delta so a replayed settlement cannot pay a win out twice, and `applyRaidCondition()` replays it through `Pokemon.gainExperience()` before writing the HP, so a level crossed in the field arrives with the stats and the learned move that come with it. Level is never stored beside it - it is a function of experience through `experienceForLevel` on the species' own FireRed growth curve, and `deserializePokemon` holds a save's XP inside its saved level's span (floored at the level's own total, so a save written before XP was recorded is not charged the whole curve again). A save from version 6 or earlier was written on the old shared cubic curve, so each Pokemon keeps its level and its share of the way to the next, carried onto its own curve (`LAST_CUBIC_EXPERIENCE_SAVE_VERSION`). Found loot rides home inside that delta, so an extraction banks the delta's own positive entries rather than `snapshot.foundItems` - a Potion found and then drunk left the stash no better off, and listing it as banked contradicted the line saying it was spent. A faint comes home as a faint rather than a deletion: deleting deployed Pokemon is the wipe's job, and reviving is the Pokemon Center's premium. Because a raid's damage and status are now what the stash keeps, the in-raid party screen and the lobby state condition through the one `conditionLine()` in `src/game/ui/condition.ts` - it is Phaser-free so the wording is testable, and two phrasings of the same fact would be two answers.
- A lost raid divides its supplies against the pack it went down with, and `buildWipeSettlement()` in `raidSettlement.ts` is the one place that happens: what is still in the pack was either protected (`survivingSecureItems`, capped by what is still held) or destroyed, and what is missing from it was spent. The three add back up to everything the raid held, which is what stops the result screen listing one Potion twice - the ledger used to name the whole loadout while the panel beside it named part of that loadout again as spent. It also stops the secure slot resurrecting a drunk supply, which made wiping the cheapest way to heal. `RunManager.resolveWipe()` cannot do this: it never sees the bag, so its `lostItems` counts what the raid drank as though it were still carried, and neither wipe scene uses it. Both wipe scenes read `this.bag` and nothing else - the raid's pack holds Poke Balls like every other supply, and the second ball counter that used to sit beside it is why the report had to overwrite its own ball line by hand. A survived raid reconciles the same way inside `buildExtractionReport()`: secured and at-risk read `carriedOut`, so secured, risked, banked, spent and put down add up to everything the raid held and a drunk Potion is never also 'made it back'. Put down is `RunSnapshot.droppedItems`, recorded by `RunManager.registerDroppedItem` from the pack's DROP and the battle's make-room drop, and the result screen names it apart from what was spent (playtest 43 B6: a crate dropped for a catch read as supplies spent). That is display only - what is banked is the settlement's business.
- Healing at base is two priced choices, and they must stay distinct: the Pokemon Center restores in full for raid time (`src/game/hub/recovery.ts`), while `src/game/hub/treatment.ts` spends one medicine out of the stash from the loadout step. Medicine cannot revive - that is what keeps `RECOVERY_REVIVE_MS` meaningful, and `useFieldItem` itself refuses a fainted target so the raid pack, the battle and the bench all hold it - and `DeploymentFlow.items` clamps every stack to the live stash so a Potion drunk at base can never also deploy.
- A raid's clock is charged for walking, dialogue and hesitation, but never for a fight: `WorldScene.advanceRunClock()` ticks zero milliseconds while `pendingTrainerBattle` is set, because the challenge that announces a battle is the battle's first beat rather than the world's last. Dialogue the world raises on its own - a trainer's line of sight, being caught - goes through `WorldScene.interrupt()`, which marks it `unsolicitedDialog` so a direction key advances it as well as SPACE and ENTER. A box that lands in front of you unasked has to answer the key you were already pressing; one you opened with the interact key does not. The deployment briefing is free for the same reason (`openingBriefingOpen`): the raid raises it on its first frame. A direction press that dismisses an unsolicited box is *spent* on it - `src/game/world/spentPresses.ts` reads that key as up until it is released - because the one press otherwise also walked a tile, into the hunter in the playtest.
- The in-raid overlays are corner chips: `src/game/scenes/raidHud.ts` decides what they say (pure, tested without Phaser, in the manner of `battlePresentation.ts`) and `src/game/ui/RaidHud.ts` draws them. Anything added there is drawn over the map the player is reading, so it is sized to its own text and appears only while it is true. The clock's flash-and-sting threshold is `raidClockAlertTier`, separate from the colour ramp, so a visual tier can be added without adding an interruption.
