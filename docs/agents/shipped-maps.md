# The shipped maps

Viridian Forest, the four older maps, Route 1, Pallet Town, Viridian City and Floodplain Relay - all retired from play for now, kept fit to return.

Moved out of `AGENTS.md` on 9 Nov 2026 so that file stays small; nothing here was reworded. Keep it current the way `AGENTS.md` asks: a change that adds a subsystem or makes a note here untrue updates the note in the same change, and a new note goes in the doc whose area it belongs to.

## Notes

- **Viridian Forest is 64x72 and the vast one now** (`maps/viridianForest.ts`): four times the footprint it shipped in, 1448 walkable tiles of 4608. The north-west quarter is the old drawing tile for tile - every clearing, trail, gate, landmark and measured route fact on it is where it was - and everything east of x31 and south of y35 is new wood carved out of two more lattice blocks. Seventeen new districts, and the rule they were drawn to is the captain's: *bigger has to mean more places, never more nothing*, so each one is a thing you would say describing the way you came - CINDER RIDGE carrying the rock the length of the wood to RAVEN CRAG, THE BURN (ash, snags, and why there is a fire tower), THE TARN, HORNET GLADE, THE BLOWDOWN, the CHARCOAL BURN somebody still works, THE SAWPIT, BROOK FOOT, THE MERE, THE WARREN, STONE ROW, THE HOLLOW WAY, THE QUARRY, THE LONG DRIVE, THE ROOKERY, BEECH FLAT and THE SOUTH ROAD out to Viridian City. Everything priced against map size scaled with it and the list is the thing to walk when the next map grows: exits (nine - `hunterFlee.test.ts` holds the blind window against the walk to the nearest one, and at 65 tiles that is the binding constraint on how far apart they may be), insertions (five), landmarks, loot, signs, trainers and a second boss. The raid clock did **not** grow: `RAID_DURATION_MS` is five minutes and the nearest way out of every landing is 3-24 steps, because the vastness is meant to live in what a raid did not get to see. Two traps this redraw paid for: a carve block written with `T` where it means "leave the wood alone" **deletes the lattice's trees** (`T` is ground, `' '` is keep - `mapGrid.ts`), which left the whole east wood bald until a render showed it; and `hunterFlee.test.ts` timed out at 30s until its escape and detour searches were memoised per player rather than per destination.
- **The four older maps are vast now**, each with its own entry; the Floodplain is 64x64 and the other three 64x72 or 64x76. All four are drawn in the Floodplain's hand and each file's header says what the place is for. Viridian keeps its approved trail skeleton tile for tile (it already was a network, and every authored fact still stands where it stood) and has since grown a wood round it; Route 1 keeps its shipped braid the same way; Pallet Town keeps the town it shipped as and has a valley round it. Every grid in `mapDesign.test.ts` is now a proposal, not an approval, until the captain signs it off. **A plate is read once; a place is remembered because it looks like something.** A stranger who toured the three once and drew them from memory (`tools/playtest/README.md`, 'The stranger's memory test' - `gridtour.mjs` plus an agent told to open nothing else) placed every exit and every clearing that holds an object, and none that was only a name: so every Viridian clearing holds the thing its name says (`districts.test.ts` pins object to district), DEEP STAND is cut onto the tree block a player calls deep, Pallet's sheds have dug rows and produce beside them, and a district boundary at a crossing is the *far* step - THE STOCKYARD raised on the bridge head taught the hut band that name. Two figures in the same sheet and title read as the same job: Viridian's Ivy is a bug catcher because Route 1's Wren is the warden with a gate. Viridian's earth stops at the Crossroads' arms on purpose - 'no dry way to anywhere' is that map's pinned rule, and THE RIDGE is what beating its boss buys against it: the one ground in that forest with no tall grass on it, longer in steps than the trails it runs above and a fifth of the fights (`cheapestWalk()` in `redrawnMaps.testkit.ts` counts a walk in grass steps first and steps second, which is the currency that map is priced in). The rule itself is unchanged - asked with the ridge open, nothing on the map is still reached dry from the landing. What a drawing is *for* is pinned per map in walking steps - `palletTown.test.ts`, `route1.test.ts`, `viridianForest.test.ts`, through `steps()` in `redrawnMaps.testkit.ts`, which can stand a trainer, shut a tile or refuse tall grass - because a redraw that loses "the bridge is quick, the far ford is dry" still passes every structure rule. It earned its keep at once: a two-row sluice hatch on a two-row apron walled Pallet's east ford off from its own sluice, the same bench-in-a-yard fault the Floodplain had, and only a measured route said so.
- **Route 1 is 64x72, and the shipped 32x32 braid is its top-left quarter, unchanged.** The captain asked for maps "much much much much bigger" so that a raid covers part of one and leaves you wondering what else is out there, and this is the first of them. Nothing inside the old map's sealed border changes: what changes is twenty-one tiles OF that border, all of them in column 31 or rows 30-31, which is what lets the Outpost's two roads run on south, a grass path leave the station yard east, and the Overlook's shelf carry east. `route1.test.ts` pins that as a rule rather than a claim. The map's idea is repeated rather than replaced - below the Outpost the two roads become THE DROVE and THE OLD ROAD, THE COMMON lies between them, and across it is half the distance and every tile of it grass. Twenty-one places; the new eleven are the fenced ORCHARD with its packing shed, the Cut-gated THORN DELL, THE PADDOCKS and THE STEADING that works them, THE POUND, THE HOLLOW OAK, THE WAYSIDE SHRINE, THE BROOK (three crossings, none of them alike: a plank bridge on the west road, a ford on the east one that DROVER GIL stands on, and the steading's own stepping stones), THE WATER MEADOWS, THE CHARCOAL BURN and SOUTH GATE. **The raid clock does not grow with the map** - the vastness is meant to live in what you did not get to, not in longer walks - so what has to hold instead is that every landing has an open door well inside it: the worst is the front door's at 43 steps, under a tenth of the clock, and `route1.test.ts` pins all five. Two things the expansion taught. A **turf** ground is wrong under a `tree` prop: the crown carries grass baked into its corners, so every orchard tree stood in a green square of its own until the orchard was redrawn as grass with a **fence** ring, which is also the only one-tile wall on this sheet that reads as an enclosure rather than as more wood. And **a region a raid can start in has to have room for everything that raid can be promised**: the Overlook was a twenty-tile shelf and the map's loot pool is twenty-five pieces, so `generateLoot` ran out of reachable tiles and fell back on authored ones behind the shut gate. The shelf carries east now, which is also a better reward for the one door on the map a fight opens; the rule is held in `route1.test.ts` rather than the number. **Twenty-two places, and density measured rather than asserted**: `tools/tileset/density.mts` came out at **6.0 steps on the new ground against 6.3 on the shipped quarter**, so the new ground is no thinner than the old - but only after four notices, a trough in the paddocks' dead-end pen and a well in the far corner of the steading's yard went into the three places the tool named. The corner of a yard you can only reach the long way round is exactly the ground a measure catches and an author does not: it was 23 steps from anything authored. The one stretch still thin is the **shipped** WEST ROAD at 11.9, left alone on purpose.
- **Pallet Town is 64x76, the valley the mill town stands in.** The shipped 32x44 town is the top-left of it, tile for tile, and every route fact `palletTown.test.ts` already pinned still measures the same - the three crossings, the square's two doors, the town ring Vance's gate opens. What is new is the valley round it, read as one piece of water from the top down: a spring in THE HANGER feeds the millpond, the millpond the race, the race the leat, the leat THE FLOOD, and below the ridge the tide owns everything - THE SALTINGS, THE STRAND, THE HARD, THE NESS. Twenty-one places, seven ways out, four landings, two keepers with two doors each (Vance on the millpond as before, and SALTER COBB on the headland, whose COBB GATE and COBB STEPS turn the whole south into a ring). The east side is deliberately two lobes with no new crossing of the leat: THE HIGH WOOD / THE SCARP / THE QUARRY above, THE DROVE / THE KILNS / THE OLD FIELDS below. The raid clock did **not** grow (`RAID_DURATION_MS` is five minutes): the worst landing is 49 steps from an open door and crossing the valley corner to corner is 144 steps, 7% of the clock, because the vastness is meant to live in what a raid did not get to. It was drawn down from 64x88 to 64x76 for the lobby's old drop-in banner, which clipped a taller picture; that banner is gone (the picture is fitted to the window, `ui/mapPicture.ts`), so no menu limits a map's size any more. Measured with `density.mts --old=0,0,31,43`, the valley is **110% as dense as the town it grew out of** (4.4 steps against 4.8), where Viridian's new ground came out at 60% of its old.
- **Viridian City is drawn to FireRed's own standard, on Kanto's own art** (`maps/viridianCity.ts`, 72x76, `docs/screens/viridian-city/`). It is the one map held to a drawing standard at all: `FIRERED_STANDARD_MAPS` in `mapStructure.testkit.ts` gives it no tile more than five steps from a wall, a held-direction median of 2-5 and a longest walk of 47, which are FireRed's own numbers - and measured, it lands on FireRed Viridian's (median 4, 3% one-tile lanes, 1.8 wall crumbs per 1000). Its art is `frlg-kanto.png`, cut from The Spriters Resource's FireRed map renders by `scripts/cut-frlg-kanto.mjs` (the only thing that names a source cell; `kantoSheet.test.ts` reads every piece back) and catalogued by `KANTO_TILESET`: Viridian's buildings, the grey post-and-rail, the town paving, a rock mound and the route conifer. Four things are drawn by rule because FireRed draws them that way (`MaterialTiles`): the grass is a 2x2 `weave`; the conifer stands on a `lattice` (`tileset/lattice.ts` - bodies on even rows and columns, a tile no whole tree covers is a bush, and the tip hangs as walked-under canopy on the row above, so a `T` drawn off the lattice comes out as shrubs); a fence's uprights take the `railSides` of the corner they run into and a free end is plain rail; and rock stands in `mound`s. Two rules the photographs added: a Kanto roof's top row is a wall (FireRed lets you walk behind a house, which left the player a head), and nothing authored may stand under canopy while loot and seated trainers never land there (`crowns.test.ts`, `standingGround` in `runGeneration.ts`). Density is 5.0, worst 16, and was answered with places and people - eighteen townspeople, each where their business is and each saying where something is.
- Floodplain Relay (`maps/floodplainRelay.ts`) is the vast map, played a district at a time: **128x128, and the river town that used to be the whole of it is its top-left quarter, tile for tile**. One three-tile river in a reversed S round Market Isle, cut out of lattice forest, and below the town it keeps going - one reach south, one square bend, one reach into the tide at the bottom of the map. The raid clock did not move with the size (`RAID_DURATION_MS` is still five minutes): what four times the ground buys is somewhere to have not been, not a longer walk, and the numbers that say so are in `floodplainRelay.test.ts`. Twenty-two districts, twenty ways out, ten landings, five keepers, a hundred and seven notices and people. **The new ground is drawn the way an enclosed landscape actually is** - `fields()` in the generator splits a place and splits the pieces until none is more than four across or three deep, with one gap in every boundary, so no line runs the width of a district and no two lines meet; a ruled grid is the one thing nobody ever draws, and the one part of this map that *is* ruled - THE LEVELS - is ruled on purpose and reads as it. What made 128x128 authorable at all was looking rather than imagining: `mapReport.mts` for the structure numbers, `renderMap.mts` and `minimap.mts` for the picture, and the drawing loop of "cut it, then ask what it broke". Three performance faults only a map this size exposes, all fixed: `MapSketch` walked every landmark's claims on every tile written (three seconds a build, which is a raid starting with a stutter), `stepDistances` allocated a `number[][]` per call in a loop the test suite runs a few hundred thousand times, and the flee sweep's "did this tile lengthen the walk" walked the whole map to find out - it asks the cheap question first now (a tile on no shortest path cannot lengthen one), which is exact and twenty seconds instead of three minutes. **Density is the acceptance test a vast map fails first, and it is a number**: from every walkable tile, the walk to the nearest *permanent* authored thing - a way out, a landing, a landmark, a sign, a gate, a trainer, a contract stop, never loot, which is re-seated every raid. `tools/tileset/density.mts -- floodplain-relay --old=0,0,63,63` measures the halves apart, which is the number an expanded map is judged on: the Floodplain's shipped quarter runs at a mean of 5.8 steps and the ground added to it at 7.7, which is 75% of it. The first draft of that ground measured 36.7 and looked exactly as thin as that sounds; what closed it was eighty-four more authored things, each put at the tile *measured* to be furthest from anything and then written to the ground that is actually there. Most are notices, because a notice costs a raid nothing and tells it where it is. **A place is judged from a screenshot, not from the drawing**: the density tool cannot see monotony, and THE STAITHE measured fine while looking like thirty screens of bush maze - on this sheet a hedge is a round bush, so a *built* place drawn in hedge is a hedge maze with paving in it. It is drawn in `wall` now (same collision, same lanes), and THE WITHY BEDS and THE CIDER YARD are the two that still have the fault. Photograph a new district with `tools/playtest/tour.mjs` before believing it; `docs/screens/floodplain-vast/` is all thirteen of them, with that wharf before and after. The river is never wider than three tiles on purpose - the far bank is always on screen, so water is the wall, the landmark and the sightline that shows the place you cannot reach yet (the keep's tower stands across the north reach from the front door). Three bosses hold **two gates each**: the door in front of the player, and a second that opens onto ground they already know, so every way back is shorter than the way in and the sluice keeper's second door - the drowned Relay Causeway, deep water shut and the quay's own stone open - is the map turning out to be a ring. Every district holds its own exit and the east-bank ones a drop-in. `standingBoard.ts` only offers a door whose keeper can be walked up to today, because with two doors per boss "adds ground from the front door" is true of a boss behind another boss. The design grid pinned in `mapDesign.test.ts` is labelled as proposed, not approved, until the captain signs it off.
- Two lessons from drawing it, both paid for in hours. **Two staircase routes between the same corners are the same length**, so a way round only becomes the long way if the map makes it double back - the Reedbeds' flooded cut, whose one gap is at its west end and off the footpath's line, is what makes the reeds 83 steps to the road's 79; nudging tiles on either route changes nothing. And **a trainer watching a one-tile narrows stands below its mouth, off the route**, with the road running across in front of them: in the narrows they are a locked door, and the way round must rejoin without crossing their line. And **an open exit is a wall to anyone who is not leaving**: it takes whoever steps on it with no prompt, so an exit in a one-tile lane shuts the lane the moment it opens - the Radio Exit stood in the only gap round Maya, and working the ranger station (beside the kit) turned the detour into an extraction. An exit is seated in a pocket off the lane, and `floodplainRelay.test.ts` walks the way round her with every other exit tile blocked. When a route length or a caption surprises you, print the path (or read the rule) before editing the map - guessing the cause was wrong three times running.
- A drop-in point is just another `RUN_INSERTIONS` entry on the same map: the first entry for a map is its front door (`frontDoorFor`), every other is unlocked only by standing on it. `WorldScene.tryReachDropInAt()` records it on the step through `SaveManager.recordReachedInsertion()`, and `availableInsertionIds()` - contract unlocks plus `raidProgress.reachedInsertions` - is the one list the lobby offers. The lobby's insertion rows only spell out the chosen entry's description, because this list grows for as long as the player explores.
- Floodplain Relay is the starting area while it is in play: `DEFAULT_RAID_PROGRESS` unlocks only it, `FIRST_CONTRACT` lives on it, and extracting that contract unlocks the other three insertions. While it is active `HubScene` offers only insertions on its map, and `generateRunPlan` refuses to attach any contract to a raid starting elsewhere - it takes the contract itself and has no default, because `undefined` for "no contract" silently taking a default would attach one anyway. The authored teaching fight repeats while that contract is open, but its three teaching lines are owed once per save: `SaveManager.claimBattleLesson()` records `raidProgress.battleLessonGiven` at the moment the fight is handed over, so a raid lost inside it does not buy the lecture again. The lecture teaches FIGHT up front; ITEM is owed to the first-contract raid rather than to the teaching fight (which can be fled unhurt), said once a raid after the first turn of any fight that leaves the player hurt with medicine packed (`itemLessonMessage` in `battleFlow.ts`, `owesItemLesson`, `ActiveRunSession.itemLessonSaid`; playtest 47 lost to Maya with Potions unused), and `raid.mjs --said` prints every battle line.
