# Battle and Pokemon

Generation III rules, the 151, moves, abilities, evolution, doubles, gear, machines, status, experience, weather and the battle screen.

Moved out of `AGENTS.md` on 9 Nov 2026 so that file stays small; nothing here was reworded. Keep it current the way `AGENTS.md` asks: a change that adds a subsystem or makes a note here untrue updates the note in the same change, and a new note goes in the doc whose area it belongs to.

## Notes

- **A Pokemon may have a nickname, and `pokemon.name` is what it is called** - the nickname, else the species. Every screen about one Pokemon prints `name`; `base.name` is the species and is kept for where the game describes the species (the dossier shows both, an evolution says what it became). `pokemon/nickname.ts` is FireRed's rule (ten characters, `POKEMON_NAME_LENGTH`; blank or the species' own spelling is no nickname, so it still renames on evolution) plus this game's: printable ASCII less `< > & "`, which is what lets a name sit on a DOM screen unescaped. It is given once, after a catch: `BattleScene`'s `nickname` mode asks FireRed's `sText_GiveNicknameCaptured` in the battle panel (cursor on NO, B is NO) and YES opens the naming screen (`ui/NamingScreenOverlay.ts`, markup in `ui/namingScreen.ts`). A gift is asked the same once its hand-over is read (`giftNicknamePrompt`, `WorldScene.askGiftNickname`, with the world paused under the naming screen). Saved as `SavedPokemon.nickname`, optional with no version bump, and read back through the same rule.
- **A machine is a disc that teaches one move, and what it can teach whom is canon's answer rather than a designer's.** `src/game/pokemon/machines.ts` is the catalogue - a number (`TM40`, `HM06`), a `MoveBase` and a `reusable` flag - and the item is the same id with `effect: { type: 'machine' }`, so nothing names the move twice, exactly as the evolution stone names no stone. Who may read what is **generated**: `tools/moves/frlg-machines.json` is a committed PokeAPI snapshot of every machine FireRed lets each of the 151 be taught (`node tools/moves/machines.mjs [--harvest|--move=x]`, whose roster is read off `tools/species/frlg-species.json` rather than typed), `node tools/species/generate.mjs` turns it into `generated/machineLearners.ts`, and `machines.test.ts` reads the snapshot straight back: a TM this game refuses is FireRed refusing it. It was seventeen hand-written lists until the 151 import made that stale, which is the shape of bug to look for after any roster change. **Ten discs ship: six TMs and four HMs** (TM39 Rock Tomb is Brock's, handed over for his errand as FireRed's Brock hands it over for his badge - the one machine given rather than found; HM04 Strength came with the map maker's boulders and moves them for one raid, never for good), and the two HMs added before it are also *doors* (see the gate entry above), which is why reusability is not decoration - a door opened by a disc that could run out would be a capability a player could lose. The first six were each a category PR #130 made writable, and the import shrank the claim they were chosen on to an exact one: FireRed teaches four of the six to nine species by levelling (Spearow's Aerial Ace, Onix's Iron Tail, Diglett's and Dugtrio's Dig, Ice Beam on the Seel, Shellder, Lapras and Articuno lines) and `machines.test.ts` lists them, while Bullet Seed and Rock Smash are in no learnset at all. Cut and Surf are in no Kanto learnset at all. No starter, no trainer's Pokemon and nothing on a shipped wild table levels into any of the eight, which is the part that matters. `items/teaching.ts` is the whole rule (Phaser-free): already-known and cannot-learn refuse and spend nothing, a free slot learns, and a full moveset **queues the move on `pendingMoves` and hands back `choose`** so the caller opens `openMoveChooser` - the same screen a level-up opens, reused rather than rebuilt, with `canDefer: false` because the disc is in your hand now. A disc leaves the pack only when a move was actually learned *and* the machine is not `reusable`; an HM therefore never runs out and is the only kind that may be bartered. `BagScene.readMachine()` is the only caller - a machine is read in the field like the evolution stone, and `useFieldItem` deliberately refuses one so the battle's ITEM command and the base treatment bench cannot. **Where they come from is the design:** five TMs are rolled field loot (TM39 is the errand's, once) at `chance: 0.25`, one per map and two on the vast one, seated where the place explains the move - so a machine is carried out past the clock and the hunter and lost with the pack on a wipe unless a secure square is spent on it, which is the only sentence this game has for "this mattered". The HMs are `once` Bill barters paid in materials and never in money (a taught move cannot be taken off a Pokemon even by a wipe, so no sum buys one) - and a wipe still takes a carried HM, so Bill deals a once-HM again, on the same terms, whenever the vault holds no copy (`barterTaken` in `hub/trader.ts`, the owner's ruling of 2026-10-11; the vault is the only place a disc is held between raids). `MINIMUM_SUPPLIES`, `restockMinimumSupplies` and the standing board can none of them reach one, and `teaching.test.ts` holds that. Screens: `docs/screens/machines/`. One trap it paid for twice: a stored move name resolved through learnsets alone drops a taught move silently - `restoreMoveset` did on every raid settlement until #147, and the save loader kept its own copy of the same lookup and deleted every TM and HM move on the next load until playtest 21. Both now ask `knowableMoves()` in `Pokemon.ts` (the line's learnsets plus its machines), so resolve a saved move name through it and never build another lookup; `save/machineMovePersistence.test.ts` round-trips a TM and each HM.
- **Gear is one slot on a Pokemon, and the only item a wipe can take with the Pokemon it is on.** `Pokemon.heldItemId` is the slot (one item, never two: `giveHeldItem` hands back what it displaced), persisted as `SavedPokemon.heldItemId` at save version 6 with an empty default so versions 1-5 keep loading. The catalogue is four `ItemCategory.Held` rows in `items/items.ts` and the rules are `pokemon/battle/heldItems.ts` - the one module the engine asks, so a fifth piece is a catalogue row plus one function. `SupplyItemId` (derived from the catalogue, not a second list) is what types Brock's prices, `ContractStack` and the standing board's payouts, so gear can never be asked for or handed out by anything that repeats. Gear comes off a boss, once per save: `RunTrainerEncounter.carries`, handed to the raid's own pack by `WorldScene.takeBossGear()` off the ids `recordDefeatedBosses` reports as new, so it has to be carried out past the clock and the hunter and a wipe on the way home loses it for good. Three things follow. The *identity* of the item is read through to the Pokemon and never copied onto `BattleCombatant`, which owns only `heldItemSpent` - the fight-scoped half, like sleep turns - because a snapshot that drifts from the live Pokemon is the class of bug `refreshCombatantAfterLevelUp` exists to undo. It travels home on `RaidCondition.heldItemId` rather than in the supply delta, because it was never in the pack. And it is named on every screen a Pokemon is listed on (`HubScene.pokemonRowBody`), on the battle plate's bottom row (`heldGearLabel` - the band under the plate is twelve pixels and a twelve-pixel line with its outline is fifteen), and on the result screen as `ReportGear`, where a piece that did not come home is named as gone. **A berry may go in the same slot** (`holdable` on its row, `HoldableItemId`; still medicine and still a `SupplyItemId`, so `HeldItemId` stays gear only): what it does held is read off its own bag effect (`heldItemEffect`), it is eaten by itself in FireRed's two moments (`berryBite`: a status berry after any move and at the end of the turn, Oran and Sitrus at the end of a turn at half HP or less), and eating it empties the Pokemon's own hand, not just the fight's, so the raid carries the empty slot home; the plate stops naming it on the line it is eaten. `tools/playtest/heldBerries.mjs` plays a Chesto Berry and TM11 end to end.
- **BALL means any ball, and the ball thrown is the player's choice.** `ballCount()`, `carriedBalls()` and `ballModifierOf()` in `src/game/pokemon/battle/battleItems.ts` are the rule: the BALL command counts every kind carried, throws the only kind directly, and opens a `balls` list (as ITEM does) when two or more are carried, so the best ball is never spent unasked. The thrown ball's own `capture-modifier` reaches `resolveCatchAttempt` - a Great Ball's 1.5 had been unreachable because the scene counted and spent `'poke-ball'` by name. A rule that means "a ball" must go through those helpers; loot tables, contract rewards, trader stock and the wipe kit name balls as authored content. Screens: `docs/screens/battle-balls/`.
- Items are usable in every battle, including the authored trainer fight that cannot be left - an unescapable fight must not also be an unequipped one. `src/game/pokemon/battle/battleItems.ts` is the rule (medicine only, no reviving, and the heal goes through `BattleState` because the active Pokemon's HP lives on the combatant rather than on the Pokemon); `BattleScene`'s ITEM command spends it and then resolves the enemy's turn, which is the price. It spends the raid's own `Bag`, passed from `WorldScene` into `BattleSceneData.bag` and handed back on return: the persisted save's bag is the free-roam inventory and has nothing to do with a raid, and reading it in `resolveRunWipe` is how a wipe report came to price supplies the raid never carried.
- **This game is generation III - FireRed/LeafGreen - and the captain's roster is Kanto's original 151** (his ruling, 2026-09-19). Every stat, learnset, move number, type matchup and evolution rule is sourced from Gen III canon and labelled in line with where it came from; nothing is invented. The chart is **seventeen types**, which is exactly generation III: Dark and Steel arrived in generation II and are live in FRLG (Magnemite and Magneton are Electric/Steel, and fifty of the 151 learn a Dark or Steel move by level - Charmander's Metal Claw at 13 and Squirtle's Bite at 18 among them), while **Fairy is generation VI and stays out**. It was written up here as fifteen, which is true of generation I only; at fifteen the player's damage was simply wrong. Do not add Fairy, and do not shrink it back. Battle data was originally ported from the public Unity original, `OllySalanson/escapeFromPalletTown` (readable through `gh-axi api`), which took it from a tutorial whose author invented his own monsters; that is why the web game adds a 1.5x same-type bonus Unity has no equivalent of, why Unity fought its encounter tables with a level-10 five-strong party rather than one level-5 starter, and why a handful of shipped numbers are on a later generation's values or on none. **The whole 151 are in the game**, and none of it is typed out: `tools/species/harvest.mjs` writes the committed PokeAPI snapshot (generation III typing through `past_types`, catch rate, growth rate, the FireRed/LeafGreen level-up learnset, every evolution chain), `node tools/species/generate.mjs` turns it and the move and ability snapshots into `src/game/pokemon/generated/`, and `species.ts` builds a `PokemonBase` from three owners and nothing else - canon, `shippedSpecies.ts`, and what the engine can actually play. **No base stat is written from memory** (the captain, 2026-09-23): `node tools/species/verifyStats.mjs` reads all 151 out of the FireRed disassembly (pret/pokefirered, pinned commit), Bulbapedia's generation II-V table (pinned revision) and PokeAPI's `past_stats`, refuses any row the three disagree on, and writes `tools/species/frlg-base-stats.json`; the generator will not run against a snapshot that disagrees with it, and `speciesImport.test.ts` pins every species' stats and types to it. Twenty of Kanto's own had a stat raised later (Dugtrio's Attack by twenty in generation VII, which recall had missed), so a modern fan-site number is the wrong one to reach for. `shippedSpecies.ts` is *every* disagreement between this game and canon and holds **learnsets only** - its type has no field for a stat - and it is **empty**: the captain ruled on 2026-10-11 that every learnset matches FireRed exactly, so the seventeen species that kept Unity-era learnsets (Bulbasaur's Super Sonic and level-7 Vine Whip, Squirtle's level-7 Water Gun, a Pidgey with nothing but Tackle) and Tackle's and Vine Whip's later-generation numbers (Tackle 40/100, Vine Whip 45) were moved to canon through the generator, and `moves.ts` only *names* those two (`TACKLE = GENERATED_MOVES.tackle`). A save's moves from the old learnsets still resolve through `RETIRED_LEARNSETS` in `shippedSpecies.ts`, read by `knowableMoves`. The moves of one level are in **FireRed's own order** (`tools/species/verifyLearnsets.mjs` reads pret's `level_up_learnsets.h` into `frlg-learnsets.json`; PokeAPI's order is alphabetical, which opened a Charmander's first fight with the cursor on Growl). What FireRed's kits did to the early game, measured: a Bulbasaur has nothing typed but Leech Seed before Vine Whip at 10 and a Squirtle nothing but a 20-power Bubble before Water Gun at 13, against a Charmander with Ember from 7, so every toll is held to `TOLL_BAND` (see the toll entry), a FireRed Pikachu (Thunder Shock from level 1) is a Water starter's lottery wherever it leads, and FireRed's Jigglypuff can hurt nothing before Pound at 9. The status moves this game gives 100 accuracy are still `moves.ts`'s, and `speciesImport.test.ts` prints that list and fails if it grows without being written down. Unity carries **no** evolution data at all - `PokemonBase.cs` has no field for it - so every evolution rule is generated from PokeAPI's chains; every stone is had (Bill barters them, Oak hands a set out at 50 and 125 kinds), the four **trade** lines (Kadabra, Machoke, Graveler, Haunter) evolve when THE TRADER sends one over and straight back (`hub/trades.ts`), and `hub/pokedexComplete.test.ts` holds that all 151 can be owned on the maps a player is offered; `docs/pokemon/roster.md` is the list of what else the import could not bring in, by species and with the reason.
- **Two Pokemon a side is a slot, and a slot is a place.** `BattleState` holds four of them - `player`/`enemy` are slot 0 and `playerPartner`/`enemyPartner` slot 1, one field per slot so nothing can drift out of step with anything else - and `unitAt`/`withUnit` in `battleEngine.ts` are the only two things that know that shape; everything else addresses a slot by `SlotRef`. Two a side and no more, because generation III's multi battle **is** the double battle. A double needs two able Pokemon on *each* side, so `createTrainerBattleState` refuses the second slot when the player has nobody for it and a `unitCount: 2` trainer fights a lone starter exactly as it always did - which is canon and also the only fair reading, since the alternative hands one Pokemon two actions a turn against its one. Every event's `slot` is optional and **absent means the lead**, and the engine omits it when it is 0, so a single battle emits character-for-character the events it emitted before slots existed: `battleEngine.test.ts` was not touched to make the refactor pass, and that is the regression guard. `resolveTurn` takes a move index (the lead's, at the lead foe) or one `PlayerMoveChoice` per slot. **The turn is ordered on four keys** - move priority, Quick Claw, Speed, then a fixed tie-break (the player's side first, lead before partner); generation III tosses a coin for a true Speed tie and this does not, because `trainerMeasure.ts`, `encounterMeasure.ts` and the whole boss ladder are numbers measured over this engine and a coin would move them. What fires **once per unit** and what fires **once per hit** is the distinction the whole thing turns on: the weather is the field's own turn, charged once for each unit after everybody has acted (`applyWeather`, four chips and never eight, and its five-turn clock spent once a turn); burn, poison, Shed Skin and Leftovers are each Pokemon's end of turn (`applyEndOfTurn`, once per unit after the weather, in the order the turn was played), so each is charged once; an ability that answers a hit is asked once per attacker, so two Pokemon hitting the same Static both answer it; and gear is per Pokemon (`heldItemSpent`), so nothing double-applies. Intimidate lands on **both** foes. `doubleBattle.test.ts` holds all of it, and `docs/screens/double-battle/` is what it looks like.
- **A move that hits more than one is `MoveTarget.BothFoes`**, which is PokeAPI's `all-opponents` read off the committed FRLG snapshot rather than chosen: five shipped moves carry it (Growl, Tail Whip, Razor Leaf, Heat Wave, Bubble) and each was checked by hand against generation III, because PokeAPI has no `past_values` for a target. **The import carries it too**: `tools/species/generate.mjs` mapped PokeAPI's `user` to `MoveTarget.Self` and let everything else fall to `Foe`, which flattened every `all-opponents` move the 151 brought in - invisible while there could only be one foe on the field. Mapped through, sixteen distinct spread moves are in play and 106 of the 151 learn one by level, and `doubleBattle.test.ts` pins that off the same snapshot the generator reads so a regeneration cannot flatten it again. It changes nothing in a single battle by construction (one foe is one foe), which the boss ladder confirms number for number. `all-other-pokemon` (Earthquake, Explosion, Magnitude, Self-Destruct) also hits your own partner and has no `MoveTarget`, so those four are left aiming at one foe rather than silently made into something the engine cannot say - quietly narrow rather than quietly wrong. Each target gets its own accuracy roll, damage roll, effectiveness and secondary roll, at `SPREAD_DAMAGE_MULTIPLIER` (generation III's half) while more than one is actually hit; the same-type bonus is a fact about the attacker and so is the same against both. A spread move **names itself once** and then reports each target in its own `spread-damage` line, because one number could only be a sum and a sum explains neither hit - and it reports only a hit that took HP, so a spread *status* move puts no "nothing happened" lines on screen. A single-target move aimed at a foe that fell earlier in the same turn is re-aimed at whoever is still standing; with nobody left it is `no-target`.
- **The one double battle is WARDEN HOLT's**, and where it is was measured (`tools/trainers/report.mts`). A double battle is two decisions a turn, and in a raid measured in the player's patience only a boss holding the last door can charge that: Maya and the route trainers are tolls paid on a route you can walk round, and Briggs is the first door in the game. Holt is behind Briggs, is the top of the ladder already and has the vault behind her fence. Three measured facts: it costs a player who brought **one** Pokemon nothing (24/12/0% for the three starters, unmoved, because the engine refuses the second slot); it is harder for the player who brought a team (73% -> 48% for Charmander 12 + Pidgey 10, which puts the last door level with Dane instead of a third easier), and a party of three barely notices (78% -> 77%), which is the lesson the door teaches; and it is **shorter, not longer** - the same three Pokemon fall in 3.8 turns instead of 8.8 and put 25 lines of log on screen instead of 33, because both of yours swing every turn. What doubles is the decisions, not the reading.
- **The battle panel's lists keep their rows still and move a cursor** (`seatRowsBesideCursor` in `BattleScene`): every row stands a "▶ " gutter to the right of where its layout says and one `▶` text moves between them, because typing the cursor into the row moved the chosen row about seven pixels every time (FireRed keeps its text still). `BattleScene.test.ts` reads a list back with `read(renderedTexts)`, which puts "▶ " / "  " in front of each row the way the screen shows it; a new list drawn through `commandTexts` gets the gutter for free. The small caption lines under and over a list are `fitCaption`ed to `CAPTION_LINE_WIDTH` - a wide word space (U+2004, because the face's own space at 12px is barely wider than its letter gap), then plain, then closed-up separators, then the move summary without its category (which a Gen 3 type implies) - measured on the game's own text; a line built on the face's own spacing ran off the battle screen in a double battle. The backdrop is drawn from its art rectangle only (`GRASS_BACKDROP_ART`: the file carries a black and white border its crop left), and a double battle draws `battleBackdrop.ts`'s redrawn copy, with the foe's platform stood under the player's pair.
- The double battle's screen is a second layout, not a generalisation of the first: `statusPlateLayout`/`combatantSpot` in `battlePresentation.ts` keep the single battle's authored numbers untouched and give the double two plate bands (148x44, three rows) with the field between them. What came off to make four plates fit is the floating WILD/FOE/RIVAL banner - nothing is set below `CHIP_FONT_SIZE`, the typing moved onto the plate itself because that is what a player reads to pick a move, and position already says which side is which. The player's compact plate carries its HP numbers on the bar's row (with a shorter bar) because the row below is holding the typing and the gear. `BattleScene` keeps one `CombatantPlate` and one sprite per slot in maps keyed by side and slot; a tween that outlived the plate it was drawing on stops rather than writing to a destroyed `Text`. Asking who to aim at is its own command mode (`target`), offered only for a single-target move with two foes standing, and the move list leads its guidance line with the chooser's name because a double battle asks twice before it resolves anything.
- **A move says what it does; the resolver reads it and knows no move's name.** `MoveBaseInit` in `src/game/pokemon/MoveBase.ts` is the tutorial's `MoveBase` shape ported (its field list, the `MoveEffects`/`SecondaryEffects` split, `MoveTarget`, `MoveFlag`) onto our own pure resolver - `priority`, `alwaysHits`, `target`, `hits`, `critStage`, `recoil`, `drain`, `healing`, `charge`, one guaranteed `effects` and any number of `secondaries`, each with its own `chance` and its own `target`. `MoveBase`'s constructor normalizes every optional, so `applyMove` never guards one. **A new status move is a data row**: the name-matching table that used to inflict four statuses by string is gone, which is also what stops a renamed move losing its effect silently. Two targets matter more than they look - a secondary with `target: Self` is how Metal Claw raises the *user's* Attack, and before it existed every boost in the engine went to the other side. Accuracy and evasion are stages with no stat behind them (`statStages.ts`), on generation III's **own `3/(3-n)` ladder**, which is not the damage table; `critStage` indexes a ladder too. `charge`/`recharge` live on `BattleCombatant.pendingMove` and survive into the next turn, so `resolveTurn` overrules the move it was passed and `BattleScene` asks `lockedMove()` before it opens the command menu. `rollHitCount` uses generation III's uneven 2-5 weighting rather than a uniform roll. `userFaints` is FireRed's `EFFECT_EXPLOSION` (Self-Destruct, Explosion), read off FireRed's effect id by `tools/moves/fireRedRules.mjs`: the user faints hit or miss and the target's Defense is halved for the blow, and `bestPlay.ts` throws one only when it has no other damaging move - they came in as plain 200/250-power hits reading "No side effect.". `MoveEffects.weather` and `MoveEffects.seed` are the fields added since - see the weather note below; `seed` is Leech Seed, FireRed's own (`Cmd_setseeded`, `ENDTURN_LEECH_SEED`): `BattleCombatant.seededBy` is the seeder's *slot*, the drain is an eighth of max HP after the gear and before poison and goes to whoever stands there, Grass types are immune, a miss or a second seed is "evaded the attack!", Liquid Ooze reverses it, and it comes off with the seeded Pokemon. `bestPlay.ts` plants it when that does not lengthen the fight. `moveEffects.test.ts` holds every category without Phaser. **`MoveFlag` is no longer decoration**: Static reads `contact` and Soundproof reads `sound`, so a physical move shipped without its flag is an ability quietly not working - which half the catalogue was - and `abilityCanon.test.ts` holds every shipped move's flags against PokeAPI's own tables (`tools/abilities/frlg-move-flags.json`; the REST API serves no flags at all). **What fraction of the 273 moves the 151 need is expressible is a number, not a guess**: `node tools/moves/coverage.mjs`, 188 of them, counted by `tools/moves/classify.mjs` - which is also what the species generator builds the catalogue with, so the count and the moves a Pokemon actually knows cannot disagree, and a move that is not expressible is left out of a learnset rather than approximated. `tools/moves/harvest.mjs` rebuilds the snapshot (see `tools/moves/README.md` for the three generation III traps it handles). Two things a playtest of a move effect has to know: a **fainted Pokemon takes no secondary**, which is canon, and a wild Pokemon on the shipped tables dies to one swing from anything that has learnt these moves - so a 10% roll only has somewhere to land on a **resisted** matchup or against a trainer.
- **An ability is a data row with hooks; the resolver reads the hooks and knows no ability's name.** `src/game/pokemon/AbilityBase.ts` is the vocabulary - the tutorial's `Ability` hook set, made pure: every hook is a function of a small context returning a number or a decision, and only the engine writes state. `abilities.ts` is the catalogue and `battle/abilityHooks.ts` is the one module the engine asks, exactly as `heldItems.ts` is for gear - so adding an ability is a catalogue row and nothing else. *Which* ability is carried is read through to `PokemonBase.abilityId` and never copied onto a `BattleCombatant`; what a fight owns is two bits on the combatant (`abilityCharged`, which only Flash Fire sets, and `abilityAnnounced`). One ability per species, because generation III gives a Pokemon one of its species' one or two slots and no save records which. **Everything an ability does arrives as a battle line** (`AbilityEffectKind` plus `abilityLine()` in `battlePresentation.ts`), because an ability is never shown in a menu and watching it is the only way to learn one; the *continuous* effects (`CONTINUOUS_ABILITY_EFFECTS` in `battleEngine.ts`, the weather's Swift Swim and Cloud Nine lines among them) speak once a battle and the rest every time. `node tools/abilities/coverage.mjs` is the number - 45 of the 55 abilities the 151 carry - and names what is out with the reason; the four that are **only** about the weather (Chlorophyll, Swift Swim, Sand Veil, Cloud Nine) read `battle/weather.ts`'s own table through their hooks rather than restating a rule of it, and `effectiveWeather()` in the engine is the single place Cloud Nine is asked, so no other rule knows it exists; `tools/abilities/README.md` has the three generation III traps a re-harvest must handle (`past_abilities` reads **forwards**, unlike a move's `past_values`) and the four abilities whose generation III meaning is not their modern one. `npx vite-node tools/abilities/measure.mts` prints what a change to abilities moved: every district's table against each starter, and every hunter rung against a party at its level plus one. It earned its keep at once - a wild Pikachu's Static took the Floodplain Vault from 55% to 46% for Charmander, under the floor `districtEncounters.test.ts` holds the starting maps to, and the answer was the table's composition rather than a quieter ability.
- Evolution is `src/game/pokemon/evolution.ts`: a **generated** rule table (`from`, `to`, and a level, a stone or a trade - `generated/evolutionRules.ts`, whose header says what the filtering throws away), and four pure questions asked of it. A trade can never be triggered, so those four lines are unreachable and `evolution.test.ts` pins that rather than letting it be rediscovered; the rules are kept because the *family* is what a save's move lookup and `pokemonCargo.ts`'s stage count read. **Every stone rule is live**: all sixteen of them name one of the five shipped stones, which since the prize work is fourteen more than the two the Thunder Stone alone reached, across thirteen species - so `evolution.test.ts` holds that each of the five has a line and a Pokemon on a shipped table to read it, rather than holding a list of dead ends. A level evolution happens inside `Pokemon.gainExperience`, after the level's moves are learned as the species that reached it and before they are learned again as the species it became (FireRed's order: evolving first lost a Charmander's level-19 Smokescreen to a Charmeleon), and rides home in `ExperienceResult.evolutions` - except in a battle, which passes `gainExperience(xp, { evolve: false })` and asks: "What? X is evolving!" plays for `EVOLUTION_MS` with ESC/Backspace (this game's B) able to stop it in FireRed's words (`BattleScene.beginEvolution`/`stopEvolving`, `evolveByLevel`), and a stopped Pokemon is due again at every later level-up (`levelEvolutionDue` reads `>=`), never on a fight it did not level in. That is the only way to keep a partner small enough for the secure container, so a settlement's replay must never re-derive it: `applyRaidCondition` replays with `evolve: false` whenever the condition carries a `speciesId`; a stone evolution is an `evolution-stone` item effect resolved by `useFieldItem`, so the in-raid Bag is already the place it is spent and no menu knows about it. `Pokemon.base` is therefore mutable, written only by `evolveInto`, which refuses anything that is not further along the same line - that refusal is what makes a replayed settlement a no-op. A raid carries the species home in `RaidCondition.speciesId` as well as the experience, because a stone spends no experience for `applyRaidCondition` to replay. A save needs no new field (`SavedPokemon.speciesId` already is the species), but a save's moves are looked up across `evolutionFamily()` rather than one learnset, or an Ivysaur would lose the Super Sonic only Bulbasaur teaches. Evolution lands **above** the hunter ladder rather than inside it, and the ladder's fourth rung is the answer: see the note below.
- Every species sprite is `public/assets/pokemon/{front,back}/<dexId>.png` - all 302 of them, the FireRed/LeafGreen rip of PokeAPI's sprite repository, fetched by `node scripts/fetch-pokemon-sprites.mjs`, which checks the signature and the size of each file as it lands. `spriteAssets.test.ts` enforces the format and a size bound, because a dimensionless SVG once rasterised to 150x150 and covered the battle screen.
- **A trainer's next Pokemon arrives at the end of the turn, never mid-turn** (`sendOutReplacements` in `battleEngine.ts`, called at the end of `resolveTurn` and `resolveEnemyTurn`): the fallen Pokemon's queued action is lost with it, the slot stays empty while the rest of the turn plays (a move aimed there is re-aimed or has no target), and the newcomer is never chipped by weather it was not out for. Filling the slot at the faint gave the newcomer the fallen Pokemon's queued move - a free hit on every knockout, which every trainer, boss and hunter number was first measured with (playtest finding B1). The XP for a trainer knockout is read straight after its "fainted!" line, as FireRed reads it. The player's bench counts the same way: `BattleState.playerParty` (set by `BattleScene` and by `trainerMeasure.ts`) means a side is beaten only when nobody on it is standing *and* nobody is left to send in (`outcomeOf`), so the field emptying mid-turn never ends the turn early - before it did, both actives falling together skipped the trainer's bench and handed the player the win (playtest 20, N1). Both last Pokemon falling together is FireRed's draw, which is a loss. And the mirror of B1: a Pokemon the player sends in because the last one fainted comes in free (`BattleScene.switchPokemon` runs no enemy turn on a forced replacement; a chosen switch still costs the turn) - running the foe's turn there gave it a free hit on every replacement (playtest 20, N2). The measuring harness always replaced for free, so the ladder was measured without that hit.
- **Three FireRed rules the engine holds by construction** (pret/pokefirered at 037335f is the source; a pinned sparse clone is quick to make and is how each was read): a Pokemon is built knowing each move once, walking the learnset in level order and dropping the oldest when full (`Pokemon.initializeMoves`, FireRed's `GiveBoxMonInitialMoveset` - the learnsets list moves twice, and taking the last four entries built 44 species with a repeated move); a type refuses a status whatever sends it (`typeRefusesStatus` in `battle/status.ts`: Fire no burn, Poison and Steel no poison, Ice no freeze - a status *move* says "It does not affect...", a rolled secondary or a contact ability says nothing); and a Pokemon with no PP left in any move Struggles (`STRUGGLE` in `moves.ts`, `STRUGGLE_MOVE_INDEX` and `mustStruggle` in the engine, "X has no moves left!" in `BattleScene`) - typeless, a quarter of the damage back as recoil that Rock Head does not waive, no PP spent. Whose stats an attack changes is PokeAPI's `meta.category` (`damage-raise` is the user's), never its `target`, which is always the foe for an attack - reading `target` sent Silver Wind's and Superpower's changes to the foe (playtest 22); the user's share is rolled even when the hit knocks the foe out, as FireRed does; and PokeAPI has no history for a move's priority, target, stat changes or flinch, so every generated row is corrected from FireRed's own move table (`tools/moves/frlgBattleMoves.mjs` writes `frlg-battle-moves.json` from pokefirered's `battle_moves.h`; `tools/moves/fireRedRules.mjs` derives the stat changes from the effect id) and `moveFireRed.test.ts` holds every generated move to it. A Pokemon that knows *no* move still stands still in the engine - a wild Ditto is a free catch, as a cocoon is - and tests use that to hold a target in place, so stand one still with `moves: []`, never with PP at zero; only the player's own that knows nothing (an Abra, whose Teleport is not in the engine) Struggles, and that is `BattleScene.struggleIfStuck`, not `mustStruggle`. A beaten legend's one throw opens on the best ball carried (`bestBallIndex`), and letting it go - ESC on the list, or KEEP THE PACK with a full pack - asks "Let X go?" on NO first (mode `let-go`; the endgame bug hunt, finding 2).
- **A shiny is one wild encounter in 500** (the captain, 2026-10-11; FireRed's is 1 in 8,192): `pokemon/shiny.ts` is the rule. It is rolled once, in `WorldScene.transitionToBattle` (the one door every wild Pokemon comes through), on the raid's own `ActiveRunSession.shinyRng` so it moves no other seeded roll; never for a trainer, the hunter or a gift. It is `Pokemon.shiny`, saved only when true (`SavedPokemon.shiny`, no version bump), and it changes nothing but looks. Its colours are FireRed's own (`pokemon/shiny/{front,back}/`, beside the ordinary rip; paths and texture keys are `pokemon/pokemonSprites.ts`) and are **not** loaded at boot - `BattleScene.preload` fetches a shiny's whole evolution line for the fight that fields one, and `spriteTexture` falls back to the ordinary colours if they never arrive. Landing in a slot plays FireRed's sparkle frame for frame (`ui/shinySparkle.ts`, read off `AnimTask_ShinySparkles`), and every menu that names a Pokemon marks one with FireRed's summary-screen star through `pixelPokemonName`/`pixelPortrait(..., shiny)` - a new list of Pokemon should use them rather than print `base.name`.
- **Status conditions are FireRed's, read off pret/pokefirered** (`battleEngine.ts`, `damage.ts`; the tutorial's numbers are gone): burn takes an eighth a turn and halves a physical hit before the +2 (Guts exempt); poison an eighth; both at least one HP and charged at the end of the turn after Shed Skin and Leftovers; paralysis quarters Speed for the turn order and stops a quarter of moves; freeze thaws one time in five, a Fire hit thaws it, and nothing freezes in harsh sunlight; sleep and confusion are counters of 2-5 spent before each move (one to four turns lost, and the Pokemon acts on the turn its counter runs out), and a confused Pokemon hits itself with a 40-power typeless hit of its own Attack on its own Defence in FireRed's integer arithmetic (`confusionSelfHitDamage`). Static, Poison Point and Flame Body fire one time in three, Effect Spore one in ten. Only a status move says its target already has a status; a damaging move's rolled side effect on one (Lick's paralysis on a paralysed foe) does not take and says nothing, as `SetMoveEffect` breaks out silently. **Catching is FireRed's `Cmd_handleballthrow`** (`catchOdds`/`shakeThreshold`/`getCatchChance` in `battleEngine.ts`): the species' own `catchRate`, the ball's bonus in tenths, the HP gone, x2 for sleep or freeze and x1.5 for the other three, then four 16-bit shake checks under a threshold built on the GBA BIOS's whole-number square root - so a test that pins a catch roll has to work the threshold out, and the ball list states the chance as RUN states its odds.
- A knockout in a trainer fight is a decision, not a cutscene: by the time the turn is read the engine has swapped the next Pokemon in and emitted `enemy-sent-out`, and `BattleScene.showNextCombatMessage()` holds that one line at the head of the queue to ask whether to switch. Either answer puts the same line back up, which is what makes the switch free - `switchPokemon` spends the enemy's turn on a swap, and there is no turn to spend on one made against a Pokemon that has not landed. The cursor starts on NO, as `trainerChallengePrompt` starts on BACK AWAY, because the prompt opens on the key that finished the previous line; it is offered once per Pokemon in the trainer's party, never during a forced replacement and never with an empty bench. It is a state of the battle panel rather than a cutscene: `cutscene.ts` moves overworld figures and is measured against the raid clock, and nothing in a battle is either.
- A fight already won asks for nobody: when the last foe and one of yours fall in the same turn (recoil, Leech Seed), the engine's `outcome` is `victory` even with a bench, because the bench counts (`outcomeOf`), and `BattleScene.prepareForcedReplacement` leaves it alone - writing `active` over it made the player send somebody in and then skipped the experience (the 9 Nov battles hunt). The experience goes to whoever faced the foe and is still standing, as FireRed's `Cmd_getexp` pays it; `BattleScene.test.ts`'s 'both sides fainting on the same turn' plays it.
- Battle text lives in `src/game/scenes/battlePresentation.ts` as pure functions, so message wording, move guidance and layout are testable without Phaser. The bottom of the battle screen is one panel: `BATTLE_PANEL` is the rectangle the dialogue, the command grid (`mainCommandLayout` - two rows always, a third *column* for a wild fight's five commands), the move and item lists and the party list (`partyRowLayout`, two abreast so six fit) are all drawn in, in the same cream `pixelWindow`, and it stops below the player's HP plate because that is the number a Potion's target is chosen by. A refusal takes over the party list's prompt line (`partyPrompt`) rather than being pinned under the list. Every combatant is named through `combatantName()` - `Wild` or `Foe` before the other side's Pokemon by `foeWordFor`, as FireRed words it, so a WILD banner is never over a "Foe RATTATA" line - and every level through `levelLabel()` - `:L6` read as `16`. The enemy banner's role is `enemyBannerRole()`: RIVAL is the hunter's word and an authored trainer fights as FOE. A turn is resolved whole before its first line is read, so a plate's status tag is moved line by line (`statusBeforeLines` rewinds it when the lines are queued, `statusAfterLine` steps it as each is shown) and synced to the state only once the last line is read - set from the state, it showed CNF under "used SUPERSONIC!".
- A `BattleCombatant` in `src/game/pokemon/battle/battleEngine.ts` is a snapshot of its Pokemon taken when it was sent out, which is what lets a battle own PP, status, its counters and stat stages without writing them to the party. Level and stats are read through `combatant.pokemon` and so are already live; the move *list* and the HP a raised maximum grants are not, and both arrive with a level-up. `refreshCombatantAfterLevelUp` is the reconciliation - PP carried across by move identity rather than position, a fainted combatant left fainted - and `BattleScene.applyMidBattleLevelUp` is its only caller, from `awardVictoryExperience`, where it also rewrites the level plate the scene now holds as `playerLevelText`. Only a trainer battle with a second Pokemon keeps a fight alive across a knockout, which is why a plate frozen at the old level and a move menu that would not offer the move it had just announced went unseen. A party member that levels on the bench needs none of it: `replacePlayerPokemon` reads it live when it is sent out. Relatedly, `Pokemon.gainExperience` grants the new maximum's HP but never to a fainted Pokemon - a level is not a revive.
- **A Pokemon stands on the field by its own picture, as FireRed stands it** (`seatedSpriteY` in `battlePresentation.ts`): a 64x64 battle picture drawn at one centre stood a Bulbasaur on the far rim of its platform, so each species is moved down by FireRed's own empty rows under it and a foe that flies or floats is lifted by its elevation (`pokemon/generated/battlePicCoords.ts`, generated from pret by `scripts/fetch-frlg-pic-coords.mjs`). A spot is a `ground` line - the player's back picture on the top of the panel, the foe's feet on its platform just above the banner over the player's plate - and anything that re-seats a sprite asks `seatedY`, never `spot.y`.
- **A fight's entrance slides run on the scene's clock** (`BattleScene.slideOntoSpot`, advanced from `update`), never on a tween: Phaser's tween manager keeps the wall clock, and under a stepped or slow frame the foe was left part-way in, half off the field's right edge.
- **A battle slot keeps one sprite for the whole fight**, and every Pokemon sent into it is drawn on that sprite, so nothing a tween started for the last occupant may outlive it. `BattleScene.standOnSpot()` stops the sprite's tweens and stands it on its spot before any change of what it shows (send-out, switch, evolution, faint); every motion is measured from `combatantSpot` rather than from wherever the sprite already is; and a lunge's blow lands on `time.delayedCall`, dropped if the target's plate has been rebuilt since, because a rebuilt plate already shows it. A player reading the knockout lines quickly put each trainer Pokemon on the sprite while the last one's faint was still fading it, each fade sank from where the last left off, and the third was fought invisible. A timing like that is tested with `BattleScene.test.ts`'s fake tween clock, which reads lines at zero elapsed time and then lets time run.
- **Experience is FireRed's** (`Cmd_getexp`): a knockout pays `floor(expYield x level / 7)` of the fallen Pokemon, shared evenly by every Pokemon that faced it and is still standing (`BattleScene.foesFacedBy`, FireRed's `gSentPokesToOpponent` - noted after every change to the field, and reset to the newcomer alone on an about-to-use switch), at least one each, x1.5 from any trainer (the hunter included); each species levels on its own growth curve (`experienceForLevel(level, growthRate)`, FireRed's `gExperienceTables`). `expYield` is FireRed's own, read out of pokefirered's species table by `tools/species/verifyExperience.mjs` into `frlg-experience.json` - PokeAPI's `base_experience` is the modern value and 148 of the 151 differ. A trainer knockout pays straight after its "fainted!" line on every kind of turn, a bag or a switch included (`awardTrainerDefeatExperience`).
- **Weather is the field both sides are standing in, and it is a place before it is a move.** `src/game/pokemon/battle/weather.ts` is the table (the tutorial's `WeatherConditionsDB` ported as rows the engine reads, so nothing in `applyMove` knows a weather's name): sandstorm and hail chip a sixteenth of maximum HP at the end of every turn sparing generation III's own types - **Rock, Ground and Steel**, which the tutorial's fifteen-wide chart could not say - while rain and harsh sunlight bend Fire and Water. `BattleState.weather` is what is over the field now and `ambientWeather` is what the *place* has, so a move covers a place's weather for `WEATHER_MOVE_TURNS` and hands it back rather than clearing it. The field is charged once a turn, after both sides have acted, at the end of `resolveTurn` **and** of `resolveEnemyTurn` - every path that resolves a turn ends in exactly one of the two - and then every Pokemon's own end of turn (`applyEndOfTurn`) - FireRed's field effects, then its battler effects. Set two ways: `MoveEffects.weather` (the gap `MoveBase` named as the next thing it wanted; Rain Dance is the only one of the four the shipped roster learns by level, on the Squirtle line at 33/37/42, and `tools/moves/coverage.mjs` now counts all four as expressible - 198 moves to 201) and `MapDistrict.weather`, read off the tile by `weatherAt()` and passed into the fight by `WorldScene.transitionToBattle` - never carried in `RaidCarriage`, because it is a fact about where you are rather than raid state. The HUD says it: a chip under the objective, above the arrival plate (`weatherChipLine` in `raidHud.ts`), because a caption now speaks only within five steps and weather has no tiles to stand near.
- **A place may bend damage and may never chip HP**, and that is measured rather than tasteful - `tools/weather/measure.mts` and the rule in `districts.ts`. The chip is a sixteenth **floored at one**, and at the levels this game is played at the floor is what binds, so weather is a flat one HP a turn to everybody - which is paid by whoever has the fewest Pokemon. Over 300 fights a cell it *helps* the player in a wild fight and at the bottom of the hunter ladder (rung 2, 94% -> 99%) and destroys the top (rung 4, four against three: 49% -> 17%, and the health kept on a win 19% -> 9%). The hunter arrives wherever the player stands, so a chipping district would silently reprice the one fight a raid cannot decline. Of the two that only bend, harsh sunlight is a 14-point *gift* on rung 4 - 63% against 49% - because the hunter's team carries no Water and the player's Fire starter does, while rain costs 10, so **rain is the only weather any place has**: THE REEDBEDS, THE FLOOD and BROOK HEAD, three of twenty districts, each one a place made of water. What rain is *worth* is measured with the lead using its own signature move (`measure.mts --lead`), because a fight with no Water move in it is a fight rain cannot touch and a resisted signature move is one `bestPlay.ts` will rightly decline to throw. That flag once had a second reason, which is gone: the shared harnesses scored power times effectiveness and could not see the same-type bonus, so a Squirtle answered a Pidgey with Tackle. Played honestly a Water lead leaves those places on 94% of its health against 82% clear, a Fire lead on 70% against 90%, and a Grass lead is untouched; Brook Head is the one place rain decides outright (Charmander 88% -> 44%) and it is the *wild* Squirtles doing it, because at level 7-9 they have Water Gun. Sandstorm and hail ship as rules with nowhere that has them - no Kanto species learns either by level in FRLG - and a desert or an ice cave may author one once the ladder has been re-measured under it. It runs through `world/trainerMeasure.ts`, the harness the authored trainers are measured with (and now carries what a win *cost* as well as how often it is won), which is harsher than the one `HUNTER_TIERS` was set in - no items unless asked for, no switching before a faint - so read its columns against each other and not against the note above. Rain is also what the Floodplain checkpoint is fought in, THE REEDBEDS being one of the three: inert while nobody has a typed move, and from level 7 it halves the Fire starter's Ember, so that road comes free for a Charmander a level after the other two.
- Nothing distinguishes the three starters before level 7 except their level-1 kit. `computePokemonStats` is `floor(base * level / 100) + 5`, so at level 5 every starter stat sits within one point of the others, and every typed move is gated to level 7 - which is why pulling signature moves earlier has been measured and rejected twice (it makes one starter dominant, or arms the wild roster with 3x hits a 17 HP starter cannot read). Balance changes here belong in movesets, type matchups or encounter composition, re-measured over the real engine.
