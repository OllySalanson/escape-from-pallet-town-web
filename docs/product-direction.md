# Product direction

## Game promise

Escape from Pallet Town is a single-player Pokémon extraction roguelike. Multiplayer is not a
current commitment: turn-based Pokémon battles leave real-time PvP and third-party interference
as unresolved design problems.

The game combines a modern, intuitive HTML/CSS meta layer with Pokémon-style overworld exploration
and turn-based battles. The original Unity project remains the baseline for battle logic and
mechanical frequencies. The web game may improve on that baseline where it supports this direction.

## Core deployment loop

1. Prepare a loadout and select what can fit in the secure slot.
2. Enter a raid to explore, battle, complete objectives, and find equipment.
3. Extract to bank carried rewards, or wipe and lose unsecured items.
4. Use the persistent stash, contracts, and improved equipment access to prepare the next raid.

The secure slot should be small and upgradable. It preserves the tension of risking gear while
giving players a limited way to protect meaningful progress.

## Risk and agency

Risk must create decisions, not unavoidable punishment. Valuable loot should make extraction an
active decision to leave. Threats need to be readable and counterable; avoid unseen ambushes and
single exposed mandatory objectives.

Raids should offer:

- stealthier, longer routes and faster, riskier alternatives;
- optional rewards worth changing a route or delaying extraction for;
- several exits that open under different conditions; and
- choices that let players assess danger and respond before committing.

## Progression and replayability

Contracts and connected story progression should provide clear long-term goals. Persistent stash
growth, better access to equipment, and occasional map events should keep raids varied and make
successful extractions feel consequential.

## Player research - 2026-09-06

An experienced extraction-game player and former Pokémon player identified the strongest moments as
an underdog win, securing valuable loot, completing a difficult task, and escaping with a persistent
reward. Extraction is therefore mandatory, and high-value finds should create a meaningful choice
between continuing and leaving.

The interview supported gear fear as desirable tension, provided a small upgradable secure container
softens total-loss pressure. It also identified unavoidable or unseen ambushes and single exposed
mandatory objectives as frustrating. Players need both cautious and fast route options, plus
counterplay.

Contracts, story-linked progression, stash growth, improved equipment access, and occasional map
events were identified as replay drivers. The interview did not resolve how turn-based Pokémon
battles would coexist with real-time PvP or third-party interference, so the game should not promise
multiplayer.
