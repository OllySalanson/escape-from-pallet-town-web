# Project agent memory

This file is the project's committed home for project-intrinsic agent knowledge: build, test, release, architecture, and sharp-edge notes that should travel with the code.

- Update this file in the same change whenever work adds a subsystem, changes where something is registered, or contradicts a note below. This is part of finishing the task, not optional cleanup.

## Project notes

- The overworld's tile collision is built by `buildCollisionData()` in `src/game/worldMap.ts`; interactive NPC and sign definitions live in `src/game/world/npcs.ts`.
- Each raid map is authored tile by tile in its own file under `src/game/world/maps/`, as character art through `MapSketch` in `src/game/world/mapGrid.ts` - `draw(x, y, rows)` puts a block of one-character-per-tile art on the map and `plant(x, y, name)` stands a named landmark on it. Edit the drawing, not a generated grid: the previous pass built maps out of one-tile polylines and `for` loops over staggered offsets and produced exactly what it was. A map may declare **stamps** - letters of its own that stand a landmark where they are drawn, anchored at a chosen cell (a tree at its trunk) - so a wood is part of the picture rather than a list of coordinates that drifts from it. A block's ground is laid before its stamps; walkable ground that a *later* block cuts under a tile a stamp `blocks` takes the landmark away and gives its tile back to `bare`. That is what lets a district be **carved out of solid forest** instead of built up from blocks that must meet: woods drawn as grass with trees on it left a walkable seam between every pair of trunk rows and along every block boundary, one of them the whole length of the map's east edge.
- A map names **materials and roles, never tile numbers**. `src/game/world/tileset/materials.ts` is the vocabulary (sixteen: grass, turf, tall-grass, earth, sand, beach, paving, stone, gravel, ford, water, hedge, tree, cliff, fence, wall) with the one character each is written as; `catalogue.ts` is the schema; `autotile.ts` turns eight neighbours into a role; `buildMapLayers` in `tiles.ts` assembles four layers (ground, overlay, detail, canopy), the collision, the tall grass, any shoreline and the planted props. Nothing outside a catalogue knows a tile index, which is what makes swapping the art data-only - and the role set is exactly the thirteen-piece block a FireRed material is drawn as, so `frlgTileset.ts` maps one to one onto it. `FRLG_TILESET` is the sheet maps are drawn on, and `FLOOD_TOWN_TILESET` (`floodTownTileset.ts`) is that ground with a *chosen* list of the CC0 sheet's buildings standing on it - the FireRed sheet has one building, and each object was looked at on this grass before it went in. All four maps draw from it. Sheets share one numbering and `Overworld.png` runs to 2439, so the FireRed sheet starts at 3000; at 2000 every Overworld tile from row 25 down drew from the wrong sheet, and `catalogue.test.ts` now holds the whole loader list apart rather than only the catalogues that exist. Tall grass is the sheet's spiky plant tile, which therefore is never a grass accent: the sheet's own tall-grass bed reads as topiary nobody would walk into. No shipped map draws from `CLASSIC_TILESET`, `OVERWORLD_TILESET` (the CC0 sheet) or `POKEMON_GROUND_TILESET` (GBA ground with its objects) any more; they are kept because `tiles.test.ts` and `worldMap.test.ts` hold the catalogue rules - shoreline, tints - against them. A prop carries its own collision and is the last word on its own tiles: a cell marked as one you may stand on is ground whatever it is laid over, which is what makes a bridge deck over a river a bridge (and means a gate under a deck must be shut by props standing on it, not by the material beneath). A cell drawn on the canopy layer may never also be a wall, and never opens what it hangs over - `buildMapLayers` enforces that, because a crown over a figure means the figure can be under it.
- **Ground is one art family or it is not ground.** `tileset.png` is GBA Pokemon art (91% exact 15-bit colour, grass at hue 152); ArMM1998's CC0 `Overworld.png` is hue 123 and 11%, so a seam between the two grasses reads as two games stitched together. Objects travel freely - `character.png` is from the CC0 pack and every figure has stood on GBA grass since the first commit. `pokemonGround.ts` is that split: classic materials, Overworld props. A catalogue is chosen per map in `worldMap.ts`, so one map can be redrawn without dragging the others through it.
- Two things the FireRed sheet decides about how a map is drawn. **Earth and paving are two tiles wide or not at all**: the sheet draws a lane's fringe on one side of a tile, so a one-tile lane has a fringe down one side and a bare cut down the other - what is one tile wide is grass or tall grass, which have no edge to get wrong. And `buildMapLayers` asks all eight neighbours, so a lane turning a corner gets the sheet's inside-corner tile (for the Floodplain's whole first life only four were asked, and every inside corner was a square notch), while a material's `joins` lists what it meets without an edge - a ford and deep water, or a ford reads as a pool with a bank down both sides. A tree's crown tiles have grass baked into their corners and are drawn over the figures, so **a crown may only hang over thicket or grass, never over a tile somebody walks (one tile is enough to stand hidden on - Viridian shipped one), and never over the one band of sky a caption needs beside the thing it names** - `tools/tileset/crowns.mts` lists the trees that break that and `plantThicket.mts` proposes what to plant where the wood is only a hedge thick; crowns.mts clears a caption's sky three tile rows deep, but a seat is placed in pixels and not tile-aligned, so a 48px caption straddles **four** rows and a crown in that fourth row blocks a seat the tool has passed - which is why Viridian's TOWER STEPS still seats itself under the player (`whyHidden.mjs` is what says so; deepening the band asks for ten trees across two maps, so it is map work, not a tool patch); a playtest found exits with no name, a road with a green square in it and a hunter standing unseen before either existed. The sheet's ledge is `bankWest`/`bank`/`bankEast`, and a bank is solid: a ledge may only stand where nobody can get on top of it, with growth or a fence along its brow, **unless it is authored as one that can be gone over** - see the ledge entry below.
- Shorelines are never placed by hand. A catalogue declares a `shore` rule and `buildMapLayers` derives every piece from the collision, so a nudged bank cannot leave a bare seam. A sheet with no shoreline art simply gets a plain edge.
- To see a sheet or a map rather than imagine it: `docs/tilesets/README.md`. `tools/tileset/contactSheet.mjs` prints any sheet zoomed, gridded and numbered (committed for both sheets under `docs/tilesets/`), `atlas.mts` draws the catalogue, `props.mts -- <catalogue>` draws every landmark with its collision hatched, and `renderMap.mts` draws the real maps through the real layer builder. `mapReport.mts -- <map> [--runs] [--clashes]` is the inner loop while drawing: the structure numbers with coordinates, every over-long lane listed once, stranded pieces, landmarks drawn over each other (a crown eats a roof one tile away) and trunks left standing on roads. Viridian Forest shipped with no forest in it because nobody could see what they had authored.
- Pallet Town, Route 1 and Viridian Forest are drawn in the Floodplain's hand in their shipped footprints - dense, not vast - and each file's header says what the place is for. Viridian keeps its approved trail skeleton tile for tile (it already was a network, and every authored fact still stands where it stood); the other two are new drawings. Every grid in `mapDesign.test.ts` is now a proposal, not an approval, until the captain signs it off. **A plate is read once; a place is remembered because it looks like something.** A stranger who toured the three once and drew them from memory (`tools/playtest/README.md`, 'The stranger's memory test' - `gridtour.mjs` plus an agent told to open nothing else) placed every exit and every clearing that holds an object, and none that was only a name: so every Viridian clearing holds the thing its name says (`districts.test.ts` pins object to district), DEEP STAND is cut onto the tree block a player calls deep, Pallet's sheds have dug rows and produce beside them, and a district boundary at a crossing is the *far* step - THE STOCKYARD raised on the bridge head taught the hut band that name. Two figures in the same sheet and title read as the same job: Viridian's Ivy is a bug catcher because Route 1's Wren is the warden with a gate. Viridian's earth stops at the Crossroads' arms on purpose - 'no dry way to anywhere' is that map's pinned rule, and THE RIDGE is what beating its boss buys against it: the one ground in that forest with no tall grass on it, longer in steps than the trails it runs above and a fifth of the fights (`cheapestWalk()` in `redrawnMaps.testkit.ts` counts a walk in grass steps first and steps second, which is the currency that map is priced in). The rule itself is unchanged - asked with the ridge open, nothing on the map is still reached dry from the landing. What a drawing is *for* is pinned per map in walking steps - `palletTown.test.ts`, `route1.test.ts`, `viridianForest.test.ts`, through `steps()` in `redrawnMaps.testkit.ts`, which can stand a trainer, shut a tile or refuse tall grass - because a redraw that loses "the bridge is quick, the far ford is dry" still passes every structure rule. It earned its keep at once: a two-row sluice hatch on a two-row apron walled Pallet's east ford off from its own sluice, the same bench-in-a-yard fault the Floodplain had, and only a measured route said so.
- A map is judged as a network of passages, not by how much ground it has: counting walkable tiles says nothing, because an open field scores well on it. `src/game/world/mapStructure.ts` computes the two numbers that do say it - longest straight walk and connected open ground - and `mapStructure.test.ts` holds every map to them, along with reachability of every exit and landmark from every insertion, a sealed map edge, a fair hunter arrival from any tile, and a flee that buys real separation without walling the player in. Change a map and re-run that suite rather than eyeballing the result. One thing that suite cannot see: it asks whether every exit and landmark can be reached with every gate *open*, and with them open anything reaches anything. A two-row bench in a two-row yard sealed the Floodplain's front door off from its own quay, route board and Ferry Dock for eleven commits and every rule passed, because the quay could still be reached through three bosses and the keep. What a fresh save can walk to is therefore pinned by name, per gated map - `floodplainRelay.test.ts`, 'a fresh save, from the front door' - and `tools/playtest/raid.mjs --exit=LABEL` leaves by a named exit, waiting for a timed one, because the driver's nearest-open-exit rule never chooses one. Two more things every map is held to that a connected map can still get wrong: an open exit takes whoever steps on it, so an exit is a pocket a player steps *into*, never a through-tile - `mapStructure.test.ts` fails one that cuts ground off, and (`walksLengthenedBy()` in `mapStructure.ts`) one that any walk between two places is shorter over than round: district doorways, gates, insertions, landmarks and contract stops, asked again with each trainer's watch shut. A place of several tiles is as near as its nearest, so an exit may stand at the side of a two-tile lane. The Floodplain's Signal Fire stood in the neck between the keep's court and the causeway, so the sluice keeper's reveal ended the raid halfway along it; it is now a nub of stone under the tower, and no exit is excused. `raid.mjs --via=x:y,..` walks through tiles that are not a destination, which is how a reveal is walked; and a landmark is worked by walking over it, so one standing on a through-lane opens its sealed exit for anyone passing - Viridian's Fire Tower and Route 1's field station both did, and both now stand in a nook beside the lane.
- **A figure is collision, so wherever one stops is a door - and a door in the neck of a pocket is a wall.** The captain fled on 2026-09-19 and the hunter settled in the gap he had come through: for `HUNTER_SEARCH_MS` it is blind (it will not engage) and solid (it will not move), so he was sealed in with nothing to do about it. The answer is a rule about *who may occupy a tile*, held by `mapStructure.test.ts` per map and per gate state (`doorsFrom()` in `hunter.ts` names them). A figure the player may **walk into** may stand on a door, because walking into them is the way through it: the hunter is caught by (`WorldScene.tryWalkIntoHunter`, which routes into the same `hunterCatchCutscene` a pursuit contact plays, whatever the search window is doing), and a trainer is fought (`askForTrainerChallenge`, and a beaten one is never rebuilt, so the tile comes back for good) - both a price, neither a wall, and what each needs is that whoever is shut in can reach them. A figure the player may **not** walk into - a sign, a townsperson keeping a beat - may not stand on a door at all, at any tile of that beat, because there is nothing to pay and nothing to wait for. That is what makes the trap impossible by construction rather than unlikely, and it is why the map-side fix (the flee that no longer seals, above) is a preference rather than the guarantee. `hunterWall.test.ts` holds the scene half: the tile is still solid, walking into it still catches, and a hunter that is merely near is still just a bump.

- Floodplain Relay (`maps/floodplainRelay.ts`) is the vast map, played a district at a time: 64x64, one three-tile river in a reversed S round Market Isle, cut out of lattice forest. The river is never wider than three tiles on purpose - the far bank is always on screen, so water is the wall, the landmark and the sightline that shows the place you cannot reach yet (the keep's tower stands across the north reach from the front door). Three bosses hold **two gates each**: the door in front of the player, and a second that opens onto ground they already know, so every way back is shorter than the way in and the sluice keeper's second door - the drowned Relay Causeway, deep water shut and the quay's own stone open - is the map turning out to be a ring. Every district holds its own exit and the east-bank ones a drop-in. `standingBoard.ts` only offers a door whose keeper can be walked up to today, because with two doors per boss "adds ground from the front door" is true of a boss behind another boss. The design grid pinned in `mapDesign.test.ts` is labelled as proposed, not approved, until the captain signs it off.
- Two lessons from drawing it, both paid for in hours. **Two staircase routes between the same corners are the same length**, so a way round only becomes the long way if the map makes it double back - the Reedbeds' flooded cut, whose one gap is at its west end and off the footpath's line, is what makes the reeds 83 steps to the road's 79; nudging tiles on either route changes nothing. And **a trainer watching a one-tile narrows stands below its mouth, off the route**, with the road running across in front of them: in the narrows they are a locked door, and the way round must rejoin without crossing their line. And **an open exit is a wall to anyone who is not leaving**: it takes whoever steps on it with no prompt, so an exit in a one-tile lane shuts the lane the moment it opens - the Radio Exit stood in the only gap round Maya, and working the ranger station (beside the kit) turned the detour into an extraction. An exit is seated in a pocket off the lane, and `floodplainRelay.test.ts` walks the way round her with every other exit tile blocked. When a route length or a caption surprises you, print the path (or read the rule) before editing the map - guessing the cause was wrong three times running.
- A caption is drawn beneath figures and a canopy above them, so writing seated under a tree crown or the gatehouse's walk-under cells would be hidden by the thing it is beside. `CaptionSurroundings.canopy` in `labelPlacement.ts` is the rule: canopy is ground a caption may not take, exactly as a person is, and the ordinary order of seats does the rest - the other row, slid along it, then beside the subject - so a shut gate whose keeper stands below it is captioned beside its arch rather than under its roof. `WorldScene.canopyInView()` hands over only the crowns in the camera's view, merged into row runs and recomputed when the view crosses a tile, because a wooded map has hundreds and this is asked every frame. The rule is strict, so on the map side keep forest trees out of the space a caption has to sit in - list what stands there with a script before removing one; a guessed tree was one that did not exist. Judge captions in the 3x `PIXEL_WINDOW`, never the 1x logic window: the small window gives the game a smaller stage, captions have less room in it, and a survey run there reported the toll bridge's caption hidden when every player sees it.
- Hedges, trees and tall grass are the same leafy art in the classic tileset, so on maps built out of them nothing distinguishes a wall from a lane. `TREE_TINT` and `TALL_GRASS_TINT` in `tileset/classicTileset.ts` are what does. A tint is declared per material on the catalogue and applied by `WorldScene.createTileLayer`, never by the scene naming tile numbers; tints multiply, so they can only darken - and a sheet with real art declares none.
- `public/assets/frlg-tiles.png` is the ground sheet, loaded as `frlgTiles`. It has the thing the 104-tile classic set has none of: transition art. Eight ground materials arrive with a complete 13-tile edge set including the four inner corners, which is what lets a path turn a corner instead of ending in a rectangular step. `src/game/world/frlgSheet.ts` is the addressing layer - constants only, no logic - and `frlgSheet.test.ts` reads every coordinate back out of the PNG, so a cut that lands on empty sheet fails there rather than in a playtest. Two things it cannot do: its trees are 3x3 with the trunk on the bottom row, and its tall grass and hedges are drawn as two-row beds, so neither is a one-tile wall. It is cut from a Pokemon FireRed/LeafGreen rip and the source sheet must never be committed - see `public/assets/ASSET_PROVENANCE.md`, which quotes the publisher's terms.
- **Every map has a boss now, and every boss two doors.** Route 1's Overlook was the proving ground and the Floodplain has three keepers; Pallet Town's Miller Vance holds the far bank of the millpond (TOWPATH GATE at its head, TOWPATH STEPS at its foot on the sluice apron - beaten, the town turns out to be a ring you can walk from the square to the South Gate without crossing the leat) and Viridian Forest's Lookout Pell holds the rock along the top of the wood (RIDGE GATE at the nub under the fire tower, RIDGE STAIR down into the stair clearing). A boss-held gate is a different map, not a flag checked at the door. `src/game/world/gates.ts` holds the authored `MapGate`s (tiles, a `bossId`, and both states as a `GateAppearance` - a material plus any props planted only in that state, never a tile number - and the gate overrides whatever the sketch drew under it), and `getWorldMap(id, defeatedBosses)` rebuilds the map from its sketch in that gate state and remembers it by which doors are open. `WORLD_MAPS` is every gate shut, which is right for a fresh save and for tests and wrong for a live raid: `WorldScene` asks through `mapFor()` and `generateRunPlan` takes `defeatedBosses`, so the player, the hunter's search, loot placement and the exit promised open at the start all read this raid's collision. `mapStructure.test.ts` runs every rule against every gate state (shut, each boss alone, all open) and adds the one a gate creates - every region a raid can start in holds a way out it can open from inside; `gates.test.ts` holds what only a gate can get wrong (a door nobody holds, a gate that seals nothing, a chain that cannot be opened in walking order from the front door).
- **A boss's party is measured over the real engine, never guessed.** `src/game/world/trainerMeasure.ts` plays a whole authored trainer fight out - best damaging move, next Pokemon in on a faint, seeded - `tools/trainers/report.mts` prints every trainer against a spread of parties while one is being chosen, and `trainerLadder.test.ts` holds the order the ladder settled on (Briggs, Wren, Vance, Pell, Dane, Holt) and keeps a new boss inside the band the others sit in. Two things about the measuring itself. The move a measured player throws is `world/bestPlay.ts`, shared with `encounterMeasure.ts`, and it pays the **same-type bonus** and reads the **weather over the field**: scored as power times effectiveness alone it tied a starter's 40-power signature move with Tackle, the tie went to the first slot, and every measured starter answered every fight with Tackle - a level-7 Charmander went from 0% against the Floodplain checkpoint to about half, and correcting it moved every number in that report. And a **rung is the mean over four reference parties**, one per starter plus the team, because against a lone Fire lead the last two doors invert on a type matchup (Ember is 3x on Holt's Butterfree and half strength on Dane's Squirtle) rather than on difficulty. The optional `potions` argument plays the **pack**, a turn a Potion as the battle screen charges it: a door is measured bare, but a *toll* on a route is paid in supplies, and `floodplainCheckpoint.test.ts` is the one fight measured at the level a fresh save meets it. Guessing put Pallet's first draft two rungs above the Floodplain's last boss on the gentlest map in the game. Two things the table says that reasoning does not: a party with two Flying types takes a Bulbasaur to nil, which is the starter lottery Scout Lee's Squirtle exists to avoid, and the *anchor* decides the fight - Pidgey at 12 is a harder wall than Pikachu at 12. `trainerMeasure.ts` plays a double out too - it fields `party[1]` beside the lead, chooses for every engaged slot through `bestPlayFor` (which scores a spread move over both foes at the halved figure) and refills each empty slot from the bench. A boss is an authored trainer with a `bossId` (`src/game/world/trainers.ts`). Beating one is written to `raidProgress.defeatedBosses` at the moment of the win, not at extraction - `WorldScene.settleBossProgress()` does it on the battle return through `SaveManager.recordDefeatedBosses()`, which touches nothing but `raidProgress` - because a gate is the map changing rather than loot being carried out. The list the scene acts on is `plan.defeatedBosses` plus the bosses among this raid's `defeatedTrainerIds`, never a read-back from storage, so the door opens in the raid that won it even where nothing can be saved. A beaten boss is dropped by `withoutDefeatedBosses()` on every later raid - which is why, unlike any other trainer, a boss may stand in the lane it guards - and what is left is the open gate, captioned OPEN.
- A drop-in point is just another `RUN_INSERTIONS` entry on the same map: the first entry for a map is its front door (`frontDoorFor`), every other is unlocked only by standing on it. `WorldScene.tryReachDropInAt()` records it on the step through `SaveManager.recordReachedInsertion()`, and `availableInsertionIds()` - contract unlocks plus `raidProgress.reachedInsertions` - is the one list the lobby offers. The lobby's insertion rows only spell out the chosen entry's description, because this list grows for as long as the player explores.
- A trainer is a price on a route only if walking the route is what starts the fight. `WorldScene.tryInteract()` needs the player to press the interact key, so a trainer standing in a one-tile lane is a locked door, not a cost. `src/game/world/trainerSight.ts` is the answer: an authored `sightRange` makes the trainer challenge anyone who steps into the straight run of tiles they are facing, `WorldScene.tryTrainerChallengeAt()` fires it after extraction and before the tall-grass roll, and `createTrainerWatch()` shades that ground and captions the trainer so the price is on screen before the player is in it. The Floodplain checkpoint and Route 1's boss are authored with a watch; the other three trainers are still spoken to. **A trainer who holds no gate is a toll, and a toll has to be payable at the level the route is walked at.** Only a boss may be a wall, because only a boss has a door behind it that stays open; every other trainer stands up again next raid, so "come back stronger" buys them nothing. RAIDER MAYA was two level-7s ten steps into the first contract, which a level-5 starter beat 0% of the time with its whole pack open - and at level 10 with nothing packed was still 10% for the Fire starter against 100% for the Water one - for a road measured at two steps and nine tall-grass steps (forty steps to anything east) - see her party's note in `trainers.ts` and `floodplainCheckpoint.test.ts`. What a toll is paid in is **supplies**: the pack, not the raid. Being caught is a beat, not a cut: it is an authored cutscene (see the note below), and `src/game/world/trainerApproach.ts` is only the geometry it walks - the ground between the trainer and one tile short of whoever they caught. The payoff is `liftBeatenWatches()`: a beaten trainer is simply not rebuilt, so on the return from the win the shading is redrawn and released tile by tile from the trainer's end, once per trainer (`ActiveRunSession.watchesLifted`, because the world is rebuilt after every later wild fight too). `mapStructure.test.ts` holds every watch to the rules that keep it fair (never over an insertion, an exit or a warp arrival) and `floodplainRelay.test.ts` holds that map's road-versus-reeds choice to route facts.
- **An authored beat is a list of actions, not a method.** `src/game/cutscene/cutscene.ts` is the machine - the action vocabulary (`wait`, `say`, `turn`, `move`, `emote`, `teleport`, `show`, `fade`, `sound`, each carrying the tutorial's own `waitForCompletion`), a `CutscenePlayer` driven by elapsed ms, and `checkCutscene`, which is what makes the three raid constraints checkable rather than remembered. `src/game/world/cutscenes.ts` is every beat there is, authored as data. `WorldScene.playCutscene()` starts one (its first instant plays on the same tick, so the turn and the sting land where the player was caught), `advanceCutscene()` gives it the frame from `update()`, and `endCutscene()` is on the per-raid reset list and is called by `transitionToBattle` - a beat overtaken by the fight it was leading to must not be left holding the frame. The three constraints, and why: **the raid clock is charged for time the player can spend and never for time the world spends on itself**, so a self-running beat is free (and bounded by `CUTSCENE_TIMED_CAP_MS`, or there would be an authored way to stop the clock) and a `say` is billed exactly as any other dialogue; **every beat is skippable or self-advancing**, so the only beat that may wait is the dialogue box, which prompts; and **a cutscene may not move the player**, because `WorldScene`'s step machinery owns their tile and extraction, warps, tall grass and the district plate are all read off it - turning them is free of all that and is all any beat has wanted. Two are authored: the trainer approach (the mark, the walk to one tile short, the line) and the hunter's catch (both figures turn to look at each other, a breath, FOUND YOU.). A mark is seated a tile above the figure it belongs to and drawn above every figure, so `markWouldCoverThePlayer()` drops it in the one geometry where it would sit on the player's own face - hidden rather than drawn over something, the same rule `labelPlacement.ts` follows. `teleport`, `show` and `fade` are the tutorial's vocabulary and are held by `cutscene.test.ts`; nothing authored uses them yet.
- **A ledge is a route only the player has.** `src/game/world/ledges.ts` authors the one-way drop: a `brow` of tiles, the `drop` direction and how many solid tiles the hop clears. Nothing about the map changes to make one - the brow is ground already walked, the bank is already the sheet's art and already solid, the landing is already walkable - so every structural rule goes on being asked of the collision alone, and the hunter, whose pursuit is a breadth-first search over that collision, simply cannot follow. `WorldScene.tryLedgeHop()` fires it where the step planner refuses a step, before the bump, with its own duration, an arc and its own sound; the hop is a whole step of the raid (clock charged, tall grass rolled, whatever it lands on worked). `ledges.test.ts` holds what an author can get wrong: the way round has to exist in every gate state and be at least four steps longer, the landing may not be an exit (an exit would end the raid on a step taken to escape), an insertion or watched ground, and pushing any direction from the landing must find nothing. Viridian Forest's EAST RISE is the shipped one, and the reasoning for it rather than Route 1's identical bank is in that file.
- Active raid scene data is defined by `ActiveRunSession` in `src/game/run/RunSession.ts` and must pass unchanged between `WorldScene` and `BattleScene`. What lives only on `WorldScene` - the hunter, beaten trainers, taken loot, worked landmarks, the return tile - is `RaidCarriage` in `src/game/run/raidCarriage.ts`: a battle shuts the world down and the world is rebuilt from what the battle hands back, so anything not in that payload is forgotten. `WorldScene.raidCarriage()` packs it once for every kind of fight (a call site names only the fight), `BattleScene` must hand back a whole one, and every key is required, so new per-raid world state is added to the interface and the compiler names each place that must carry it. A hand-written wild payload two fields short of the trainer one is how every wild fight re-announced the hunter and stood beaten trainers back up; `consecutiveRaids.test.ts` drives the real round trip through both scenes. The bag, party and field guide pause and resume the world, so they rebuild nothing.
- The raid field guide is `ObjectivesScene` in `src/game/scenes/ObjectivesScene.ts`; derive its objective text from `buildObjectiveGuide()` in `src/game/objectives/ObjectiveGuide.ts`, and have it resume only the `WorldScene` instance it paused. The guide reads the contract's own `briefing`, so a new contract writes its lines in `contracts.ts` and needs no change here. It names a map's landmark the same way - from the `WorldPoi`'s own `label` and `description` - because the guide is read on four maps and a landmark name written as a literal there sent players on the other three to a place that does not exist.
- A giver is `src/game/world/gifts.ts`: an authored NPC (the `WorldEntity` named by `giverId`) who hands over one Pokemon, once, in their own words - `WorldScene.speakForGift()` from `tryInteract()`. **The gift is made inside the raid and is at risk**: it rides in the pack as a catch does (`RunManager.registerGiftedPokemon`, into the party while there is room, else the raid's caught stash), banked on extraction and lost on a wipe or timeout, and the giver says so. It is *received* only when a raid banks it - `RunResult.gifts` is written to `raidProgress.giftsReceived` by `SaveManager.bankRun`/`bankContract` (`withGiftsReceived`) - so a lost gift is offered again, never lost for good, and the giver's state is derived (`isGiftSpoken`: banked, or already carried this raid) with nothing stored beside the list. No save version bump: the list defaults to empty. A gift must not outclass the starter choice (`starterIdentity.test.ts`): the shipped one is a level-4 Pikachu with Tackle and Growl, and `gifts.test.ts` holds a gift under every starter in stats and kit - a new gift is a row in `POKEMON_GIFTS` plus an entity at the head of a one-tile lane.
- Contracts live in `src/game/objectives/contracts.ts` - the data is the design. A contract earns its place by changing the shape of a raid, so each one carries the mechanism that does it: several `markers` (the braid survey is three stops), a `requiredExitLabel` (only the West Culvert banks the cordon ledger), or a marker's `carriedIn` (the warden's resupply spends Potions out of your own pack). Adding a contract that is only a new position on a map is adding a waypoint. They unlock in a chain via `unlockedBy`, one contract belongs to one map so choosing an insertion *is* choosing a contract (`contractForMap`), and `contracts.test.ts` holds the whole set to those rules.
- A contract's permanent reward is derived from `raidProgress.completedContracts`, never stored separately: `secureItemStackLimit()` in `contracts.ts` is the whole of the secure-slot upgrade - it takes the banked contracts *and* the built Outfitter upgrades, both required, because a defaulted second list would silently price a paid-for locker at nothing - so a save can never disagree with either list. A contract pays in items once or in capability for good, never in supplies that refill - the warden's resupply used to raise the wipe restock, which made a reward a subscription. `SaveManager.bankContract()` is the one banking path and grants each reward exactly once; `bankFirstContractRun()` is only its alias. A save written before contracts were a list is migrated by `firstContractExtracted`.
- Once the authored chain is banked the board is the standing board, `src/game/objectives/standingBoard.ts`: one generated contract per deployable map, and **nothing about it is stored** except `raidProgress.standingContractsBanked`. The board is a pure function of that count and the rest of `raidProgress` (deployable maps, beaten bosses, unbuilt Outfitter rungs), so it cannot be re-rolled by reloading and turns over only when one of its contracts is banked. Every contract comes from a template built on the three mechanisms `contracts.ts` already has (`survey` is three spread stakes, `dispatch` a `requiredExitLabel` that is never the exit nearest the case, `resupply` a `carriedIn` paid back a grade up, `sealed` a stop behind a gate still shut - the board naming the door and its boss), with tiles measured off the map in walking steps rather than authored. Escalation is `hunterPressure` on the contract, spent through `hunterThreatFor(party, pressure)` - the same ladder the loadout is priced on, clamped to it - and only the board's top contract carries the full pressure, stepping down to none so the safe raid stays on offer at safe pay. It pays in what the Outfitter consumes (`outfitterMaterialKinds()`, plus a Pokemon wherever the contract cost a fight that could not be declined). `boardContracts()`/`boardContractForMap()` are what the lobby and the deploy ask, never `availableContracts()` directly. `SaveManager.bankContract()` takes a standing contract **whole** rather than by id, because the raid that carried it can change the progress it was drawn from (the sealed contract is finished by beating the boss whose gate it was drawn behind); the round in its id is what makes it pay once. The lobby lists it in the same scrolling row-and-detail board the authored chain uses, most hunter first; what a row and its detail say is `src/game/hub/contractBoard.ts`, Phaser-free, so everything a contract costs (the door, the exit, the pack, the hunter) is on screen before the insertion is chosen. What the result screen says of a contract that was not banked is `contractReportLine()` in `src/game/objectives/contractReport.ts`, which is handed the progress the raid *left behind* and promises "stays on the board" only if the board dealt from that progress still holds the same contract - compared by content, because a redealt standing contract shares its id (round and map) with the one it replaced. A stop's position is worded as a part of the map ("the north-west of the map"), never a heading - the field guide prints the heading from the player on the line above.
- The Outfitter is the stash's one sink: `src/game/hub/outfitter.ts` holds the ladder of permanent base upgrades, paid for in Pokemon the player names and in **materials**, and it is a sink rather than a shop - no currency, nothing handed back, capability or information only. The save records only `raidProgress.outfitterUpgrades`; every effect is derived from that list by the owner that already existed (secure slots in `contracts.ts`, the bay's `RecoveryTerms` in `recovery.ts` as shares of the raid, the mast's line in `raidHud.ts` from `hunterIntelFor`, the beacon in `runGeneration.ts`), and a raid reads the same ids off `ActiveRunSession.outfitterUpgrades`. `SaveManager.buildOutfitterUpgrade()` is the one path that spends. **The Outfitter is not the shop and the Ferryman is not the base**: one builds, the other deals - see the trader entry below. Two things are never payment, and `checkPayment()` is where they are held: the partner species (nothing in a save identifies the original starter, so a caught lookalike is refused too) and the last Pokemon fit to raid. The supply half is never the raid's own stock: potions, balls and antidotes are what staying alive spends, so a ladder priced in them competed with the raid, and the wipe restock would refund any kit spent into it (`Stash.spareCount()` still guards the whole price and only bites if a rung is priced in kit again).
- **Materials** (`radio-valve`, `cable-coil`, `parts-crate`, `lamp-oil`, `mooring-rope`, `linen-roll` - `MATERIAL_IDS` in `src/game/items/items.ts`, effect type `material`, the bag's Other pocket) exist only to feed the Outfitter, and every rung's `cost.supplies` is made of them. They have no field, battle or treatment use, are never packed (`DeploymentFlow.adjustItem` refuses them and the loadout list hides them), are field loot from each map's pool in `worldMap.ts` (which are live varies per raid through `generateLoot`), and are no part of `MINIMUM_SUPPLIES`, so the wipe restock never hands one out or counts one. They must not become a faucet: their only exit is the ladder, and the standing board pays them only while a rung still wants them (`outfitterMaterialKinds`), falling back to `FINISHED_BASE_PAY` supplies once the ladder is built. **A material is found, not brought, so the secure container holds *room* for it:** `adjustSecureItem` sets aside squares for a kind the loadout never packed, and what fills them is decided by the pack at the end (`survivingSecureItems`); `validateSecureSlot` exempts them from the "was in the loadout" check; and `Stash.applyWipeLoss` adds a secured material back because its loop over brought items never met it, so its caller must hand over the slot already cut to what the pack held. A new material needs a `MATERIAL_SHARE` entry, an icon (drawn by `scripts/draw-material-icons.mjs`) and a rung that asks for it; `items.test.ts` holds that. Money shares every one of those found-only rules, which is why the rule is asked as `isFoundOnly()` (material **or** currency) everywhere it means "found in a raid rather than packed for one", while `isMaterial()` still means only "the Outfitter takes it".
- **The bag is chequered squares, and each item takes a different number of them** (the captain's own words, 2026-09-19). `ItemDefinition.footprint` is authored beside an item's name in `src/game/items/items.ts` - nothing is more than two squares on a side - and `itemGrid.ts` is the only module that knows a container has corners: `packContents(contents, size)` seats big pieces first and returns where each sits, what would not go in, and how many squares are used. **Nothing about a layout is ever stored.** Contents are still `BagContents`, one record of id to quantity, and the picture is re-derived from it every time it is asked for - so the wipe ledger, the supply delta, the result screen and every save field are untouched by the grid, and a container is re-seated round a big find rather than refusing it for having been packed in the wrong order. That is also why **the player never places anything**: manual placement would need stored coordinates and a cursor that drags, which on a 320x240 stage is slower than the list it replaced. The decision the grid exists to create is *what to carry*, and the auto-pack is what puts that decision in front of the player. `containers.ts` holds the two sizes a save starts with (`RAID_BAG_GRID` 6x3, `BASE_SECURE_GRID` 2x2); both grow where the thing that pays for them lives - `raidBagGridFor()` in `hub/outfitter.ts` off a `bagRow` rung, wired with nothing selling it yet, and `secureGrid()` in `objectives/contracts.ts`, a column per cordon ledger and per locker. `Bag` carries a `capacity` and `add()` now returns false when a thing will not fit; every caller already checked that boolean, so loot pickup, boss gear and the run's collection seam all refuse out loud without new wiring. The vault's bag has a `null` capacity on purpose: a warehouse has no size, and capping it would make banking a raid a thing that can fail. A material is two or four squares against a Potion's one, which is what makes a good raid the raid where the pack runs out before the map does - and `itemGrid.test.ts` holds the wipe restock kit to fitting the starting pack, because that promise is the one place a player has nothing left to trade. A container also holds **cargo** - see the next entry - which is a `GridCargo` rather than a catalogue row, seated before every supply and listed separately in `GridPacking`, so nothing that counts items ever meets one.
- **A Pokemon carried home takes four squares, six when it has evolved once and nine when it has evolved twice** (the captain's ruling, 2026-09-19). `src/game/pokemon/pokemonCargo.ts` is the whole rule: the stage is counted off `evolution.ts`'s own table rather than stored on a species, so importing the other 144 needs no footprint written for any of them, and the shapes are 2x2, 3x2 and 3x3 because the grid is drawn. **Your deployed party is not cargo** - six at four squares each would be 24 against an 18-square pack - so what costs squares is what you are *bringing back*: `RunSnapshot.caughtPokemon`, which gifts join, turned into pieces by `run/raidCargo.ts` and handed to `Bag.setCargo()` on every scene that rebuilds the world (re-derived, never counted up, because a battle tears the overworld down and hands the same pack back). Three catches is twelve of eighteen squares, which is the decision: catching costs the room you were keeping for Potions and loot. A pack with no room **refuses the catch before the ball is thrown** (`BattleScene.throwBall`) and the giver keeps their gift and says so (`PokemonGift.offerNoRoomLine`) - losing it afterwards would be the same fact told dishonestly, and finding out should not cost a ball. In the **secure container** those squares are the container's own, so the 2x2 every save starts with holds exactly one first-stage Pokemon and nothing else, an Ivysaur needs a column first, and a fully evolved Pokemon is three squares tall against a container that only ever grows columns - it can never be secured, which is why `secure-locker-2` now brings a column as well as a slot (two Pokemon are eight squares; `outfitter.test.ts` states that as the one rung allowed two effects).
- **The secure container fills itself, Pokemon first and by level** (the captain's addition, 2026-09-19), so nobody deploys with their best unprotected because they did not open a screen. `src/game/hub/secureAutofill.ts` is the rule and `DeploymentFlow.refillSecureSlot()` runs it on every loadout change *until the player touches the container*, after which it is theirs - a default, never a cage. What is remembered for next raid is `raidProgress.securePreference`: a **flag** for the Pokemon half, because it is a policy and the ids change every raid, and the supplies as themselves. It is optional with a default, so every accepted save version keeps loading with no version bump (`SaveManager.test.ts` pins that), and `HubScene` writes it on the way out of the lobby rather than on the way home, because a raid that goes badly chose it too. The sharp edge is stated on screen rather than worked around: the Pokemon rows carry their price in squares, and `HubScene.secureRoomNote()` says why nothing else fits and what would change it.
- **The secure container was filled at the door, and what it accepted is what it is holding.** A Pokemon takes squares by evolution stage and can evolve mid-raid, so a starter secured as a four-square first stage is a six-square second stage by the time the clock runs out - and re-measuring it at settlement threw out of `WorldScene.update`, with the raid already lost and nothing to catch it. `RunManager` records `secureFootprintsValue` when the slot is filled and `validateSecureSlot` takes those squares rather than measuring the Pokemon again, so the fit is still checked and growing is not a reason to lose protection. Reachable by anything that can evolve, which since the import is most of the roster.
- The secure slot is lists all the way down - `SecureSlot.pokemon` on the run side, `pokemonIds` on the stash side - because the second locker protects two. How much a wipe protects is read from the save inside `SaveManager.applyWipeLoss()`, never passed by a scene: `Stash.applyWipeLoss` used to cap stacks at a literal two, which destroyed the third stack the cordon ledger pays for while the result screen reported it safe.
- **Money is loot, never a score, and the Ferryman is where it goes.** `scrip` is one catalogue row (`items.ts`, effect type `currency`, the Other pocket, a bundle to a square through `stackSize`): found in every map's loot pool, carried in the pack, refused by `DeploymentFlow.adjustItem` because it does nothing in a raid, and destroyed with the pack on a wipe unless the secure container reserved squares for it. There is no wallet anywhere in a save - a player's money is `stash.itemCount('scrip')` and nothing else, which is what the captain's ruling of 2026-09-19 means by loot. `src/game/hub/trader.ts` is the whole trader and carries the four binding constraints in its header; `SaveManager.buyTraderStock/takeTraderBarter/buyTraderBerth` are the only paths that spend, and `HubScene`'s `trader` view only renders. Three parts, and they must stay three: **standing** derived from what has been banked (contracts, bosses, and scrip *turned over* - never held), which gates everything; a **shelf** of supplies only, typed `SupplyItemId` so the compiler refuses a price tag on gear, gated by standing and by a per-raid ration (`traderRationUsed`, cleared by `RAID_RESOLVED` like the ward's bed) and priced above what a raid finds, so buying supplies can never make raid scarcity decorative; and a **barter table** of gear and the evolution stone, paid in found goods and never in scrip, so the best things cannot be bought at any price. The **berth** is the heavy scrip drain and the one sentence that tells the two base screens apart: the Outfitter *builds* a secure-container column for good, the Ferryman *rents* the same column for one trip (`traderBerthPaid`, also cleared by `RAID_RESOLVED`, and read by `secureGrid()`'s third required argument). Every price is set against the faucet, which is the scrip in `worldMap.ts`'s pools - `trader.test.ts` recomputes that from the maps themselves, so a redrawn map that pays out too much fails there rather than in a playtest.
- The quarantine ward's free bed is per-raid state, not an upgrade effect, so it is stored: `wardTreatmentsUsed` rides beside `pendingRecoveryMs` and is cleared by the same `RAID_RESOLVED` on every raid ending, so a reload cannot hand it back. `wardBedIds()` gives the bed to whoever it saves the most on across everyone hurt at base, which is what makes a row's listed price the price whether it is clicked alone or in "recover all"; it never waives the revive.
- The beacon exit stands on the insertion tile itself (`beaconExit()` in `runGeneration.ts`), which is what lets one upgrade serve every map and every drop-in point without authoring a tile: the landing is reachable by construction and no trainer watch may cover it. It carries an authored `elapsed` requirement at half the *deployed* clock and spends no randomness, so a seed plays the same raid with or without it.
- Scenes are registered in `src/game/gameConfig.ts`, not `main.ts`.
- The title screen (`TitleScene.ts`) is the first thing drawn and is made of the game's own parts: its backdrop is a crop of the Floodplain Relay's real layers (`WORLD_MAPS`, same tints, `CROP` picks Market Isle) under dusk, on `pixelWindow` plates, set in `GAME_FONT`. Under the name plate it offers **CONTINUE and NEW GAME** (`ui/titleMenu.ts`, Phaser-free: the wording, the cursor rule and the layout; `SaveManager.describe()` is what it reads - none, unreadable or a game with counts). CONTINUE is dim and skipped by the cursor when there is no game to continue; a save the loader refuses is `unreadable`, never `none`, so it is not offered as empty; and **NEW GAME over any save asks first, with the cursor on KEEP MY GAME** (the key that woke the game is still down), because a save is never erased by one keypress. Space on a fresh browser still goes straight to the starter picker and on a save straight to the lobby, which is what the playtest drivers rely on. There are deliberately no save slots - `docs/screens/title-menu/README.md` says why. It is rebuilt on `RESIZE` and never scales a tile. It needs no font wait of its own - `BootScene` starts it only after `awaitGameFont()` - and that is proved rather than assumed by holding the woff2 back and checking the title's first paint (`docs/screens/title-screen/`, before/after at 3x and the 320x240 stage).

- Both starter selection surfaces (`StarterScene` and `HubScene`'s `reselect` view) share their markup through `src/game/ui/starterPicker.ts`; the reselection rule itself is `Stash.swapStarter()`, and `SaveManager.reselectStarter()` also rewrites `starterSpeciesId` so later wipe re-grants follow the new choice. A swap is a change of species and nothing else: `starterInConditionOf()` hands the newcomer the old partner's share of HP (rounded down, so trading back and forth only loses), its status and its faint, and the swap adds no supplies - a partner home at 1 HP with no Potions was once traded for a full-health one and a full kit, which priced the recovery bay at nothing. The swap screen previews through the same function, so what it promises is what the stash holds.
- Background music stays off, as the captain's standing preference (2026-09-19: sound effects welcome, music "pretty annoying") rather than a temporary switch: `SILENCE_BACKGROUND_THEMES` in `src/game/audio/AudioManager.ts` makes `startTheme()` a no-op while sound effects keep playing. Leave it and the scene `startTheme` calls alone; a theme written for later ships silent.
- Every sound effect is a named entry in `SOUND_EFFECTS` in `src/game/audio/soundEffects.ts` - square, triangle, sawtooth and noise voices as data, no audio files - and `audioManager.play(name)` is the only way one is sounded. The mixing rules live in `play()` and nowhere else: a channel says one thing at a time, anything that is not `ui` cuts `ui` (so the blip of the key that advanced a line never lands on the hit the line describes), a fanfare cuts everything, and the same effect asked for twice within `RETRIGGER_GUARD_S` sounds once. Which event gets which sound is pure and tested beside it - `battleSounds.ts` (battle events, and `BattleNote` for narration that carries a sound), `worldSounds.ts` (the edge-triggered ones: bump, tall-grass entry, hunter proximity) and `menuSounds.ts` (the DOM menus' baseline click, applied once in `MenuOverlay`). `soundEffects.test.ts` fails an effect that is too long or loud for its channel, identical to another, or played by nothing. A sound cannot be reviewed from a diff: audition it on the Test Lab's sound board (`?test-lab=1`), and check "did that play twice?" against `__audio.recentlyPlayed` on a dev build, which records only what was actually heard. Every effect is a one-shot, so none can outlive its scene; do not add a looping one. **Music ducks under a sting**: themes sound through a music bus under the master, and an effect with `ducksMusic` (every fanfare by default, plus the four one-off alerts that opt in - never a rhythmic one, which would hold the music down through a whole chase) takes it to `DUCK_LEVEL` for the effect's own length; two in a row are one quiet stretch. It works on whatever is on the bus while music stays silenced, so the day a theme is wanted it already ducks (`__audio.musicLevel` reads it; `docs/screens/audio-ducking/`).
- A wild battle must be handed everything a trainer battle is: `WorldScene`'s wild payload once dropped `hunterState` and `defeatedTrainerIds`, so the world came back with a fresh hunter - arrival announcement and all - after every wild fight. `resumeThreatsAlreadyAnnounced()` is the other half: `create()` runs on every return from battle, so a threat tier the player was already warned about is restored silently rather than re-announced.
- `SaveManager` accepts five save versions, and accepting one is a promise to honour everything in it. Versions 1 to 3 were written by the free-roam game, where the player's team was `party` and their supplies were `bag` - fields the extraction game never reads, because the vault is the team. Loading one used to say yes and hand back an empty vault, which `Stash.ensurePlayable()` filled with a fresh level-5 starter and `TitleScene` wrote back over the file, destroying a levelled team without a word. `moveFreeRoamHoldingsIntoVault()` in `SaveManager.ts` is the migration, gated on `LAST_FREE_ROAM_SAVE_VERSION` and on the vault being entirely empty - the signature of a free-roam save and of nothing else - and it moves rather than copies, so nothing is banked twice. Versions 4 and 5 are deliberately untouched: there an empty vault means a wipe, and the fresh starter is the intended answer. Every accepted version is pinned in `SaveManager.test.ts`; a new version must be added there as well as to the accept list.
- A level-up never deletes a move. `Pokemon.gainExperience` learns a move into a free slot and otherwise queues it on `Pokemon.pendingMoves` (returned as `movesToChoose`); `resolvePendingMove(move, forgetIndex | null)` is the only thing that forgets or declines, and `pendingMoves` is saved beside `moves`. The question is one reusable pixel-ui screen, `openMoveChooser()` (`ui/MoveChooserOverlay.ts`, markup and wording in `ui/moveChooser.ts`), written against a Pokemon and a move so a TM or tutor asks it too. Two askers: `BattleScene` (a `BattleNote` with `offerMove` opens it once the announcing line is read, then re-syncs the combatant's move list) and `HubScene.offerPendingMoves()` on entering base, the only place with someone to answer - Escape there is "decide later", in a fight it is "do not learn". A raid's answer travels home in `RaidCondition.moves`/`pendingMoves`, because settlement replays experience on a reloaded Pokemon and would otherwise re-derive the level-up and lose the choice; a condition with no moveset (nobody was asked) leaves the move queued for base. The chooser ignores input for `MOVE_CHOOSER_ARMING_MS` because it opens on the Space that finished the last line and a Space *release* activates the focused button. Levelling can evolve, learn and offer in one call, and the beats go in that order: XP, grew, evolved (the sprite and plate change with that line), learned (a free slot), then the chooser for a move with no room - a Charmeleon reaching 36 becomes Charizard and is offered Wing Attack; held gear is untouched by the choice. `restoreMoveset` resolves names through the whole evolution line, as the save loader does. Ivysaur, Charizard and Pidgeot-line learnsets pass four moves, so this path is live; tests that need a fifth use `Charmeleon` at 35.
- Every path back into play after a wipe must leave the player able to attempt a run: `MINIMUM_SUPPLIES` and `Stash.restockMinimumSupplies()` in `src/game/stash/Stash.ts` own that guarantee, and `Stash.ensurePlayable()` and `SaveManager.applyWipeLoss()` route through it. `Stash.swapStarter()` deliberately does not: the swap is reachable without a wipe, and a wiped player was already restocked by the wipe before the swap was on offer. It is a last resort and not an allowance: `Stash.supplyShortfall()` counts anything that does the same job towards a kit line (a Super Potion is a Potion, a Great Ball is a Poke Ball, an Antidote is neither), the kit is the same for every save, and only the shortfall is handed over - so it never removes anything, cannot be farmed, and gives nothing to a vault that can already field a raid. Supplies are meant to be scarce enough that banking them matters; do not add a faucet that refills itself.
- **Boxes are where a Pokemon is kept, not who owns it.** `Stash` keeps the flat `listPokemon()` and a box assignment beside it (`BOX_CAPACITY` 30, `listBoxes()`, `movePokemon()`, `renameBox()`, `addBox()`, `removeBox()`), every Pokemon in exactly one box and never two, so the Outfitter's payment, the swap-partner offer (`canSwapStarter`), the loadout, the secure slot and the wipe all read the whole vault and a boxed Pokemon is exactly as spendable and deployable as before. Read `listPokemon()` for "what do I own"; `listBoxPokemon()` is for a screen showing one box. A full vault grows a box in `addPokemon`, so banking never fails. The save is `SavedStash.boxes`, optional with no version bump (absent = everything in box one; unknown ids dropped, unboxed ones placed by `Stash`'s constructor), pinned in `SaveManager.test.ts`. Finding beats capacity: `src/game/hub/stashBrowser.ts` (pure) is the sort and find - as kept, species, level, fit first; a name-prefix search across every box - and `HubScene.browseBar()` leads the stash, the loadout's Pokemon list and the Outfitter's payment list with it. How the player is looking (`boxScope`, `stashSort`, `stashSearch`) is scene state and never saved. The stash's text fields (rename, find) are the one place the overlay keyboard lets a field own its keys, so their Enter is the form's submit and their Escape and arrows are read on key *up* (`wireBoxForm`); `MenuOverlay`'s cursor rests on inputs. Screens: `docs/screens/storage-boxes/`.
- **"How many?" is one widget, and its ceiling is worked out by whoever owns the limit.** `src/game/ui/countSelector.ts` is the +/- pair with a running total (markup and the key rule, Phaser-free); `HubScene.stepCount()` is the one place a change lands, for the loadout (`DeploymentFlow.packLimit`/`setItemQuantity`, the pack's squares), the secure container (`secureLimit`/`setSecureSquares`, in squares beside cargo) and the Ferryman's shelf and table (`traderStockLimit` - the smaller of the ration and the purse, `traderBarterLimit` - what the vault's goods cover; `buyTraderStock`/`takeTraderBarter` take a quantity and charge money, ration and goods for every one). The selector shows only numbers that will be accepted: the plus at the ceiling is `aria-disabled` and its help line is the reason, a held key or End clamps to it, and a request bigger than fits is met as far as it goes. Left/Right step by one, Shift or Page Up/Down by five, Home/End to the ends; Up/Down still move between rows. The Outfitter has no selector because nothing there is a quantity - a rung's price is fixed and its payment is a chosen set of Pokemon - so do not add one for symmetry. A row with a selector beside it is a `.px-pair` (the row still acts, the selector says how many). Screens: `docs/screens/count-selector/`.
- Deployment is a stepped route owned by `DeploymentFlow` in `src/game/hub/deploymentFlow.ts`: base → loadout → drop-in → final check → raid, with the secure slot a detour off any of them. `HubScene` renders the steps; only `deploy()` on a confirmed flow yields a party, so nothing may pre-select a raid party or start a run from an earlier step. A test that walks the route advances until `step === 'confirm'` rather than counting `advance()` calls (`readyToDeploy` in `HubScene.test.ts`), so adding a step does not have to be counted again in twenty tests.
- **Where to drop in is its own screen, and its centrepiece is a map that is dark where nobody has walked.** What to take and where to go are two decisions, and the second used to be a pane a third of the loadout wide with four names in it. `src/game/hub/dropIn.ts` is everything the screen says, Phaser-free: the place's own description, its **grade** (the hunter ladder's own four rungs, placed by the highest level anything still standing there can field - so beating a boss lowers a map's grade and "grade 3" means what "hunter tier 3" means on the final check), its ways out, its doors and who holds them, and the wildlife of *only the places the survey has reached*. Difficulty is never typed in and never measured live: `world/encounterMeasure.ts` gives the real win rate but is two hundred battles a level, so the screen states levels, which are facts. `src/game/world/minimap.ts` draws the picture as character art - one character a tile, in the map's own alphabet, with a palette - built every frame from `WorldMapDefinition.terrain` (materials, added for this) and the collision beside it in the gate state the save has earned, so a redrawn map cannot leave a stale image; `HubScene.paintMinimaps()` is the only part that touches a browser, and `tools/tileset/minimap.mts` draws the same picture big enough to criticise. **The dark is not blank**: unwalked ground is shaded a four-tile block at a time by what is mostly in it, water in a colour of its own, so a map keeps a coastline and a treeline without giving away a lane, a door or a clearing - a full bird's-eye view of an unexplored map would hand over the answer the vast maps are built to make you go and find. What lights it is `raidProgress.surveyed` (a bitset per map, `world/survey.ts`; a whole Floodplain is under a kilobyte) plus two derived lights that cannot go stale - every landing the player holds, which is what stops a fresh save being a black square, and every gate their beaten bosses opened, which is the payoff loop: push further, come back to base, watch the dark retreat. The survey is accumulated on `ActiveRunSession.surveyed` as the player walks (a disc, `SURVEY_RADIUS`, not the camera's view - a map uncovered a screen at a time is never dark again) and written once per raid ending, because a save is the whole game serialised and a step is 150ms. `raidProgress.raidRecord` counts raids per map, deployed where it is committed to and closed where it ends. - The hunter arrives at a fixed walking distance: `findHunterSpawnTile()` in `src/game/world/hunter.ts` takes every tile exactly `HUNTER_SPAWN_DISTANCE` steps away, so the spawn is always reachable and always the same lead time, and returns null rather than place it closer than `HUNTER_MINIMUM_SPAWN_DISTANCE`. `WorldScene.placeHunterIfDue()` retries on the next tick when it does, and `moveHunterToCurrentMap()` must run after `createMap()` on a warp or the search reads the map the player just left. - The hunter is priced by the party deployed against it: `hunterThreatFor()` in `src/game/world/hunterThreat.ts` opens it on the highest `HUNTER_TIERS` entry the strongest *unfainted* deployed Pokemon out-levels and brings its arrival forward `HUNTER_ARRIVAL_LEAD_PER_TIER_MS` a tier, `generateRunPlan()` folds that into `plan.hunter` (the one tuning `WorldScene` reads), and `hunterThreatLine()` words it for the final check. - **The hunter ladder is four rungs, and the fourth is a fourth Pokemon** (`HUNTER_TIERS` in `src/game/world/hunter.ts`, which carries the whole derivation). It was added because evolution begins at level 16 and a party that had crossed it drew the top of a three-rung ladder and could never draw anything else; it is pitched at level 15 so that **16 opens it** - the ladder answers evolution on the level that grants it. **A rung is measured over the real engine against the party that opens it** (a rung at level N is met by a party at N+1), and what the shipped rungs promise is a win costing a third to a half of the party's health: 97-98% wins at 36-57% HP left for rung 3, 90-100% at 36-63% for rung 4. Two things that reads as, both paid for in measurement: **evolving the rival's team instead overshoots at every level** (Pidgeotto/Ivysaur/Raichu beat their own opener 90-93% whether pitched at 13 or 18 - an evolved form is worth far more than the levels it costs, and Raichu alone turns a 97% rung into a 14% one), and **raising three levels is not a rung at all**, because the opener rises with them. Team size is the only lever that does not scale with the party opposite. The fourth body is **Jigglypuff because it is Normal**: the rival's three cover Flying, Grass/Poison and Electric, so a typed fourth checks one starter line and not the others - Butterfree took the Squirtle lead to 83% and 22% HP while leaving Charmander's at 100% and 60%. The enrage must sit above every rung (the old Lv 15 trio is now weaker than the top rung) and is pitched by what the old enrage did to the old top rung's opener. The **schedule** is even in *hunted* time rather than raid time, because the hunter is only on the map from 55-75s: 120/180/240 gives the first rung 45-65s of hunting and the rest 60s each, and 60s is the escape's own length - a first breakaway (40s) fits inside a rung with the search window to spare, and the second (60s) costs exactly one rung. `raidClock.test.ts` holds both relationships, so a retuned rung or a retuned flee fails there.
- Hunter pursuit is a breadth-first search in `src/game/world/hunter.ts`: `findHunterPursuitPath()` returns the whole route so `WorldScene.advanceHunterPursuit()` searches once per tick however many steps the hunter takes, and `chooseHunterPursuitStep()` is the single-step wrapper. Equal-length routes are broken deterministically towards the long axis of the chase; an unreachable player makes it close on the nearest tile it can stand on and hold.
- **A caption is an answer, not a signpost.** The map used to draw a permanent window over every exit, landmark, gate, drop-in, contract stop and route boundary in view, and on the 400x256 stage seven at once left no map to walk - the captain could not see where he was going, and no seating rule could have fixed it because every one of those captions had found a legal seat. `src/game/ui/captionReveal.ts` is *whether* a caption speaks, as `labelPlacement.ts` is where it sits: a `name` speaks only while the player is within `CAPTION_NEAR_STEPS` (5) walking steps of any tile it is about; a `warning` (the trainer watch, `CANNOT BE FLED`) always speaks, because the step it prices is decided from wherever the shading can be seen; an `exit` speaks near, and also once it is **open** and the clock has gone red (`EXIT_CALL_MS` is `RAID_CLOCK_URGENT_MS` - the same second, on purpose). Holding **L** is the look: every caption in view speaks while it is held, which is the map-editor view asked for rather than imposed, and the objective chip teaches the key (`RAID_KEY_HINTS`). Steps rather than a square reach, because four across *and* four down is eight steps and named three things at once on Route 1's apron. A `WorldLabel` is built with a `speech` (`voice` plus the tiles it is about) and answers `describe(audience)` each frame; `CaptionRequest.speaks` carries that into the seating, where a silent caption is still an obstacle and still one of its group's doors in view. Nothing that was permanent is lost: the objective chip names the contract stop and its bearing, the arrival plate the district, the field guide the exits and what opens them. `tools/playtest/whyHidden.mjs` lists the silent captions and how far off they are, `--look` holds the key, and `tour.mjs --look` / `--remaining=SECONDS` photograph the looked-at map and the last of a raid.
- A map caption is seated, not positioned: a `WorldLabel` is given the *rectangle of map it names* (`tileRect`/`figureRect`/`landingRect` in `WorldScene`) plus a preferred side, and `placeCaptions()` in `src/game/ui/labelPlacement.ts` seats every caption on the screen together each frame - authored side first, then the other row, then beside the subject, each slid along its edge to stay in view - taking the first seat clear of the view's edge, the raid HUD chips (`RaidHud.occupied`), every captioned subject, the `keepClear` list (`WorldScene.captionKeepClear()`: signs, crates, standing figures, watched ground) and the captions already seated. One keeper's doors are one sentence: gate captions carry a `group` and, when two or more of a group's doors are on screen, `resolveGroups()` silences them and seats the one `speaksFor` caption (`jointGateCaption()` in `gates.ts` - OVERLOOK GATE + STEPS) by the first door with a clear seat, because seated one each the second door lost its ground to the first and to the keeper's warning, and a door nobody can read is a wall. The raid clock owns the screen's top-right corner and the camera stops at the map's edge, so nothing captioned stands in a map's north-east corner (the Overlook Stile did). Seating order is priority, and a warning outranks a name: a caption made with `warns` (only the trainer watch, whose third line is `CANNOT BE FLED`) is seated before every other, whatever order the scene created them in (`seatingOrder()`) - a boss stands at the gate they hold, and in creation order the gate's name took the one clear seat and the warning went undrawn on two of the Floodplain's three doors. The player is the one obstacle that is conditional (`CaptionSurroundings.player`, from `WorldScene.captionPlayer()`: figure plus chevron, on whole tiles - standing and stepping-to - so a caption moves once as a step begins): a caption gives the player room while any other seat is clear and is offered its two rows again lifted out past the player after every ordinary seat, and only a `warns` caption keeps a seat *under* them rather than vanish - a name with nowhere else is hidden, because a name now speaks only once the player has walked up to the thing, which is exactly when they are in its way. One with no clear seat, or whose subject has scrolled off, is hidden rather than drawn cut or over something; a held seat is kept while it stays clear so nothing flickers. A caption missing in play is asked about, not guessed at: `tools/playtest/whyHidden.mjs <url> name:x:y ["CAPTION TEXT"]` stands the player there and prints what was wrong with every seat, what the caption names and what was seated before it. The same file's `placeDialog()` moves the world's dialogue box to the top when the bottom would cover whoever an `interrupt()` is about (the hunter arriving south of you). It is Phaser-free, so the rules are in `labelPlacement.test.ts` rather than eyeballed - extend it there, never by nudging a caption's coordinates in a scene.
- What is drawn over what on the overworld is `src/game/world/depths.ts`, and only there: terrain (the ground, overlay and detail tile layers), the watch shading, map art (`MARKER_BAND` - exits, caches, landmarks, contract stops, signs), captions (`CAPTION_BAND`), figures (`FIGURE_BAND`, y-sorted with the player), then `CANOPY_BAND` - the one band above the figures, which is what gives a wood an inside. - The raid duration is `RAID_DURATION_MS` in `src/game/run/raidClock.ts`, which also records the route measurement it was derived from; `raidClock.test.ts` holds the relationships a change must not break (every hunter tier reachable, every exit openable in time, the objective route never a sprint, the flee cost a visible share). The clock only advances inside `WorldScene.update`, so battles, the bag, the party screen and the field guide are all free - a raid's clock cost is walking, dialogue and hesitation, and a duration argued from walking alone will be wrong.
- Between-raid healing is priced in raid time, not items: `src/game/hub/recovery.ts` owns the prices and the cap, `Stash.recoverPokemon()` performs the restore, and the debt rides in the save as `pendingRecoveryMs`. It is subtracted from the raid clock when a raid starts and only cleared when that raid resolves (`bankRun`/`bankFirstContractRun`/`applyWipeLoss`), so abandoning a shortened raid cannot shed the bill. Every price there is a share of `RAID_DURATION_MS` rather than an absolute number of minutes, and `MAX_PENDING_RECOVERY_MS` is literally half of it, so shortening the raid can never leave a recovered party deploying into a zero clock; `recovery.test.ts` and a HubScene test pin both.
- Every way a raid can end - extraction, the timer expiring, and losing the last Pokemon - builds a report with `buildExtractionReport()` in `src/game/run/extractionReport.ts` and hands it to `ExtractionScene`. The report is passed exactly what the caller wrote to the stash rather than deriving it, so the screen can never claim a reward the save did not receive; wording and grading live in that module so they are testable without Phaser. Experience is the one part of the haul the ledger cannot list, so it is reported per Pokemon from `RunSnapshot.deployedExperience` - the party's experience at deploy, recorded by `RunManager` because a raid is played on the stash's own Pokemon and by the time it resolves their own numbers are the end of the raid. It counts towards `gradeHaul`, and only Pokemon that came home are listed: a wipe deletes everything outside the secure slot, and reporting a level on one of those would be the cruellest way to be wrong.
- A defeat opens the result screen on a short line-up sequence before its accounting: `buildDefeatSequence()` in `src/game/run/defeatSequence.ts` owns every word, and `ExtractionScene` plays it as additive `[data-beat]` states over one tableau. It is offered only for `cause: 'defeated'` - a raid lost to the clock has no line-up on the ground - and it needs `report.fallen`, which `buildExtractionReport` fills from the deployed party plus the `lastStand` Pokemon the losing scene passes in, because by wipe time every party member is at 0 HP and otherwise indistinguishable. Nothing in it advances by itself: each beat holds until a key or a click, and it carries its own `prompt` (`PRESS SPACE`, then the blinking `promptIndicator` the battle dialogue uses) because a beat that waits without saying so is a dead end. The player arrives pressing SPACE - it is how they read the faint off the battle screen - so `createBeatGate()` swallows a held key's repeats for good and any press inside a beat's first `holdMs`, and the prompt only appears once the beat is listening; two ordinary presses used to reach the last beat unseen. `leadInMs` is the tableau arriving and listens to nobody. All three raid endings then land on the same `showReport`, which disables its own way out for `INPUT_LOCK_MS` and waits - no ending moves the player on.
- A raid is played on the stash's own Pokemon objects and on a Bag copied from its supplies, but every write-back path reloads the vault from storage first, so nothing a raid does survives it unless it is carried across explicitly. `src/game/run/raidSettlement.ts` builds that carriage for all three endings - the condition each deployed Pokemon came home in, plus a signed supply delta of the bag carried out minus the supplies carried in - and `Stash.applyRaidCondition()`/`applyRaidSupplies()` write it. That condition carries what the raid earned as well as what it cost: experience travels as an absolute total rather than a delta so a replayed settlement cannot pay a win out twice, and `applyRaidCondition()` replays it through `Pokemon.gainExperience()` before writing the HP, so a level crossed in the field arrives with the stats and the learned move that come with it. Level is never stored beside it - it is a function of experience through `experienceForLevel`, and `deserializePokemon` floors a save's XP at that level's own total so a save written before XP was recorded is not charged the whole curve again. Found loot rides home inside that delta, so an extraction banks the delta's own positive entries rather than `snapshot.foundItems` - a Potion found and then drunk left the stash no better off, and listing it as banked contradicted the line saying it was spent. A faint comes home as a faint rather than a deletion: deleting deployed Pokemon is the wipe's job, and reviving is the recovery bay's premium. Because a raid's damage and status are now what the stash keeps, the in-raid party screen and the lobby state condition through the one `conditionLine()` in `src/game/ui/condition.ts` - it is Phaser-free so the wording is testable, and two phrasings of the same fact would be two answers.
- A lost raid divides its supplies against the pack it went down with, and `buildWipeSettlement()` in `raidSettlement.ts` is the one place that happens: what is still in the pack was either protected (`survivingSecureItems`, capped by what is still held) or destroyed, and what is missing from it was spent. The three add back up to everything the raid held, which is what stops the result screen listing one Potion twice - the ledger used to name the whole loadout while the panel beside it named part of that loadout again as spent. It also stops the secure slot resurrecting a drunk supply, which made wiping the cheapest way to heal. `RunManager.resolveWipe()` cannot do this: it never sees the bag, so its `lostItems` counts what the raid drank as though it were still carried, and neither wipe scene uses it. Both wipe scenes read `this.bag` and nothing else - the raid's pack holds Poke Balls like every other supply, and the second ball counter that used to sit beside it is why the report had to overwrite its own ball line by hand. A survived raid reconciles the same way inside `buildExtractionReport()`: secured and at-risk read `carriedOut`, so secured, risked, banked and spent add up to everything the raid held and a drunk Potion is never also 'made it back'. That is display only - what is banked is the settlement's business.
- Healing at base is two priced choices, and they must stay distinct: the recovery bay restores in full for raid time (`src/game/hub/recovery.ts`), while `src/game/hub/treatment.ts` spends one medicine out of the stash from the loadout step. Medicine cannot revive - that is what keeps `RECOVERY_REVIVE_MS` meaningful - and `DeploymentFlow.items` clamps every stack to the live stash so a Potion drunk at base can never also deploy.
- Finishing a step on an extraction tile resolves the raid before anything else is rolled into the same tick (`WorldScene.tryExtract()` returns whether it took the step). Authored exits stand in tall grass, and rolling an encounter first banked the raid and then tore down the scene the result screen was scheduled on, leaving the player in a dead world. A step is not the only way onto an open exit: one can open under a player waiting on it (a timed exit's locked line invites exactly that, and the beacon opens on the insertion tile) and a battle can hand the raid back on one, so `update()` also asks `tryExtractWhereStanding()` whenever the player is at rest with nothing on screen to read. - Fleeing is two different mechanics. The hunter's escape never fails and is priced in raid time by `RunManager.registerHunterFlee()` (schedule in `src/game/run/fleePenalty.ts`); it also disengages, via `beginHunterDisengage`/`findHunterBreakawayTile`/`tickHunterSearch` in `src/game/world/hunter.ts`, with `WorldScene` choosing the fallback tile because only it knows the map's collision. A wild escape is a roll in `src/game/pokemon/battle/escape.ts` whose odds rise per attempt, so it always terminates. Both surface their cost on the command label before the player commits - see `battlePresentation.ts`.
- The hunter's escape is a purchase, and both halves of it are derived rather than felt. The price is unchanged from PR #66 because the clock is not spent on walking (the greediest Floodplain route is ~25s of a 300s raid), so 40s is affordable and the escalation still ends a raid that flees every contact. What was wrong was the goods: `findHunterBreakawayTile` takes the player's *heading* (`WorldScene` passes `this.facing`, the only read the world has on where they are going) because a heading-blind fallback parked the hunter on the player's own route about a third of the time and lengthened that route about a quarter of the time. And `HUNTER_SEARCH_MS` is sized to the longest walk from any tile to its nearest exit at the slower measured step cost, so the window always covers the way out. `hunterFlee.test.ts` recomputes both from the maps themselves - a redrawn map that strands a corner fails there, not in a playtest. It also takes this raid's **exits** (`mustReach`), because the one thing the goods must never be is a lid: on 379 of the Floodplain's tile-and-heading pairs the fallback used to land across the player's only route to every way out, which is a jar sold for 40s of clock. `doorsFrom()` beside it reads a map as doors from where one person stands - which tiles take ground away, and which shut away every goal - in one Tarjan pass, and the ranking prefers a tile that does not seal, at the same separation, which on all four maps costs nothing at all. It is a preference and not a promise: a pocket whose one way out is a one-tile lane has no unsealing tile at any distance. See the occupancy rule below for the floor under it.
- An authored trainer fight has no exit, and that is the design rather than a fault: a trainer is the toll on a fast route, and a toll that can be declined once the bill is read is not a toll. What was missing was the warning, and `src/game/world/trainerEngagement.ts` is the one place both roads into that fight get it - the caption over a watch (`trainerWatchCaption`, drawn by `WorldScene.createTrainerWatch`) and the prompt raised when the player presses the interact key at a trainer (`trainerChallengePrompt`, drawn by `src/game/ui/ChoicePrompt.ts` and owned by `WorldScene.askForTrainerChallenge`). The prompt starts on BACK AWAY, so the key that opened the conversation can never start the fight; there is deliberately no prompt on the watch, because it would arrive after the step the watch charges for and declining it would refund the toll.
- **A machine is a disc that teaches one move, and what it can teach whom is canon's answer rather than a designer's.** `src/game/pokemon/machines.ts` is the catalogue - a number (`TM40`, `HM06`), a `MoveBase`, a `reusable` flag and the species ids FireRed/LeafGreen lets read it - and the item is the same id with `effect: { type: 'machine' }`, so nothing names the move twice, exactly as the evolution stone names no stone. `learners` is lifted out of `tools/moves/frlg-machines.json`, a committed PokeAPI snapshot beside the level-up one (`node tools/moves/machines.mjs [--harvest|--move=x]`), and `machines.test.ts` reads it back: a TM this game refuses is FireRed refusing it. The six shipped moves are each a category PR #130 made writable, and the import shrank the claim they were chosen on to an exact one: FireRed teaches four of the six to nine species by levelling (Spearow's Aerial Ace, Onix's Iron Tail, Diglett's and Dugtrio's Dig, Ice Beam on the Seel, Shellder, Lapras and Articuno lines) and `machines.test.ts` lists them, while Bullet Seed and Rock Smash are in no learnset at all. No starter, no trainer's Pokemon and nothing on a shipped wild table levels into any of the six, which is the part that matters. `items/teaching.ts` is the whole rule (Phaser-free): already-known and cannot-learn refuse and spend nothing, a free slot learns, and a full moveset **queues the move on `pendingMoves` and hands back `choose`** so the caller opens `openMoveChooser` - the same screen a level-up opens, reused rather than rebuilt, with `canDefer: false` because the disc is in your hand now. A disc leaves the pack only when a move was actually learned *and* the machine is not `reusable`; the HM therefore never runs out and is the one that may be bartered. `BagScene.readMachine()` is the only caller - a machine is read in the field like the evolution stone, and `useFieldItem` deliberately refuses one so the battle's ITEM command and the base treatment bench cannot. **Where they come from is the design:** five TMs are rolled field loot at `chance: 0.25`, one per map and two on the vast one, seated where the place explains the move - so a machine is carried out past the clock and the hunter and lost with the pack on a wipe unless a secure square is spent on it, which is the only sentence this game has for "this mattered". The HM is a `once` Ferryman barter paid in materials and never in scrip (a taught move cannot be taken off a Pokemon even by a wipe, so no sum buys one). `MINIMUM_SUPPLIES`, `restockMinimumSupplies` and the standing board can none of them reach one, and `teaching.test.ts` holds that. Screens: `docs/screens/machines/`. One trap it fixed: `Pokemon.restoreMoveset` resolved names through learnsets alone, so a taught move was dropped silently by the next load and by every raid settlement - it now resolves through `machineMovesFor` as well.
- **Gear is one slot on a Pokemon, and the only item a wipe can take with the Pokemon it is on.** `Pokemon.heldItemId` is the slot (one item, never two: `giveHeldItem` hands back what it displaced), persisted as `SavedPokemon.heldItemId` at save version 6 with an empty default so versions 1-5 keep loading. The catalogue is four `ItemCategory.Held` rows in `items/items.ts` and the rules are `pokemon/battle/heldItems.ts` - the one module the engine asks, so a fifth piece is a catalogue row plus one function. `SupplyItemId` (derived from the catalogue, not a second list) is what types the Outfitter's prices, `ContractStack` and the standing board's payouts, so gear can never be asked for or handed out by anything that repeats. Gear comes off a boss, once per save: `RunTrainerEncounter.carries`, handed to the raid's own pack by `WorldScene.takeBossGear()` off the ids `recordDefeatedBosses` reports as new, so it has to be carried out past the clock and the hunter and a wipe on the way home loses it for good. Three things follow. The *identity* of the item is read through to the Pokemon and never copied onto `BattleCombatant`, which owns only `heldItemSpent` - the fight-scoped half, like sleep turns - because a snapshot that drifts from the live Pokemon is the class of bug `refreshCombatantAfterLevelUp` exists to undo. It travels home on `RaidCondition.heldItemId` rather than in the supply delta, because it was never in the pack. And it is named on every screen a Pokemon is listed on (`HubScene.pokemonRowBody`), on the battle plate's bottom row (`heldGearLabel` - the band under the plate is twelve pixels and a twelve-pixel line with its outline is fifteen), and on the result screen as `ReportGear`, where a piece that did not come home is named as gone.
- **BALL means any ball, and the ball thrown is the player's choice.** `ballCount()`, `carriedBalls()` and `ballModifierOf()` in `src/game/pokemon/battle/battleItems.ts` are the rule: the BALL command counts every kind carried, throws the only kind directly, and opens a `balls` list (as ITEM does) when two or more are carried, so the best ball is never spent unasked. The thrown ball's own `capture-modifier` reaches `resolveCatchAttempt` - a Great Ball's 1.5 had been unreachable because the scene counted and spent `'poke-ball'` by name. A rule that means "a ball" must go through those helpers; loot tables, contract rewards, trader stock and the wipe kit name balls as authored content. Screens: `docs/screens/battle-balls/`.
- Items are usable in every battle, including the authored trainer fight that cannot be left - an unescapable fight must not also be an unequipped one. `src/game/pokemon/battle/battleItems.ts` is the rule (medicine only, no reviving, and the heal goes through `BattleState` because the active Pokemon's HP lives on the combatant rather than on the Pokemon); `BattleScene`'s ITEM command spends it and then resolves the enemy's turn, which is the price. It spends the raid's own `Bag`, passed from `WorldScene` into `BattleSceneData.bag` and handed back on return: the persisted save's bag is the free-roam inventory and has nothing to do with a raid, and reading it in `resolveRunWipe` is how a wipe report came to price supplies the raid never carried.
- Floodplain Relay is the starting area: `DEFAULT_RAID_PROGRESS` unlocks only it, `FIRST_CONTRACT` lives on it, and extracting that contract unlocks the other three insertions. While it is active `HubScene` offers only insertions on its map, and `generateRunPlan` refuses to attach any contract to a raid starting elsewhere - it takes the contract itself and has no default, because `undefined` for "no contract" silently taking a default would attach one anyway. The authored teaching fight repeats while that contract is open, but its three teaching lines are owed once per save: `SaveManager.claimBattleLesson()` records `raidProgress.battleLessonGiven` at the moment the fight is handed over, so a raid lost inside it does not buy the lecture again.
- Phaser reuses ONE instance per scene key for every `scene.start`, so any field describing the raid in progress outlives that raid. Clear per-raid state on entry - `WorldScene.resetStateFromPreviousRaid()` is that list, `BattleScene.create()` and `HubScene.applyLoadedGame()` are the equivalents - and add to it rather than guarding the read. A missed `pendingHubTransition` reset made every raid after the first unplayable; `consecutiveRaids.test.ts` drives two raids on one instance to guard it.
- `HubScene.init()` prefers the stored save over its start payload: Phaser hands a restarted scene its previous payload when `scene.start('hub')` passes none, which otherwise re-renders a pre-raid snapshot after extraction.
- A map's `loot` table is a pool, not a layout: `generateLoot()` in `runGeneration.ts` draws half to all of it per raid and re-seats every piece on a free tile, so the authored `position` is only a fallback and no two raids lay the same ground (`runGeneration.test.ts` holds that across 500 seeds per insertion). A design review once read the table and took it for what the player sees.
- Generated runs never randomise an exit out of existence, and the exit promised open from the first second is only *forced* where the map has not authored one: it used to force the first exit with no authored requirement regardless, which on three maps is the timed one, so the Mill Stair, the Route Outpost and the Forest Clearing were open at 0:00 under signs that say they open later. `generateExtractionPoints()` in `runGeneration.ts` offers every authored exit on a warp-reachable map and only varies the timing of exits with no authored `requirement`; `runGeneration.test.ts` guards the guarantee across 500 seeds per insertion.
- The game screen is fitted to the browser by `src/game/display/stage.ts` and applied by `stageScaler.ts`: whole-number zoom only, and the room left over is spent on logical pixels (up to 400x256) rather than on margin. `BASE_STAGE_*` (320x240) is the smallest screen and the composition every scene is authored against - never lay anything out assuming more room than that. `WorldScene` uses the whole screen; `BattleScene` and `TitleScene` keep their authored composition, centred (battle by `centreComposition()`, which is why its coordinates are still plain battle-screen coordinates). `#app` is sized to the canvas in script and *placed* by it too, on whole pixels: the room left over in a window is odd as often as it is even, and letting the page centre the screen stood the canvas and every menu on half a pixel, which the browser resolves by blurring all of it. It also carries `--px`, one game pixel, which is what the pixel-ui screens measure in; the older DOM screens still size against it as a CSS container (`cqw`/`em`). A CSS border on the canvas would take pixels off it and put the art back on a fractional scale, so it carries a `box-shadow` frame instead.
- A raid's clock is charged for walking, dialogue and hesitation, but never for a fight: `WorldScene.advanceRunClock()` ticks zero milliseconds while `pendingTrainerBattle` is set, because the challenge that announces a battle is the battle's first beat rather than the world's last. Dialogue the world raises on its own - the hunter's arrival, a trainer's line of sight, being caught - goes through `WorldScene.interrupt()`, which marks it `unsolicitedDialog` so a direction key advances it as well as SPACE and ENTER. A box that lands in front of you unasked has to answer the key you were already pressing; one you opened with the interact key does not. The deployment briefing is free for the same reason (`openingBriefingOpen`): the raid raises it on its first frame. A direction press that dismisses an unsolicited box is *spent* on it - `src/game/world/spentPresses.ts` reads that key as up until it is released - because the one press otherwise also walked a tile, into the hunter in the playtest.
- Every map names its parts: `src/game/world/districts.ts` holds each district as rectangles of tiles (first listed wins an overlap), and `WorldScene.noteDistrict()` raises the name as an **arrival plate** under the objective chip when the player walks into one - the way the games this is dressed as name a place - for `PLACE_PLATE_MS` of reading time, which does not run while a dialogue box is open (a raid opens on its briefing). Dropping in is an arrival; a battle handing the raid back is not (`arrivedFromBattle`), because the scene is rebuilt after every fight. It exists because a stranger who toured the Floodplain once could place every exit and gate from memory and still drew Old Town as two unrelated spots: every landmark said its own name and nothing said the town's. `districts.test.ts` holds that no walkable tile of a districted map is nameless and pins which place every exit, landmark and drop-in belongs to. Old Town is also held together on the ground - the town's own paving runs from the drowned street past the chapel and the last house to the South Gate, and its churchyard stones stand in the chapel's pool - because a name on a plate is not a substitute for a place reading as one.
- Every heading the raid prints - objective chip, hunter chip, field guide - is `compassBearing()` in `src/game/world/bearing.ts`, the nearest of eight points. Each used to name a bare quadrant, so a kit two tiles west and eleven south read `SW` under a briefing that called it SOUTH; `bearing.test.ts` holds the first briefing to the chip.
- The in-raid overlays are corner chips: `src/game/scenes/raidHud.ts` decides what they say (pure, tested without Phaser, in the manner of `battlePresentation.ts`) and `src/game/ui/RaidHud.ts` draws them. Anything added there is drawn over the map the player is reading, so it is sized to its own text and appears only while it is true. The clock's flash-and-sting threshold is `raidClockAlertTier`, separate from the colour ramp, so a visual tier can be added without adding an interruption.
- Phaser's key captures are global and outlive the scene that asked for them: `addCapture`, and `addKey` which captures by default, register on the game's KeyboardManager, so a paused `WorldScene` was still calling `preventDefault()` on O, P, B, K, SPACE and ENTER while an overlay it opened sat on top - which is why the field guide could only be closed with the mouse. Input ownership is therefore not a scene's to take: `src/game/ui/overlayKeyboard.ts` owns it, every `MenuOverlay` claims through it on construction and releases on `destroy()`, and one shared window listener in the *capture* phase gives the key to the topmost overlay and stops propagation so nothing below it - paused scene, Phaser captures, lower overlay - sees the key at all. Keyup is deliberately left to flow. A new overlay needs no key wiring of its own; `isOverlayDismissKey(event, 'o')` is the shared rule that Escape and the key that opened a screen both close it, and `overlayKeyboard.test.ts` reproduces the whole layering without a browser. Every DOM menu rebuilds its markup per change and so must say where focus lands: `MenuOverlay.focus()` takes an *ordered* list of selectors (`src/game/ui/menuFocus.ts`), because one comma-separated selector focuses whichever match is first in the document, and that is always the back button - whose Enter resumes the raid clock. For the same reason a timer must never `render()` a DOM screen: a rebuild between mousedown and mouseup eats the click, which is why `HubScene.setStatus()` removes its line rather than re-rendering.
- Every panel in the game is one drawn frame: `src/game/ui/pixelWindow.ts` reproduces `assets/battle/hud-box.png` - a one-pixel `#171717` border with clear corners - at any size, because that texture is 32x32 and smears when stretched small. Cream fill is screen furniture (`RaidHud`), a tinted fill with a coloured border is world annotation (`src/game/ui/WorldLabel.ts`), and `DialogBox` takes `pixelWindow: true` for the same frame, and `.px-window` in `src/style.css` is its DOM twin - drawn as backgrounds so the corners stay clear without a clip. Nothing stretches a panel texture any more - the battle dialogue and the two name plates were the last, and `hud-box.png`/`dialog-plain.png` are kept for provenance but no longer loaded; `pixelWindow.test.ts` fails any scene that passes `backgroundTexture` again.
- The classic tileset has no water art - its `POND_WATER` tile is the same green as grass - so `CLASSIC_TILESET` declares `WATER_TINT` on the water material. Do not paint detail-layer decorations over water: they render as one-tile lawns in open water.
- Overworld movement only turns in place against a blocked tile (`planNextGridStep`), so a landmark on walkable ground can never be faced from beside it unless a lane runs *into* that neighbouring tile pointing at it. The Sluice Wheel had no such lane, so the West Culvert it opens had never been openable. `WorldScene.advanceStep()` therefore also works a landmark you stand on, as it already does for loot and contract stops. All three return the line to say rather than showing it, so a deed done on ground a trainer is watching arrives in the same dialogue as the challenge instead of silencing it; `pois.test.ts` pins that, and which landmarks still fail the facing rule.
- `Pokemon` has no `cureStatus()` method; clear a status by setting `primaryStatus` to null directly, which `SaveManager` saves and restores.
- **This game is generation III - FireRed/LeafGreen - and the captain's roster is Kanto's original 151** (his ruling, 2026-09-19). Every stat, learnset, move number, type matchup and evolution rule is sourced from Gen III canon and labelled in line with where it came from; nothing is invented. The chart is **seventeen types**, which is exactly generation III: Dark and Steel arrived in generation II and are live in FRLG (Magnemite and Magneton are Electric/Steel, and fifty of the 151 learn a Dark or Steel move by level - Charmander's Metal Claw at 13 and Squirtle's Bite at 18 among them), while **Fairy is generation VI and stays out**. It was written up here as fifteen, which is true of generation I only; at fifteen the player's damage was simply wrong. Do not add Fairy, and do not shrink it back. Battle data was originally ported from the public Unity original, `OllySalanson/escapeFromPalletTown` (readable through `gh-axi api`), which took it from a tutorial whose author invented his own monsters; that is why the web game adds a 1.5x same-type bonus Unity has no equivalent of, why Unity fought its encounter tables with a level-10 five-strong party rather than one level-5 starter, and why a handful of shipped numbers are on a later generation's values or on none. **The whole 151 are in the game**, and none of it is typed out: `tools/species/harvest.mjs` writes the committed PokeAPI snapshot (generation III typing through `past_types`, catch rate, growth rate, the FireRed/LeafGreen level-up learnset, every evolution chain), `node tools/species/generate.mjs` turns it and the move and ability snapshots into `src/game/pokemon/generated/`, and `species.ts` builds a `PokemonBase` from four owners and nothing else - canon, `statCorrections.ts`, `shippedSpecies.ts`, and what the engine can actually play. **Read those two hand-authored files before arguing about a number.** `statCorrections.ts` is the one part of the import that came from neither source: PokeAPI serves no historical base stats at all, so the stats a later generation raised are hand-authored knowledge and are the first thing to check; `shippedSpecies.ts` is *every* disagreement between this game and canon, which is where Butterfree's Sp. Atk, Pikachu's Defence and Sp. Def, Jigglypuff's Sp. Def and the seventeen shipped learnsets live, left alone deliberately because changing a shipped species is an early-balance change - re-measure it over the real engine rather than porting a number across. Tackle's and Vine Whip's power, and the three status moves this game gives 100 accuracy, are the same argument for `moves.ts`, and `speciesImport.test.ts` prints both lists and fails if either grows without being written down. Unity carries **no** evolution data at all - `PokemonBase.cs` has no field for it - so every evolution rule is generated from PokeAPI's chains; the four **trade** lines (Kadabra, Machoke, Graveler, Haunter) and every stone but the Thunder Stone have no way to be triggered, and `docs/pokemon/roster.md` is the list of what else the import could not bring in, by species and with the reason.
- **Two Pokemon a side is a slot, and a slot is a place.** `BattleState` holds four of them - `player`/`enemy` are slot 0 and `playerPartner`/`enemyPartner` slot 1, one field per slot so nothing can drift out of step with anything else - and `unitAt`/`withUnit` in `battleEngine.ts` are the only two things that know that shape; everything else addresses a slot by `SlotRef`. Two a side and no more, because generation III's multi battle **is** the double battle. A double needs two able Pokemon on *each* side, so `createTrainerBattleState` refuses the second slot when the player has nobody for it and a `unitCount: 2` trainer fights a lone starter exactly as it always did - which is canon and also the only fair reading, since the alternative hands one Pokemon two actions a turn against its one. Every event's `slot` is optional and **absent means the lead**, and the engine omits it when it is 0, so a single battle emits character-for-character the events it emitted before slots existed: `battleEngine.test.ts` was not touched to make the refactor pass, and that is the regression guard. `resolveTurn` takes a move index (the lead's, at the lead foe) or one `PlayerMoveChoice` per slot. **The turn is ordered on four keys** - move priority, Quick Claw, Speed, then a fixed tie-break (the player's side first, lead before partner); generation III tosses a coin for a true Speed tie and this does not, because `trainerMeasure.ts`, `encounterMeasure.ts` and the whole boss ladder are numbers measured over this engine and a coin would move them. What fires **once per unit** and what fires **once per hit** is the distinction the whole thing turns on: the weather is the field's own turn, charged once for each unit after everybody has acted (`applyWeather`, four chips and never eight, and its five-turn clock spent once a turn); burn, poison and Leftovers hang off `applyEndOfAction`, and each unit takes exactly one action a turn, so each is charged once; an ability that answers a hit is asked once per attacker, so two Pokemon hitting the same Static both answer it; and gear is per Pokemon (`heldItemSpent`), so nothing double-applies. Intimidate lands on **both** foes. `doubleBattle.test.ts` holds all of it, and `docs/screens/double-battle/` is what it looks like.
- **A move that hits more than one is `MoveTarget.BothFoes`**, which is PokeAPI's `all-opponents` read off the committed FRLG snapshot rather than chosen: five shipped moves carry it (Growl, Tail Whip, Razor Leaf, Heat Wave, Bubble) and each was checked by hand against generation III, because PokeAPI has no `past_values` for a target. **The import carries it too**: `tools/species/generate.mjs` mapped PokeAPI's `user` to `MoveTarget.Self` and let everything else fall to `Foe`, which flattened every `all-opponents` move the 151 brought in - invisible while there could only be one foe on the field. Mapped through, sixteen distinct spread moves are in play and 106 of the 151 learn one by level, and `doubleBattle.test.ts` pins that off the same snapshot the generator reads so a regeneration cannot flatten it again. It changes nothing in a single battle by construction (one foe is one foe), which the boss ladder confirms number for number. `all-other-pokemon` (Earthquake, Explosion, Magnitude, Self-Destruct) also hits your own partner and has no `MoveTarget`, so those four are left aiming at one foe rather than silently made into something the engine cannot say - quietly narrow rather than quietly wrong. Each target gets its own accuracy roll, damage roll, effectiveness and secondary roll, at `SPREAD_DAMAGE_MULTIPLIER` (generation III's half) while more than one is actually hit; the same-type bonus is a fact about the attacker and so is the same against both. A spread move **names itself once** and then reports each target in its own `spread-damage` line, because one number could only be a sum and a sum explains neither hit - and it reports only a hit that took HP, so a spread *status* move puts no "nothing happened" lines on screen. A single-target move aimed at a foe that fell earlier in the same turn is re-aimed at whoever is still standing; with nobody left it is `no-target`.
- **The one double battle is WARDEN HOLT's**, and where it is was measured (`tools/trainers/report.mts`). A double battle is two decisions a turn, and in a raid measured in the player's patience only a boss holding the last door can charge that: Maya and the route trainers are tolls paid on a route you can walk round, and Briggs is the first door in the game. Holt is behind Briggs, is the top of the ladder already and has the vault behind her fence. Three measured facts: it costs a player who brought **one** Pokemon nothing (24/12/0% for the three starters, unmoved, because the engine refuses the second slot); it is harder for the player who brought a team (73% -> 48% for Charmander 12 + Pidgey 10, which puts the last door level with Dane instead of a third easier), and a party of three barely notices (78% -> 77%), which is the lesson the door teaches; and it is **shorter, not longer** - the same three Pokemon fall in 3.8 turns instead of 8.8 and put 25 lines of log on screen instead of 33, because both of yours swing every turn. What doubles is the decisions, not the reading.
- The double battle's screen is a second layout, not a generalisation of the first: `statusPlateLayout`/`combatantSpot` in `battlePresentation.ts` keep the single battle's authored numbers untouched and give the double two plate bands (148x44, three rows) with the field between them. What came off to make four plates fit is the floating WILD/FOE/RIVAL banner - nothing is set below `CHIP_FONT_SIZE`, the typing moved onto the plate itself because that is what a player reads to pick a move, and position already says which side is which. The player's compact plate carries its HP numbers on the bar's row (with a shorter bar) because the row below is holding the typing and the gear. `BattleScene` keeps one `CombatantPlate` and one sprite per slot in maps keyed by side and slot; a tween that outlived the plate it was drawing on stops rather than writing to a destroyed `Text`. Asking who to aim at is its own command mode (`target`), offered only for a single-target move with two foes standing, and the move list leads its guidance line with the chooser's name because a double battle asks twice before it resolves anything.
- **A move says what it does; the resolver reads it and knows no move's name.** `MoveBaseInit` in `src/game/pokemon/MoveBase.ts` is the tutorial's `MoveBase` shape ported (its field list, the `MoveEffects`/`SecondaryEffects` split, `MoveTarget`, `MoveFlag`) onto our own pure resolver - `priority`, `alwaysHits`, `target`, `hits`, `critStage`, `recoil`, `drain`, `healing`, `charge`, one guaranteed `effects` and any number of `secondaries`, each with its own `chance` and its own `target`. `MoveBase`'s constructor normalizes every optional, so `applyMove` never guards one. **A new status move is a data row**: the name-matching table that used to inflict four statuses by string is gone, which is also what stops a renamed move losing its effect silently. Two targets matter more than they look - a secondary with `target: Self` is how Metal Claw raises the *user's* Attack, and before it existed every boost in the engine went to the other side. Accuracy and evasion are stages with no stat behind them (`statStages.ts`), on generation III's **own `3/(3-n)` ladder**, which is not the damage table; `critStage` indexes a ladder too. `charge`/`recharge` live on `BattleCombatant.pendingMove` and survive into the next turn, so `resolveTurn` overrules the move it was passed and `BattleScene` asks `lockedMove()` before it opens the command menu. `rollHitCount` uses generation III's uneven 2-5 weighting rather than a uniform roll. `MoveEffects.weather` is the one field added since - see the weather note below. `moveEffects.test.ts` holds every category without Phaser. **`MoveFlag` is no longer decoration**: Static reads `contact` and Soundproof reads `sound`, so a physical move shipped without its flag is an ability quietly not working - which half the catalogue was - and `abilityCanon.test.ts` holds every shipped move's flags against PokeAPI's own tables (`tools/abilities/frlg-move-flags.json`; the REST API serves no flags at all). **What fraction of the 273 moves the 151 need is expressible is a number, not a guess**: `node tools/moves/coverage.mjs`, 188 of them, counted by `tools/moves/classify.mjs` - which is also what the species generator builds the catalogue with, so the count and the moves a Pokemon actually knows cannot disagree, and a move that is not expressible is left out of a learnset rather than approximated. `tools/moves/harvest.mjs` rebuilds the snapshot (see `tools/moves/README.md` for the three generation III traps it handles). Two things a playtest of a move effect has to know: a **fainted Pokemon takes no secondary**, which is canon, and a wild Pokemon on the shipped tables dies to one swing from anything that has learnt these moves - so a 10% roll only has somewhere to land on a **resisted** matchup or against a trainer.
- **An ability is a data row with hooks; the resolver reads the hooks and knows no ability's name.** `src/game/pokemon/AbilityBase.ts` is the vocabulary - the tutorial's `Ability` hook set, made pure: every hook is a function of a small context returning a number or a decision, and only the engine writes state. `abilities.ts` is the catalogue and `battle/abilityHooks.ts` is the one module the engine asks, exactly as `heldItems.ts` is for gear - so adding an ability is a catalogue row and nothing else. *Which* ability is carried is read through to `PokemonBase.abilityId` and never copied onto a `BattleCombatant`; what a fight owns is two bits on the combatant (`abilityCharged`, which only Flash Fire sets, and `abilityAnnounced`). One ability per species, because generation III gives a Pokemon one of its species' one or two slots and no save records which. **Everything an ability does arrives as a battle line** (`AbilityEffectKind` plus `abilityLine()` in `battlePresentation.ts`), because an ability is never shown in a menu and watching it is the only way to learn one; the four *continuous* effects speak once a battle and the rest every time. `node tools/abilities/coverage.mjs` is the number - 45 of the 55 abilities the 151 carry - and names what is out with the reason; the four that are **only** about the weather (Chlorophyll, Swift Swim, Sand Veil, Cloud Nine) read `battle/weather.ts`'s own table through their hooks rather than restating a rule of it, and `effectiveWeather()` in the engine is the single place Cloud Nine is asked, so no other rule knows it exists; `tools/abilities/README.md` has the three generation III traps a re-harvest must handle (`past_abilities` reads **forwards**, unlike a move's `past_values`) and the four abilities whose generation III meaning is not their modern one. `npx vite-node tools/abilities/measure.mts` prints what a change to abilities moved: every district's table against each starter, and every hunter rung against a party at its level plus one. It earned its keep at once - a wild Pikachu's Static took the Floodplain Vault from 55% to 46% for Charmander, under the floor `districtEncounters.test.ts` holds the starting maps to, and the answer was the table's composition rather than a quieter ability.
- Evolution is `src/game/pokemon/evolution.ts`: a **generated** rule table (`from`, `to`, and a level, a stone or a trade - `generated/evolutionRules.ts`, whose header says what the filtering throws away), and four pure questions asked of it. A trade can never be triggered and sixteen of the seventeen stone rules name a stone that is not an item, so those lines are unreachable and `evolution.test.ts` pins that rather than letting it be rediscovered; the rules are kept because the *family* is what a save's move lookup and `pokemonCargo.ts`'s stage count read. A level evolution happens inside `Pokemon.gainExperience`, right after the level and before that level's moves are learned, and rides home in `ExperienceResult.evolutions`; a stone evolution is an `evolution-stone` item effect resolved by `useFieldItem`, so the in-raid Bag is already the place it is spent and no menu knows about it. `Pokemon.base` is therefore mutable, written only by `evolveInto`, which refuses anything that is not further along the same line - that refusal is what makes a replayed settlement a no-op. A raid carries the species home in `RaidCondition.speciesId` as well as the experience, because a stone spends no experience for `applyRaidCondition` to replay. A save needs no new field (`SavedPokemon.speciesId` already is the species), but a save's moves are looked up across `evolutionFamily()` rather than one learnset, or an Ivysaur would lose the Super Sonic only Bulbasaur teaches. Evolution lands **above** the hunter ladder rather than inside it, and the ladder's fourth rung is the answer: see the note below.
- A figure with no design of its own is drawn from the one `character` sheet, which holds a single character and no second design, so nothing in that art distinguishes them. `src/game/world/characterPresentation.ts` is what does: role tints (the player is the only figure in that sheet's true colours) plus the player's own ground ring and head chevron, both pure pixel tables so `characterPresentation.test.ts` can assert the rules without Phaser. `WorldScene.setPlayerPosition()` is the only place the player moves, and it carries both marks and the player's y-sorted depth with it.
- **Townsfolk keep a beat; trainers never do.** `src/game/world/npcIdle.ts` is the schedule - Phaser-free, handed a frame's milliseconds and answering with the beats inside it - and `WorldEntity.idle` authors it: `roam` is the whole set of tiles a person may ever stand on besides the one they were placed on, `glances` the ways they look, `beatMs` their own interval (never shared with a neighbour, or a street marches in step). It runs outside the raid clock and off `Math.random` rather than the run's seed, so the street costs nothing and a seed still plays the same raid; beats stop dead while anything is on screen to read, and a figure owns both ends of its step until the step has finished drawing. A figure blocks its own tile, so a beat can break a map: `mapStructure.test.ts` holds every map with **every** beat tile solid at once - stricter than any position the game can be in - and asks separately that each beat tile is walkable ground one step from the last, that nothing a raid is for is ever stood on, and that no walk between two named places is longer with the whole street off its mark. A trainer has no beat on purpose: a watch is authored from position and facing and is the price on a route, so a trainer who turned would move the ground the player is being charged for. **It is not a cutscene and could not be one**: a cutscene is a finite authored list that owns the frame, locks the player out and stops the raid clock until it is `done` (`checkCutscene` caps its self-running time at two seconds), while a beat is endless, runs *while* the player walks, and must never take the frame or a millisecond of the clock. What the two do share is how a figure is drawn: `idleFrames()` answers in fractional tiles exactly as `CutsceneActorFrame` does, a townsperson walks a tile on elapsed milliseconds rather than on a tween for the same reason a cutscene actor does, and `WorldScene.placeFigure()` is the one place any figure that is not the player is positioned, depth-sorted and posed.
- An authored `WorldEntity` or trainer can instead name a `design` from the registry in `src/game/world/characterDesigns.ts` - FireRed/LeafGreen figures under `public/assets/characters/`, preloaded by `BootScene` with their walk cycles. `getWorldCharacterAppearance(role, design)` is the one rule: a designed figure is drawn from its own sheet untinted (a multiply tint only muddies finished art) and is never marked, and the `protagonist` designs are refused on every role but the player, so no one else can be dressed as the hero. Every non-player figure is made by `WorldScene.createFigure()` and turned by `faceFigure()`, because a frame index depends on the sheet's width (`getIdleFrame(direction, sheetColumns)`) and only the stored appearance knows it. The designs stand 17-20px tall against the shared sheet's 23, soles on the same row; `headPixelY` in the registry is where each head starts, and the player's chevron is still placed from the shared sheet's head. Adding a design is a row in `scripts/cut-frlg-characters.mjs`, a registry entry and a provenance line - `characterDesigns.test.ts` fails any one without the others. Only the Route 1 trainer is cast so far; placing people is map work.
- Ground loot draws as the icon of the item it is (`itemIconName(loot.itemId)` in `WorldScene.createLoot`), in `MARKER_BAND`; only an item with no icon falls back to the supply crate. Judged at 3x, the potion line differs by colour alone (red, orange, purple) and the radio valve's grey-on-wood body reads close to the field kit's, so a new material icon should not be grey-on-wood.
- Item, objective and raid-marker art is one 16x16 icon set under `public/assets/icons`, named once in `src/game/ui/icons.ts` and drawn by both the DOM menus (`itemIcon`/`objectiveIcon`) and `WorldScene` (`iconTextureKey`, preloaded in `BootScene`). Sizes are whole multiples of 16 everywhere so nothing is resampled; `src/game/ui/icons.test.ts` pins the file list, the size and that no scene has gone back to a text glyph.
- `public/assets/ASSET_PROVENANCE.md` records where every shipped asset came from, who authored it and under what licence; adding an asset without its entry there is an incomplete change. Most of it is carried over byte-identical from the captain's Unity project `OllySalanson/escapeFromPalletTown` (blob SHAs, re-checkable with `gh-axi api`); `Overworld.png` and `character.png` are traced past it to ArMM1998's CC0 pack and proved by hash; the icons were drawn here, and `battle/orange-kid.woff2` comes from Typodermic's own CC0 page. `frlg-tiles.png` and `characters/*.png` are ripped FireRed/LeafGreen art the captain accepted knowingly on 2026-09-19 - cut frames only, never a Spriters Resource sheet in the format it was downloaded in, and never onto a marketplace. Two files are flagged as unsettled: `battle/background-grass.png`, and `tileset.png`, whose origin is not established and which the pixels say is GBA Pokemon-family art. Verify at the publisher rather than assume, and never resolve a flagged question in code instead of in that file.
- Every species sprite is `public/assets/pokemon/{front,back}/<dexId>.png` - all 302 of them, the FireRed/LeafGreen rip of PokeAPI's sprite repository, fetched by `node scripts/fetch-pokemon-sprites.mjs`, which checks the signature and the size of each file as it lands. `spriteAssets.test.ts` enforces the format and a size bound, because a dimensionless SVG once rasterised to 150x150 and covered the battle screen.
- A knockout in a trainer fight is a decision, not a cutscene: the engine already swaps the next Pokemon in and emits `enemy-sent-out`, and `BattleScene.showNextCombatMessage()` holds that one line at the head of the queue to ask whether to switch. Either answer puts the same line back up, which is what makes the switch free - `switchPokemon` spends the enemy's turn on a swap, and there is no turn to spend on one made against a Pokemon that has not landed. The cursor starts on NO, as `trainerChallengePrompt` starts on BACK AWAY, because the prompt opens on the key that finished the previous line; it is offered once per Pokemon in the trainer's party, never during a forced replacement and never with an empty bench. It is a state of the battle panel rather than a cutscene: `cutscene.ts` moves overworld figures and is measured against the raid clock, and nothing in a battle is either.
- Battle text lives in `src/game/scenes/battlePresentation.ts` as pure functions, so message wording, move guidance and layout are testable without Phaser. The bottom of the battle screen is one panel: `BATTLE_PANEL` is the rectangle the dialogue, the command grid (`mainCommandLayout` - two rows always, a third *column* for a wild fight's five commands), the move and item lists and the party list (`partyRowLayout`, two abreast so six fit) are all drawn in, in the same cream `pixelWindow`, and it stops below the player's HP plate because that is the number a Potion's target is chosen by. A refusal takes over the party list's prompt line (`partyPrompt`) rather than being pinned under the list. Every combatant is named through `combatantName()` and every level through `levelLabel()` - `:L6` read as `16`. The enemy banner's role is `enemyBannerRole()`: RIVAL is the hunter's word and an authored trainer fights as FOE.
- A `BattleCombatant` in `src/game/pokemon/battle/battleEngine.ts` is a snapshot of its Pokemon taken when it was sent out, which is what lets a battle own PP, status, its counters and stat stages without writing them to the party. Level and stats are read through `combatant.pokemon` and so are already live; the move *list* and the HP a raised maximum grants are not, and both arrive with a level-up. `refreshCombatantAfterLevelUp` is the reconciliation - PP carried across by move identity rather than position, a fainted combatant left fainted - and `BattleScene.applyMidBattleLevelUp` is its only caller, from `awardVictoryExperience`, where it also rewrites the level plate the scene now holds as `playerLevelText`. Only a trainer battle with a second Pokemon keeps a fight alive across a knockout, which is why a plate frozen at the old level and a move menu that would not offer the move it had just announced went unseen. A party member that levels on the bench needs none of it: `replacePlayerPokemon` reads it live when it is sent out. Relatedly, `Pokemon.gainExperience` grants the new maximum's HP but never to a fainted Pokemon - a level is not a revive.
- Trainer experience is paid out per knockout by `BattleScene.awardTrainerDefeatExperience`, and only `useMove()` calls it. A trainer Pokemon that faints on a turn the player spent on an item or a switch - to poison, burn or a confusion self-hit - therefore pays nothing, because the victory branch of `handleBattleEnd` shows the trainer's defeat line instead of awarding. Not fixed; noted so it is not rediscovered as part of something else.
- Driving the real game end to end: `npm run dev -- --port <free port>` plus Playwright's Chromium, launched by `tools/playtest/browser.mjs` (`ensure-libs.sh` beside it supplies the `libnss3`/`libnspr4`/`libasound2t64` that Chromium needs, without root), and CDP `Input.dispatchKeyEvent` for keys. A tap is enough everywhere - Phaser scenes through `KeyPresses`, DOM menus natively - and `raid.mjs --taps` plays a whole raid on down-and-up-back-to-back presses to keep it so. `tools/playtest/` is that recipe packaged (`README.md` there: test modes, stepping, hygiene, and `--insertion=`/`--beaten=`/`--completed=`/`--level=`/`--work=` for deploying to any map, with a team that could have got there, and reaching any ending); `raid.mjs` plays a whole raid and picks its exit by walked distance, because an exit can be open by its own rule and behind a shut gate. `raid.mjs --grab=itemId` walks to every piece of that item *this* raid laid, read off the run plan, and `--secure=itemId[:n]` takes the secure-slot detour on the way out: loot is re-seated every raid and the container reserves room by kind, so nothing else can check either end to end. A driver that steps the loop itself must know the three states in which nothing is on screen to press through and the world is about to change anyway - an authored beat running (`WorldScene.cutscene` - the watch's mark and walk-up is one), the fight queued behind the last line of dialogue, and `WorldScene.isWarping` while the hand-over to the battle fades - or it stops stepping mid-beat and asks the map a question the fight is about to change the answer to; `raid.mjs`'s `pendingBattle` is that, and without it a boss fight simply never happened. `window.__escapeFromPalletTownGame__` (from `gameLifecycle.ts`) reaches every scene, so `scene.getScene('world').collisionData` gives a real map to path through and `getScene('battle').state` is the battle to assert against; seed `localStorage['escape-from-pallet-town.save.v1']` to start from a state that would otherwise take a long grind to reach.
- Browser checks are rationed, because six workers each holding a 60fps game under software WebGL is what got processes OOM-killed on this box. `tools/playtest/README.md` is the recipe and the measurements (the same raid: 40 CPU-seconds plain, 2.9 in test mode, 1.2 stepped); `tools/playtest/browser.mjs` is the launch - do not restate either. The rules: one browser per verification session, closed when its checks are done, and the dev server stopped with it; never a live game page while thinking or editing (`pauseLoop()`, or drive the check with `stepFrames` so it never runs live at all); the CDP port comes from the OS (`--remote-debugging-port=0`), never a fixed number, because another worker is on it; everything under `nice -n 15`; `?testmode=1` for every logic check, `?testmode=pixels` for a screenshot - Phaser's Canvas renderer draws no tints, so a `?testmode=1` frame shows grass where the water is - and one plain-URL pass at real speed before reporting done. Test mode is `src/game/dev/testMode.ts`: a development-build URL flag (`VITE_EPTW_TEST_MODE` for a build), so production cannot be put into it. `vitest.config.ts` caps the suite at four workers for the same reason; `VITEST_MAX_WORKERS` raises it.
- Anything the world times for itself must survive a 100ms frame, because that is what test mode hands it. Walking did not: a step could only end on a frame and the rest of that frame was dropped, so a tile cost 150ms of raid clock at 60fps and 300ms at ten. `src/game/movement/stepClock.ts` carries the overflow into the next step (`STEP_DURATION_MS` is the one number a tile costs, at any frame rate), and a press shorter than a frame is kept too: a scene that polls keys loses one both ways, because `isDown` is false again and `Key.onUp` clears the flag `JustDown` reads. `src/game/input/KeyPresses.ts` is what `WorldScene` and `BattleScene` ask instead of `Phaser.Input.Keyboard.JustDown`, and `pressLatch.ts` beside it is the rule - a press is kept for the frame that processed it and no later, so one made under a dialogue or mid-step is still dropped. A new scene that polls a key goes through `KeyPresses`; a new per-frame `Math.min(1, progress + delta / duration)` is the other pattern to distrust. Phaser's own timers and tweens were already safe.
- One typeface, and it is a load-time dependency: `GAME_FONT` in `src/game/ui/gameFont.ts` is the only family any canvas scene names, `index.html` preloads the woff2 with the document, and `BootScene.create()` waits on `awaitGameFont()` before starting a scene. Phaser paints canvas text with whatever face is ready at that instant and never repaints it, so without both halves the first battle of a session drew its name plates and type banners in the browser monospace while the dialogue below came out in Orange Kid. `awaitGameFont` resolves either way, so a missing file delays boot rather than wedging it. Sizes for the raid screen are `src/game/ui/screenType.ts`, picked to hold the letter heights the HUD was designed at rather than the numbers that used to be typed. Canvas text is hard-edged because `installPixelText()` (`src/game/ui/pixelText.ts`, called from `main.ts`) replaces every Phaser `Text` context's `fillText`/`strokeText`/`measureText` with a run-time bitmap font: each glyph is rasterised once in a fixed ink (Chrome antialiases white text heavier than black, so a glyph measured in one colour and painted in another came out a pixel different), thresholded by `inkMask` - which keeps a thin stroke that fell across two pixels as one - and stamped ink-tight at whole-pixel advances. Orange Kid is an outline face whose own grid is about 12.5px: it does not survive below 12px (`W` reads `V`), and at 16-18px stems are a pixel and a quarter, so one word mixes one- and two-pixel letters and the gap under a dot closes (`kit is` read `klt ls`, `appeared!` read `appearedl`). `screenType.ts` holds the sizes that come through whole - 12px chips and captions, 14px for the clock, dialogue and commands - so name one of those rather than typing a number, and check any new size on a specimen of lowercase and punctuation first. `gameFont.test.ts` fails any scene or widget that names a family literally - the title, party and bag scenes all named `monospace` while the rule was checked against three files.
- **Weather is the field both sides are standing in, and it is a place before it is a move.** `src/game/pokemon/battle/weather.ts` is the table (the tutorial's `WeatherConditionsDB` ported as rows the engine reads, so nothing in `applyMove` knows a weather's name): sandstorm and hail chip a sixteenth of maximum HP at the end of every turn sparing generation III's own types - **Rock, Ground and Steel**, which the tutorial's fifteen-wide chart could not say - while rain and harsh sunlight bend Fire and Water. `BattleState.weather` is what is over the field now and `ambientWeather` is what the *place* has, so a move covers a place's weather for `WEATHER_MOVE_TURNS` and hands it back rather than clearing it. The field is charged once a turn, after both sides have acted, at the end of `resolveTurn` **and** of `resolveEnemyTurn` - every path that resolves a turn ends in exactly one of the two - which is why it does not hang off `applyEndOfAction` the way burn and Leftovers do. Set two ways: `MoveEffects.weather` (the gap `MoveBase` named as the next thing it wanted; Rain Dance is the only one of the four the shipped roster learns by level, on the Squirtle line at 33/37/42, and `tools/moves/coverage.mjs` now counts all four as expressible - 198 moves to 201) and `MapDistrict.weather`, read off the tile by `weatherAt()` and passed into the fight by `WorldScene.transitionToBattle` - never carried in `RaidCarriage`, because it is a fact about where you are rather than raid state. The HUD says it: a chip under the objective, above the arrival plate (`weatherChipLine` in `raidHud.ts`), because a caption now speaks only within five steps and weather has no tiles to stand near.
- **A place may bend damage and may never chip HP**, and that is measured rather than tasteful - `tools/weather/measure.mts` and the rule in `districts.ts`. The chip is a sixteenth **floored at one**, and at the levels this game is played at the floor is what binds, so weather is a flat one HP a turn to everybody - which is paid by whoever has the fewest Pokemon. Over 300 fights a cell it *helps* the player in a wild fight and at the bottom of the hunter ladder (rung 2, 94% -> 99%) and destroys the top (rung 4, four against three: 49% -> 17%, and the health kept on a win 19% -> 9%). The hunter arrives wherever the player stands, so a chipping district would silently reprice the one fight a raid cannot decline. Of the two that only bend, harsh sunlight is a 14-point *gift* on rung 4 - 63% against 49% - because the hunter's team carries no Water and the player's Fire starter does, while rain costs 10, so **rain is the only weather any place has**: THE REEDBEDS, THE FLOOD and BROOK HEAD, three of twenty districts, each one a place made of water. What rain is *worth* is measured with the lead using its own signature move (`measure.mts --lead`), because a fight with no Water move in it is a fight rain cannot touch and a resisted signature move is one `bestPlay.ts` will rightly decline to throw. That flag once had a second reason, which is gone: the shared harnesses scored power times effectiveness and could not see the same-type bonus, so a Squirtle answered a Pidgey with Tackle. Played honestly a Water lead leaves those places on 94% of its health against 82% clear, a Fire lead on 70% against 90%, and a Grass lead is untouched; Brook Head is the one place rain decides outright (Charmander 88% -> 44%) and it is the *wild* Squirtles doing it, because at level 7-9 they have Water Gun. Sandstorm and hail ship as rules with nowhere that has them - no Kanto species learns either by level in FRLG - and a desert or an ice cave may author one once the ladder has been re-measured under it. It runs through `world/trainerMeasure.ts`, the harness the authored trainers are measured with (and now carries what a win *cost* as well as how often it is won), which is harsher than the one `HUNTER_TIERS` was set in - no items unless asked for, no switching before a faint - so read its columns against each other and not against the note above. Rain is also what the Floodplain checkpoint is fought in, THE REEDBEDS being one of the three: inert while nobody has a typed move, and from level 7 it halves the Fire starter's Ember, so that road comes free for a Charmander a level after the other two.

- **Wildlife is authored per district, beside its name.** `MapDistrict.encounters` (`src/game/world/districts.ts`) is the table a place's tall grass rolls on, built from the named `*_WILDLIFE` tables in `src/game/pokemon/encounters.ts`; `encounterTableAt()` in `localEncounters.ts` resolves a step (district table, else the map's `encounters`), and `WorldScene.encountersAtCurrentTile()` asks it. A raid varies every district's table on its own seed stream (`RunPlan.districtEncounters`, keyed by district id), so giving a place wildlife moves no loot, trainer or hunter roll of any seed. `PALLET_TALL_GRASS` and `VIRIDIAN_FOREST_TALL_GRASS` are now only the map fallback (no shipped district falls back to them - `districtEncounters.test.ts` fails tall grass in an unauthored place) and the standing board's reward pool. A new district with tall grass needs a table; a new species is an entry in the table of the place it lives in. `starterIdentity.test.ts` still pins the fallback, and the same 'every signature move has a target' rule is held **per map** in `districtEncounters.test.ts`, not per place. Since the import the tables are Kanto's own - Rattata and Pidgey on open ground, Oddish and Bellsprout in the dug rows, Psyduck, Poliwag and Magikarp wherever there is standing water, the Caterpie and Weedle lines through a forest that is finally mostly Bug - and three things learned by measuring them: **a wild Growlithe beats a level-5 starter every time** (Bite is 60 power at level 1), **Oddish and Bellsprout take a Squirtle to nil** and **a Poliwag takes a Charmander to nil** before either has a Water move, so each of those is the rare roll in the one place it belongs. A table may also not name a species with no move to make: Abra and Ditto know only Teleport and Transform and come out of the import with nothing to throw, which is a fight neither side can end. Difficulty is measured, not eyeballed: `tools/encounters/report.mts` (`--partner` fixed per map) prints each place's mix, mean level and each starter's win rate over the real engine via `world/encounterMeasure.ts`, and the test holds floors on it.
- Nothing distinguishes the three starters before level 7 except their level-1 kit. `computePokemonStats` is `floor(base * level / 100) + 5`, so at level 5 every starter stat sits within one point of the others, and every typed move is gated to level 7 - which is why pulling signature moves earlier has been measured and rejected twice (it makes one starter dominant, or arms the wild roster with 3x hits a 17 HP starter cannot read). Balance changes here belong in movesets, type matchups or encounter composition, re-measured over the real engine.
