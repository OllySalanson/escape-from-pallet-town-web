# Project agent memory

This file is the project's committed home for project-intrinsic agent knowledge: build, test, release, architecture, and sharp-edge notes that should travel with the code.

- Update this file in the same change whenever work adds a subsystem, changes where something is registered, or contradicts a note below. This is part of finishing the task, not optional cleanup.

## Project notes

- The overworld's tile collision is built by `buildCollisionData()` in `src/game/worldMap.ts`; interactive NPC and sign definitions live in `src/game/world/npcs.ts`.
- Each raid map is authored tile by tile in its own file under `src/game/world/maps/`, drawn as character art through the helpers in `src/game/world/mapGrid.ts`, with the tile vocabulary and its tints in `src/game/world/tiles.ts`. Edit the drawing, not a generated grid.
- A map is judged as a network of passages, not by how much ground it has: counting walkable tiles says nothing, because an open field scores well on it. `src/game/world/mapStructure.ts` computes the two numbers that do say it - longest straight walk and connected open ground - and `mapStructure.test.ts` holds every map to them, along with reachability of every exit and landmark from every insertion, a sealed map edge, a fair hunter arrival from any tile, and a flee that buys real separation without walling the player in. Change a map and re-run that suite rather than eyeballing the result.
- Hedges, trees and tall grass are the same leafy art in the classic tileset, so on maps built out of them nothing distinguishes a wall from a lane. `TREE_TINT` and `TALL_GRASS_TINT` in `tiles.ts` are what does, applied in `WorldScene.createMap()`. Tints multiply, so they can only darken.
- A trainer is a price on a route only if walking the route is what starts the fight. `WorldScene.tryInteract()` needs the player to press the interact key, so a trainer standing in a one-tile lane is a locked door, not a cost. `src/game/world/trainerSight.ts` is the answer: an authored `sightRange` makes the trainer challenge anyone who steps into the straight run of tiles they are facing, `WorldScene.tryTrainerChallengeAt()` fires it after extraction and before the tall-grass roll, and `createTrainerWatch()` shades that ground and captions the trainer so the price is on screen before the player is in it. Only the Floodplain checkpoint is authored with a watch; the other three trainers are still spoken to. `mapStructure.test.ts` holds every watch to the rules that keep it fair (never over an insertion, an exit or a warp arrival) and `floodplainRelay.test.ts` holds that map's road-versus-reeds choice to route facts.
- Active raid scene data is defined by `ActiveRunSession` in `src/game/run/RunSession.ts` and must pass unchanged between `WorldScene` and `BattleScene`.
- The raid field guide is `ObjectivesScene` in `src/game/scenes/ObjectivesScene.ts`; derive its objective text from `buildObjectiveGuide()` in `src/game/objectives/ObjectiveGuide.ts`, and have it resume only the `WorldScene` instance it paused. The guide reads the contract's own `briefing`, so a new contract writes its lines in `contracts.ts` and needs no change here. It names a map's landmark the same way - from the `WorldPoi`'s own `label` and `description` - because the guide is read on four maps and a landmark name written as a literal there sent players on the other three to a place that does not exist.
- Contracts live in `src/game/objectives/contracts.ts` - the data is the design. A contract earns its place by changing the shape of a raid, so each one carries the mechanism that does it: several `markers` (the braid survey is three stops), a `requiredExitLabel` (only the West Culvert banks the cordon ledger), or a marker's `carriedIn` (the warden's resupply spends Potions out of your own pack). Adding a contract that is only a new position on a map is adding a waypoint. They unlock in a chain via `unlockedBy`, one contract belongs to one map so choosing an insertion *is* choosing a contract (`contractForMap`), and `contracts.test.ts` holds the whole set to those rules.
- A contract's permanent reward is derived from `raidProgress.completedContracts`, never stored separately: `secureItemStackLimit()` and `contractRestockBonus()` in `contracts.ts` are the whole of the secure-slot and restock-floor upgrades, so a save can never disagree with the contract list. `SaveManager.bankContract()` is the one banking path and grants each reward exactly once; `bankFirstContractRun()` is only its alias. A save written before contracts were a list is migrated by `firstContractExtracted`.
- Scenes are registered in `src/game/gameConfig.ts`, not `main.ts`.
- Both starter selection surfaces (`StarterScene` and `HubScene`'s `reselect` view) share their markup through `src/game/ui/starterPicker.ts`; the reselection rule itself is `Stash.swapStarter()`, and `SaveManager.reselectStarter()` also rewrites `starterSpeciesId` so later wipe re-grants follow the new choice.
- Background music is intentionally off for playtesting: `SILENCE_BACKGROUND_THEMES` in `src/game/audio/AudioManager.ts` makes `startTheme()` a no-op while sound effects keep playing. Flip it to `false` to restore the music; leave the scene `startTheme` calls alone.
- `SaveManager` accepts five save versions, and accepting one is a promise to honour everything in it. Versions 1 to 3 were written by the free-roam game, where the player's team was `party` and their supplies were `bag` - fields the extraction game never reads, because the vault is the team. Loading one used to say yes and hand back an empty vault, which `Stash.ensurePlayable()` filled with a fresh level-5 starter and `TitleScene` wrote back over the file, destroying a levelled team without a word. `moveFreeRoamHoldingsIntoVault()` in `SaveManager.ts` is the migration, gated on `LAST_FREE_ROAM_SAVE_VERSION` and on the vault being entirely empty - the signature of a free-roam save and of nothing else - and it moves rather than copies, so nothing is banked twice. Versions 4 and 5 are deliberately untouched: there an empty vault means a wipe, and the fresh starter is the intended answer. Every accepted version is pinned in `SaveManager.test.ts`; a new version must be added there as well as to the accept list.
- Every path back into play after a wipe must leave the player able to attempt a run: `MINIMUM_SUPPLIES` and `Stash.restockMinimumSupplies()` in `src/game/stash/Stash.ts` own that guarantee, and `Stash.ensurePlayable()`, `Stash.swapStarter()` and `SaveManager.applyWipeLoss()` all route through it. It only ever tops up the shortfall, so it never removes anything and cannot be farmed.
- Deployment is a stepped route owned by `DeploymentFlow` in `src/game/hub/deploymentFlow.ts`: base → loadout (secure slot is a detour) → final check → raid. `HubScene` renders the steps; only `deploy()` on a confirmed flow yields a party, so nothing may pre-select a raid party or start a run from an earlier step.
- The lobby leads with the raid it is sending you on: the two action cards, then the contract board. Anything that is a recovery route rather than a next step belongs where the thing it acts on lives - the swap-partner offer is rendered in the stash view beside the Pokemon it would trade away, not on the base screen, because on a fresh save it was the loudest panel three seconds after the player chose that partner and it pushed the lobby past the frame. Every step must fit `BASE_STAGE` scaled to the window without a browser scrollbar, and the loadout's committing actions live in the full-width `confirm-bar` the final check already ends on rather than at the bottom of a column, where they went below the fold. Lobby measurements are `cqw`/`em` against `#app`, never `vw`: a margin that grows with the browser window while the screen inside it does not is what tips a screen past the frame.
- The hunter arrives at a fixed walking distance: `findHunterSpawnTile()` in `src/game/world/hunter.ts` takes every tile exactly `HUNTER_SPAWN_DISTANCE` steps away, so the spawn is always reachable and always the same lead time, and returns null rather than place it closer than `HUNTER_MINIMUM_SPAWN_DISTANCE`. `WorldScene.placeHunterIfDue()` retries on the next tick when it does, and `moveHunterToCurrentMap()` must run after `createMap()` on a warp or the search reads the map the player just left.
- Hunter pursuit is a breadth-first search in `src/game/world/hunter.ts`: `findHunterPursuitPath()` returns the whole route so `WorldScene.advanceHunterPursuit()` searches once per tick however many steps the hunter takes, and `chooseHunterPursuitStep()` is the single-step wrapper. Equal-length routes are broken deterministically towards the long axis of the chase; an unreachable player makes it close on the nearest tile it can stand on and hold.
- `WorldLabel` captions sit above what they name by default and take a `'below'` placement for the case where above would cover the thing being explained - a trainer watching north is captioned to the south of themselves. That is a *preference*: `placeCaption()` in `src/game/ui/labelPlacement.ts` is the rule, and it slides a caption along its row to stay inside the camera view, flips it to the other side of its anchor rather than let it sit under a raid HUD chip (`RaidHud.occupied` is what it avoids), and leaves a caption whose landmark has scrolled off screen to go with it. It is Phaser-free, so the rule is in `labelPlacement.test.ts` rather than eyeballed. `WorldScene.containWorldLabels()` feeds it the view and the chips every frame.
- What is drawn over what on the overworld is `src/game/world/depths.ts`, and only there: terrain, the watch shading, map art (`MARKER_BAND` - exits, caches, landmarks, contract stops, signs), captions (`CAPTION_BAND`), then figures (`FIGURE_BAND`, y-sorted with the player). The rule is that writing never covers a person - captions used to sit at depth 3-5 against a player at 2.007, so standing on the Floodplain ranger station hid the character completely. `atRow()` carries the y-sort; `depths.test.ts` holds the ordering and fails a scene that goes back to a raw `setDepth(3 + y / 1000)`.
- The raid duration is `RAID_DURATION_MS` in `src/game/run/raidClock.ts`, which also records the route measurement it was derived from; `raidClock.test.ts` holds the relationships a change must not break (every hunter tier reachable, every exit openable in time, the objective route never a sprint, the flee cost a visible share). The clock only advances inside `WorldScene.update`, so battles, the bag, the party screen and the field guide are all free - a raid's clock cost is walking, dialogue and hesitation, and a duration argued from walking alone will be wrong.
- Between-raid healing is priced in raid time, not items: `src/game/hub/recovery.ts` owns the prices and the cap, `Stash.recoverPokemon()` performs the restore, and the debt rides in the save as `pendingRecoveryMs`. It is subtracted from the raid clock when a raid starts and only cleared when that raid resolves (`bankRun`/`bankFirstContractRun`/`applyWipeLoss`), so abandoning a shortened raid cannot shed the bill. Every price there is a share of `RAID_DURATION_MS` rather than an absolute number of minutes, and `MAX_PENDING_RECOVERY_MS` is literally half of it, so shortening the raid can never leave a recovered party deploying into a zero clock; `recovery.test.ts` and a HubScene test pin both.
- Every way a raid can end - extraction, the timer expiring, and losing the last Pokemon - builds a report with `buildExtractionReport()` in `src/game/run/extractionReport.ts` and hands it to `ExtractionScene`. The report is passed exactly what the caller wrote to the stash rather than deriving it, so the screen can never claim a reward the save did not receive; wording and grading live in that module so they are testable without Phaser. Experience is the one part of the haul the ledger cannot list, so it is reported per Pokemon from `RunSnapshot.deployedExperience` - the party's experience at deploy, recorded by `RunManager` because a raid is played on the stash's own Pokemon and by the time it resolves their own numbers are the end of the raid. It counts towards `gradeHaul`, and only Pokemon that came home are listed: a wipe deletes everything outside the secure slot, and reporting a level on one of those would be the cruellest way to be wrong.
- A defeat opens the result screen on a short line-up sequence before its accounting: `buildDefeatSequence()` in `src/game/run/defeatSequence.ts` owns every word and every duration, and `ExtractionScene` plays it as additive `[data-beat]` states over one tableau. It is offered only for `cause: 'defeated'` - a raid lost to the clock has no line-up on the ground - and it needs `report.fallen`, which `buildExtractionReport` fills from the deployed party plus the `lastStand` Pokemon the losing scene passes in, because by wipe time every party member is at 0 HP and otherwise indistinguishable. It is skippable on any key or click from the first frame, and skipping still hands the report the full `INPUT_LOCK_MS`.
- A raid is played on the stash's own Pokemon objects and on a Bag copied from its supplies, but every write-back path reloads the vault from storage first, so nothing a raid does survives it unless it is carried across explicitly. `src/game/run/raidSettlement.ts` builds that carriage for all three endings - the condition each deployed Pokemon came home in, plus a signed supply delta of the bag carried out minus the supplies carried in - and `Stash.applyRaidCondition()`/`applyRaidSupplies()` write it. That condition carries what the raid earned as well as what it cost: experience travels as an absolute total rather than a delta so a replayed settlement cannot pay a win out twice, and `applyRaidCondition()` replays it through `Pokemon.gainExperience()` before writing the HP, so a level crossed in the field arrives with the stats and the learned move that come with it. Level is never stored beside it - it is a function of experience through `experienceForLevel`, and `deserializePokemon` floors a save's XP at that level's own total so a save written before XP was recorded is not charged the whole curve again. Found loot rides home inside that delta, so an extraction must not also bank `snapshot.foundItems`. A faint comes home as a faint rather than a deletion: deleting deployed Pokemon is the wipe's job, and reviving is the recovery bay's premium. Because a raid's damage and status are now what the stash keeps, the in-raid party screen and the lobby state condition through the one `conditionLine()` in `src/game/ui/condition.ts` - it is Phaser-free so the wording is testable, and two phrasings of the same fact would be two answers.
- Healing at base is two priced choices, and they must stay distinct: the recovery bay restores in full for raid time (`src/game/hub/recovery.ts`), while `src/game/hub/treatment.ts` spends one medicine out of the stash from the loadout step. Medicine cannot revive - that is what keeps `RECOVERY_REVIVE_MS` meaningful - and `DeploymentFlow.items` clamps every stack to the live stash so a Potion drunk at base can never also deploy.
- Finishing a step on an extraction tile resolves the raid before anything else is rolled into the same tick (`WorldScene.tryExtract()` returns whether it took the step). Authored exits stand in tall grass, and rolling an encounter first banked the raid and then tore down the scene the result screen was scheduled on, leaving the player in a dead world.
- Fleeing is two different mechanics. The hunter's escape never fails and is priced in raid time by `RunManager.registerHunterFlee()` (schedule in `src/game/run/fleePenalty.ts`); it also disengages, via `beginHunterDisengage`/`findHunterBreakawayTile`/`tickHunterSearch` in `src/game/world/hunter.ts`, with `WorldScene` choosing the fallback tile because only it knows the map's collision. A wild escape is a roll in `src/game/pokemon/battle/escape.ts` whose odds rise per attempt, so it always terminates. Both surface their cost on the command label before the player commits - see `battlePresentation.ts`.
- Floodplain Relay is the starting area: `DEFAULT_RAID_PROGRESS` unlocks only it, `FIRST_CONTRACT` lives on it, and extracting that contract unlocks the other three insertions. While it is active `HubScene` offers only insertions on its map, and `generateRunPlan` refuses to attach any contract to a raid starting elsewhere - it takes the contract itself and has no default, because `undefined` for "no contract" silently taking a default would attach one anyway.
- Phaser reuses ONE instance per scene key for every `scene.start`, so any field describing the raid in progress outlives that raid. Clear per-raid state on entry - `WorldScene.resetStateFromPreviousRaid()` is that list, `BattleScene.create()` and `HubScene.applyLoadedGame()` are the equivalents - and add to it rather than guarding the read. A missed `pendingHubTransition` reset made every raid after the first unplayable; `consecutiveRaids.test.ts` drives two raids on one instance to guard it.
- `HubScene.init()` prefers the stored save over its start payload: Phaser hands a restarted scene its previous payload when `scene.start('hub')` passes none, which otherwise re-renders a pre-raid snapshot after extraction.
- Generated runs never randomise an exit out of existence. `generateExtractionPoints()` in `runGeneration.ts` offers every authored exit on a warp-reachable map and only varies the timing of exits with no authored `requirement`; `runGeneration.test.ts` guards the guarantee across 500 seeds per insertion.
- The game screen is fitted to the browser by `src/game/display/stage.ts` and applied by `stageScaler.ts`: whole-number zoom only, and the room left over is spent on logical pixels (up to 400x256) rather than on margin. `BASE_STAGE_*` (320x240) is the smallest screen and the composition every scene is authored against - never lay anything out assuming more room than that. `WorldScene` uses the whole screen; `BattleScene` and `TitleScene` keep their authored composition, centred (battle by `centreComposition()`, which is why its coordinates are still plain battle-screen coordinates). `#app` is sized to the canvas in script and is the CSS container the DOM screens size themselves against (`cqw`/`em`, not `vw`/`px`), so the lobby scales with the game screen rather than with the browser window; a CSS border on the canvas would take pixels off it and put the art back on a fractional scale, so it carries a `box-shadow` frame instead.
- The in-raid overlays are three corner chips: `src/game/scenes/raidHud.ts` decides what they say (pure, tested without Phaser, in the manner of `battlePresentation.ts`) and `src/game/ui/RaidHud.ts` draws them. Anything added there is drawn over the map the player is reading, so it is sized to its own text and appears only while it is true. The clock's flash-and-sting threshold is `raidClockAlertTier`, separate from the colour ramp, so a visual tier can be added without adding an interruption.
- Phaser's key captures are global and outlive the scene that asked for them: `addCapture`, and `addKey` which captures by default, register on the game's KeyboardManager, so a paused `WorldScene` was still calling `preventDefault()` on O, P, B, K, SPACE and ENTER while an overlay it opened sat on top - which is why the field guide could only be closed with the mouse. Input ownership is therefore not a scene's to take: `src/game/ui/overlayKeyboard.ts` owns it, every `MenuOverlay` claims through it on construction and releases on `destroy()`, and one shared window listener in the *capture* phase gives the key to the topmost overlay and stops propagation so nothing below it - paused scene, Phaser captures, lower overlay - sees the key at all. Keyup is deliberately left to flow. A new overlay needs no key wiring of its own; `isOverlayDismissKey(event, 'o')` is the shared rule that Escape and the key that opened a screen both close it, and `overlayKeyboard.test.ts` reproduces the whole layering without a browser.
- Every panel in the game is one drawn frame: `src/game/ui/pixelWindow.ts` reproduces `assets/battle/hud-box.png` - a one-pixel `#171717` border with clear corners - at any size, because that texture is 32x32 and smears when stretched small. Cream fill is screen furniture (`RaidHud`), a tinted fill with a coloured border is world annotation (`src/game/ui/WorldLabel.ts`), and `DialogBox` takes `pixelWindow: true` for the same frame. Nothing stretches a panel texture any more - the battle dialogue and the two name plates were the last, and `hud-box.png`/`dialog-plain.png` are kept for provenance but no longer loaded; `pixelWindow.test.ts` fails any scene that passes `backgroundTexture` again.
- The classic tileset has no water art - its `POND_WATER` tile is the same green as grass - so `WorldScene.createMap()` tints `POND_TILES` with `WATER_TINT`. Do not paint detail-layer decorations over water: they render as one-tile lawns in open water.
- Overworld movement only turns in place against a blocked tile (`planNextGridStep`), so a landmark on walkable ground can never be faced from beside it unless a lane runs *into* that neighbouring tile pointing at it. The Sluice Wheel had no such lane, so the West Culvert it opens had never been openable. `WorldScene.advanceStep()` therefore also works a landmark you stand on, as it already does for loot and contract stops. All three return the line to say rather than showing it, so a deed done on ground a trainer is watching arrives in the same dialogue as the challenge instead of silencing it; `pois.test.ts` pins that, and which landmarks still fail the facing rule.
- `Pokemon` has no `cureStatus()` method; clear a status by setting `primaryStatus` to null directly, which `SaveManager` saves and restores.
- Battle data is ported from the public Unity original, `OllySalanson/escapeFromPalletTown` (readable through `gh-axi api`), but the web game adds a 1.5x same-type bonus that Unity's `Pokemon.TakeDamage` has no equivalent of, and Unity fought its encounter tables with a level-10 five-strong party rather than one level-5 starter. Check that repo before treating any stat, learnset or encounter table as intentional - and re-measure early balance rather than porting a number across.
- Every overworld figure - player, townsfolk, trainers, hunter - is drawn from the one `character` sheet, which holds a single character in walk and attack poses and no second design, so nothing in the art distinguishes them. `src/game/world/characterPresentation.ts` is what does: role tints (the player is the only figure left in the sheet's true colours) plus the player's own ground ring and head chevron, both pure pixel tables so `characterPresentation.test.ts` can assert the rules without Phaser. `WorldScene.setPlayerPosition()` is the only place the player moves, and it carries both marks and the player's y-sorted depth with it.
- Item, objective and raid-marker art is one 16x16 icon set under `public/assets/icons`, named once in `src/game/ui/icons.ts` and drawn by both the DOM menus (`itemIcon`/`objectiveIcon`) and `WorldScene` (`iconTextureKey`, preloaded in `BootScene`). Sizes are whole multiples of 16 everywhere so nothing is resampled; `src/game/ui/icons.test.ts` pins the file list, the size and that no scene has gone back to a text glyph.
- `public/assets/ASSET_PROVENANCE.md` records where every shipped asset came from, who authored it and under what licence; adding an asset without its entry there is an incomplete change. Most of it is carried over byte-identical from the captain's Unity project `OllySalanson/escapeFromPalletTown` (blob SHAs, re-checkable with `gh-axi api`); the icons were drawn here, and `battle/orange-kid.woff2` comes from Typodermic's own CC0 page. It names the one remaining file that is not the captain's own work and whose licence does not cover this repository (`battle/background-grass.png`) - verify at the publisher rather than assume, and never resolve a flagged question in code instead of in that file.
- Every species sprite is `public/assets/pokemon/{front,back}/<dexId>.png`; `spriteAssets.test.ts` enforces the format and a size bound, because a dimensionless SVG once rasterised to 150x150 and covered the battle screen.
- Battle text lives in `src/game/scenes/battlePresentation.ts` as pure functions, so message wording, move guidance and layout are testable without Phaser.
- One typeface, and it is a load-time dependency: `GAME_FONT` in `src/game/ui/gameFont.ts` is the only family any canvas scene names, `index.html` preloads the woff2 with the document, and `BootScene.create()` waits on `awaitGameFont()` before starting a scene. Phaser paints canvas text with whatever face is ready at that instant and never repaints it, so without both halves the first battle of a session drew its name plates and type banners in the browser monospace while the dialogue below came out in Orange Kid. `awaitGameFont` resolves either way, so a missing file delays boot rather than wedging it. Sizes for the raid screen are `src/game/ui/screenType.ts`, picked to hold the letter heights the HUD was designed at rather than the numbers that used to be typed.
- `PALLET_TALL_GRASS` in `src/game/pokemon/encounters.ts` is shared by Pallet Town, Route 1 and the Floodplain Relay; changing it changes all three. It holds one of each starter species so every starter's level-7 move has a super-effective target; `starterIdentity.test.ts` guards that.
- Nothing distinguishes the three starters before level 7 except their level-1 kit. `computePokemonStats` is `floor(base * level / 100) + 5`, so at level 5 every starter stat sits within one point of the others, and every typed move is gated to level 7 - which is why pulling signature moves earlier has been measured and rejected twice (it makes one starter dominant, or arms the wild roster with 3x hits a 17 HP starter cannot read). Balance changes here belong in movesets, type matchups or encounter composition, re-measured over the real engine.
